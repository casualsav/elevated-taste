## What works
- **Color does zero work; type and imagery do all of it.** The entire chrome is two inks — near-black `#070707` and one grey `#a2a2a9` — on an off-white field, with no accent hue anywhere. All chroma lives inside the project thumbnails, so the page reads as a neutral frame around vivid work. Transfers to any portfolio/agency where the *content* should be the only color event; the shell stays achromatic on purpose.
- **Hierarchy is built from scale + position, never weight.** One weight (400) runs the whole site; a heading is ~4–11× body and is *placed* off-grid (left word, then a right-indented word) rather than bolded. Because nothing is heavier, the giant type still reads as calm editorial, not shouting. Transfers anywhere you want drama without a "bold everything" arms race — buy emphasis with size and whitespace.
- **Emphasis inside a sentence via ink alternation, not bold/italic.** The about line — "14islands designs and builds *digital products, brands* in grey, *and experiences* in black — with offices in Stockholm and Reykjavík" — semantically weights phrases by switching between the two inks mid-sentence. Reusable for any single hero statement where you want the eye to land on specific nouns without breaking the reading flow.
- **A grid that decays into a list.** Top projects get full image tiles in a 2-up grid; lower-priority projects collapse to a text-only ledger whose rows *fade in opacity as they descend*. The layout itself encodes ranking — featured work is rich, the long tail literally recedes. Transfers to any "more items below the fold" feed (changelogs, archives, case-study indexes) to signal depth without paginating.
- **One signature motion curve = coherence for free.** Effectively everything animates on `1.2s cubic-bezier(0.25,0.46,0.45,0.94)` (slow easeOutQuad), with 0.3–0.4s variants for hover. A single, unusually long house easing makes unrelated elements feel like one material. Transfers as a discipline: pick one curve, vary only duration.
- **Asymmetry as the layout grammar.** Eyebrow text sits right-of-center, the email is indented far right, headline words straddle the canvas. Nothing is centered and nothing fills its row; the negative space is the composition. Transfers to editorial/agency sites that want to feel art-directed rather than templated.
- **Paint the page field once, behind everything, and scroll transparent sections over it.** The off-white ground is a single `position:fixed`, `z-index:-1` backdrop that never moves, and 92% of the section height is transparent — so no section repaints its own background; the page is content flowing across one held stage. Here it buys only a constant, rock-steady field (subtle, because the backdrop is the same colour as the page), but the *structure* — a fixed negative-z backdrop plus transparent sections — is exactly what lets a held image, gradient, or scene sit still and be *revealed* by moving content instead of scrolling with it. Transfers to any long scroll wanting a stable ground or a backdrop-reveal: decouple the background from the content flow rather than tiling a background per block.

## Design-system read
- **Type:** Two custom faces, both used at weight 400. BentonSans (geometric grotesque) carries all display + UI; AftenScreen carries running caption/body and the special grey `&`. Scale is extreme: ~16px body → 75/100/180px display (a 4.7–11× jump). Tracking is negative and scales with size — captured pairs: **26.67px → −1.07px (≈ −0.04em)**, **75px → −3px (−0.04em)**, **100px → −4px (−0.04em)**; the 180px head extrapolates to ≈ −9px on the same ~−0.04em ratio. So tracking holds a near-constant **−0.04em** proportionally (not tighter-per-step), which is what keeps the huge words optically dense. Hierarchy = scale + tracking + placement; weight is constant.
- **Color:** Two inks only — `#070707` foreground, `#a2a2a9` for captions, hairline rules, and de-emphasized words (the same grey reused as "ink at lower presence"). Bg `#f2f2f2` off-white; project tiles sit on `#fff`/`#000`. No semantic palette, no brand accent — chroma is delegated entirely to imagery.
- **Spacing rhythm:** Roughly 8px-derived but loose; the operative unit is the section-gap (huge vertical whitespace, ~6rem+ between blocks). Density is low by design — single statements get a full viewport. Gutters in the project grid are tight (~10px) so images read as a contact sheet.
- **Layout system:** 2-column project grid → single-column fading index → asymmetric full-bleed text sections. Mobile keeps the *stagger* (Design left / & Technology indented) rather than centering, so the art direction survives the breakpoint. Alignment is deliberately ragged.
- **Layering / page architecture:** A real fixed-backdrop stack, not a flat scroll. One full-viewport layer — `Page_background`, `position:fixed`, `z-index:-1`, 100%×100%, opacity 1 — paints the `#f2f2f2` field and never moves, while **6 of 7 content sections are transparent** (`transparent_height_pct 92`): hero, fullscreen media, intro, case-study grid and text blocks carry no background of their own and scroll *over* that one held ground (the whitespace bands between tiles in s02–s08 are the fixed field showing through). Two more fixed layers pin on top at `z:2` — the transparent nav header (12% tall) and the bottom-right cookie card, which holds pixel-identical across s02→s08 as tiles flow past. No blend or glass anywhere (`blend_glass` empty; `has_blend_overlay:false`). Because the backdrop is the *same* off-white as the page, the effect is a constant, unmoving ground rather than a scenic parallax — depth comes from decoupling the background from the content flow, so the giant type always reads against one stable field. (The only other negative-z layer is an opacity-0 hidden mobile dialog — inert.)
- **Motion / easing intent:** Two captured easings — the house `cubic-bezier(0.25,0.46,0.45,0.94)` (slow easeOutQuad) plus a bare `ease` fallback. Durations span **300 / 500 / 550 / 600 / 650 / 800 / 1200 / 1250ms** — micro state ~300ms, in-view reveals in the 500–800ms band, and the signature long 1200–1250ms on hero/large type. Entrance staggers step at **100/200/300/400/500/600ms** (~100ms/item). Communicates "premium, unhurried, crafted." The 1.2s+ long end is the expressive risk; full choreography + per-technique safety in **Interaction & motion** below.
- **Density:** Very low in the chrome, high in the project grid. Legibility holds because the only competing elements per screen are 2–3, and the ink contrast (`#070707` on `#f2f2f2`) is strong.
- **Hierarchy:** Dominant ordering device is **scale**, with **position** and **opacity** as secondaries. Color and weight are intentionally removed from the hierarchy toolkit.
- **Restraint:** No accent color, no second weight, no shadows, no rounded corners on content, no gradients in the chrome, no centered layouts. The expressiveness is entirely in scale, asymmetry, and the long motion curve.

## Interaction & motion

For an agency/spectacle site this motion is **unusually disciplined** — closer to Attio than to teak. It is **hand-rolled** (Next.js CSS Modules: 194 `@keyframes`, 9 CSS animation rules, 11 transition rules; **no GSAP/Lenis/Framer** — `libraries:[]`). The whole vocabulary collapses to four named keyframes reused per component — `fadeIn`, `fadeInAndUp`, `drawAnimation`/`undrawAnimation` (underline draw/erase) — fired through **31 IntersectionObservers over 129 targets** (`rootMargin:200px`, threshold `0`/`0.33`); ~1191 rAF in one scroll pass (no canvas — `canvas:0`); 4 scroll listeners drive 3 native `position:fixed` pins. **Critically for an expressive site: `prefers-reduced-motion` IS respected** (`prmRules:1`, `reduced_motion:true`) — the single biggest thing teak got wrong, 14islands gets right. **The four frames captured the choreography cleanly** (no scroll-hijack/WebGL near-duplication); evidence below is grounded in `corpus/expressive/screenshots/14islands-com/frames/`.

- **Staggered opacity-ladder nav entrance** — *Trigger:* load. *Mechanism:* the six nav links animate `opacity 0→1` on a stepped delay (captured resting ladder *before* settle: Services 0.53, Culture 0.36, Journal 0.18, AI 0.05, Contact 0.00 → all 1.0; firstChange 150ms then 300ms, ~100ms/item) via `fadeIn`. *Reads as:* the menu "developing in," and — pointedly — the same descending-opacity device as the page's static index, now temporal. *Transfers to:* nav bars, toolbars, any small horizontal set you want to introduce in sequence. *Safety:* **production-safe** (opacity-only, GPU-cheap, one-shot, reduced-motion-gated). → `scroll-reveal-in-view` (group/stagger variant).
- **In-view fade-rise reveal** — *Trigger:* in-view (31 IO / 129 targets, `rootMargin:200px` so it fires ~200px early). *Mechanism:* one class flip on intersect → `fadeInAndUp` runs `opacity` + a small `translateY`, once, at 500–800ms house easing. *Reads as:* the long project grid + about block "settling in" tile-by-tile rather than popping. *Transfers to:* any tall portfolio/feed. *Safety:* **production-safe** (compositor transform+opacity, once-only, gated). → `scroll-reveal-in-view`.
- **Inline-link underline draw** — *Trigger:* hover/focus. *Mechanism:* `drawAnimation`/`undrawAnimation` (+ a `…Thin` variant) animate an underline's `scaleX`/clip from 0→1 on enter and erase on leave — a left-to-right pen stroke. *Reads as:* hand-crafted, "drawn" affordance that matches the achromatic editorial voice (no color change needed to signal a link). *Transfers to:* inline links, footer/nav text links, anywhere a color-hover would break a monochrome system. *Safety:* **production-safe** (transform/clip on a pseudo-element; pair with the existing `focus-visible` outline so keyboard users get the same cue). No corpus recipe — reuse the principle (animated underline, not a color swap).
- **Native pinned chrome (header · background · cookie card)** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* three `position:fixed` pins — the nav header, the `Page_background` layer, and the consent card — hold while content scrolls beneath (4 scroll listeners; no per-frame JS transform). *Reads as:* a stable stage; work scrolls *through* a fixed frame and the off-white background never moves, so the giant type reads against a constant field. *Transfers to:* persistent nav/CTA over a long scroll; a held background behind moving content. *Safety:* **production-safe** (native sticky/fixed, no rAF cost). → `sticky-pin-scrollytelling`.
- **Subtle nav-cluster parallax** — *Trigger:* in-view (1 `transform/parallax` effect on the `Work Services Culture…` cluster). *Mechanism:* the nav/label cluster translates at a slightly different rate than the page as it enters. *Reads as:* a faint depth cue — barely perceptible, not teak-style flung layers; the frames show no dramatic displacement, so this is a light touch only. *Transfers to:* one accent element you want to feel a half-step detached. *Safety:* **production-safe** (single transformed layer, subtle); becomes spectacle only if multiplied across many layers (teak's failure mode). → `parallax-drift`.

## Grid / container
- **Columns:** a **12-column** base grid; project tiles span **3** and **4** columns (`.craft.grid.columns:["12","3","4"]`), reading as the 2-up featured grid in s03/s06. **Mobile collapses 12/3/4 → 4/2/3** (`responsive.grid_collapse`) — a 4-col phone grid that *keeps* the asymmetry rather than going single-column. Measured gutters are two-tier: a tight **~10px** between tiles in a band (contact-sheet density) and a larger **~42px** between bands. No max-width container value was captured — the 1440px shots read as a near-full-bleed field with large asymmetric margins on the text sections (container width still an estimate).

## Production-safety note
- **Achromatic two-ink shell** — production-safe. Cost: near-zero; improves a11y (high contrast) and theming simplicity. The most portable idea here.
- **Single-weight scale-driven hierarchy** — production-safe. Cost: needs a face with a strong display presence; if your font is weak at 100px+ it falls flat. Test the large end.
- **Ink-alternation emphasis in a sentence** — production-safe *with care*. Cost: the grey must still pass contrast (`#a2a2a9` on `#f2f2f2` is ~2.3:1 — **fails WCAG AA for body text**). Acceptable for a large decorative hero statement; do NOT use this grey for small reading text.
- **Opacity-decay index** — production-safe as decoration, **spectacle-only if it gates real navigation**. Cost: faded rows at the bottom can drop below contrast thresholds and read as disabled; keep the links keyboard-focusable and restore full opacity on focus/hover (a11y), and never fade the *only* path to a page.
- **1.2s house reveal curve** — spectacle-only at that duration. Cost: 1.2s on scroll-reveals delays content and hurts perceived performance / INP; respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and consider ~0.6s in production. The *discipline* (one curve everywhere) is fully transferable; the *length* is the indulgence.
- **A11y scorecard (captured, honest):** Unusually strong *for an expressive site*. **Reduced motion: respected** (`prmRules:1`) — the headline win; almost all spectacle peers (e.g. teak) fail this, 14islands ships a resting state. **Focus-visible: present** — a real `1px rgb(16,16,16)` outline on every nav link. **Image alt coverage: 1.0** (22/22 images carry alt). Defects: (1) the **`#a2a2a9` grey on `#f2f2f2` ≈ 2.3:1 fails WCAG AA** wherever it carries reading text (the ink-alternation hero statement and the muted category labels — decorative-scale tolerable, small-text not); (2) **16 of 60 interactive targets fall under ~44px** (tight nav links / inline actions) — a touch-target miss; (3) the fading index rows risk dropping below contrast as they recede — keep them focus-restorable. Net: motion + focus + alt are genuinely good; the two-ink *contrast* and small tap targets are the real liabilities, not the motion.
- **Capture note:** a small non-blocking cookie consent card ("HEJ! WE USE ANALYTIC COOKIES…") sits bottom-right over the hero in both captures — it is not a wall/preloader and does not obscure the page; the hero behind it is fully readable.

## Extracted patterns
- staggered-display-headline — oversized single-weight headline whose words are split and offset across the canvas (left word / right-indented word), tightened with negative tracking; reuse for art-directed agency/portfolio heroes that need drama without bold.
- opacity-decay-index — a list whose rows lose opacity as they descend, encoding priority into the layout; reuse for archives, changelogs, and "long tail" indexes below a featured grid.
