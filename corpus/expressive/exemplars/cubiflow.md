## What works (transferable principles)

- **One typeface, one size, one weight — let layout carry all hierarchy.** The entire UI is B612 Mono 12px/400. With type variables zeroed out, hierarchy is forced into *position and spacing*: the wordmark centers the top edge, controls anchor the four corners, the CTA owns dead-center. The lesson transfers anywhere you want a "system/instrument" feel: remove a variable (size or weight) and you're forced to compose with the ones left, which reads as discipline rather than absence.

- **A monospace HUD frame makes an empty canvas feel like an interface, not a blank page.** Tiny labels pinned to the corners (`[G]`, `[R]`, `FB IG IN`, `SERVICE APP`) imply state, modes, and keyboard affordances. The mechanism: corner-anchored micro-labels read as *chrome around a viewport*, so the user infers there's a live system in the middle even before anything moves. Transferable to any tool, map, editor, or canvas product that wants to signal "interactive surface."

- **Spend the whole screen on one spectacle; starve everything else.** ~95% of the viewport is empty off-white holding a single floating object. Because nothing competes, the 3D thumbnail sphere becomes unambiguously the subject and the eye has nowhere else to go. The transferable rule: a spectacle only reads as premium if the surrounding UI is *aggressively* quiet — one loud thing on a silent page, never two.

- **Near-monochrome + a single hyper-saturated accent = maximum signal from minimum color.** The palette is ink `#111` on off-white `#f4f5f6`; the lone orange `#ff5c00` appears literally twice (the word "APP", one control). Because it is rationed to ~1% of marks, that orange functions as a true call-to-action / state highlight without any supporting "brand color" scaffolding. Transfers directly to dashboards and editorial UIs: reserve one chroma for the one thing that must be noticed.

- **A single signature easing curve gives motion a consistent "hand."** Every transition uses `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` (expo-out) at ~0.9s — a fast launch into a long, soft settle. Standardizing on one expressive curve (rather than mixing eases) makes unrelated movements feel authored by the same physics, which is what reads as "crafted motion." Transferable: pick one non-linear curve and apply it everywhere; consistency of easing matters more than variety.

- **Gate the spectacle by capability and ship a *different* experience, not a degraded one.** Mobile drops the WebGL sphere entirely and substitutes "SHAKE TO SEE EMOTION" with a glitch-text pill — a device-native interaction (accelerometer) instead of a shrunken 3D scene. The principle: when the headline technique can't survive a context (small GPU, touch, no pointer), replace the *interaction model* rather than cramming the desktop one onto a phone.

## Design-system read

- **Type:** Single family **B612 Mono** (a legible mono designed for cockpit displays — the "instrument" connotation is intentional). One size 12px, one weight 400, leading 1.2 (14.4px). Hierarchy = **position + spacing**, not scale or weight. **Tracking:** the raw capture exposes *no* per-size tracking ramp (`tracking_by_size: []`) — unsurprising at a single 12px size, where mono's fixed advance width already does the spacing work; the apparent "tracked-out" feel of the corner labels is letter *count* and gap, not a `letter-spacing` token. The only typographic emphasis is an underline on the `CUBIFLOW` wordmark (confirmed in every frame).
- **Color:** Two-tone with one accent. `bg` off-white `#f4f5f6`; `fg`/`border` ink `#111`; inverted chips use ink bg with light fg; **accent** orange `#ff5c00` rationed to ~1% of elements. No grays-in-between, no gradients on chrome.
- **Spacing rhythm:** 4px base; tight scale 4 / 8 / 16 dominates UI chrome, with a large 64px / 128px jump reserved for section padding and gaps. Whitespace is the primary compositional material — most of the page *is* space.
- **Layout system:** Edge-anchored corners + centered axis. No grid in the conventional sense; the 3D scene is a Fibonacci-sphere point distribution rendered in WebGL. Two breakpoints of consequence: desktop (sphere) and mobile (shake/glitch fallback).
- **Grid / container:** raw reports a nominal `grid-template-columns: 3` (desktop and mobile alike — it does **not** collapse), but this is effectively meaningless here: the frames show no visible column grid, just corner-anchored HUD labels around a full-bleed centered canvas. There is no max-width content column; the "container" is the viewport itself and layout is done by absolute corner anchoring, not tracks. Treat the `3` as a thin/incidental signal, not a real design grid.
- **Motion / easing intent:** One signature curve `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` at **900ms** (the only authored duration; bare `ease` is the library default) = expo-out — a fast launch into a long, damped settle. Communicates weightless precision — things arrive quickly then ease to rest like a well-tuned instrument. But the *base* read undersells it: this is a **Three.js + GSAP** WebGL stage, not a static page — a continuously auto-rotating thumbnail sphere driven by a hot render loop, drag-to-orbit pointer physics, and a pinned HUD frame. Full choreography, evidence, and per-technique safety in **Interaction & motion** below.
- **Density:** Visually near-zero density in 2D, but high *latent* density — ~50 portfolio items packed into one navigable object. The page hides density behind interaction instead of stacking it in a grid.
- **Hierarchy:** Dominant ordering device is **position** (corners vs. center), reinforced by the single accent. Scale and weight are deliberately removed from the toolkit.
- **Restraint:** No headlines, no body copy, no hero sentence, no cards-in-cards, no shadows on chrome, no second font, no second size, no gradient UI. Almost everything a landing page "should" have is absent — that absence is the statement.

## Components

The capture exposes almost no conventional component states — this is a canvas-first page where the "components" are HUD text links, not buttons/cards/inputs. Reported honestly:

- **Button / CTA:** **not captured as a styled button.** The only real action is the centered `CONTACT` pill (text + mail/phone glyphs) plus the orange `APP` link; the raw `states` table records no button `:hover`/`:active`/`:disabled` deltas for them (`disabled: []`). What *is* captured is link-level: text-decoration `none → underline` on hover (see Interaction & motion). Treat CTA styling as unspecified by the corpus here.
- **Card:** the "cards" are the ~50 portfolio thumbnails — **WebGL-textured quads inside the canvas, not DOM cards.** No border/radius/shadow/hover tokens exist for them in computed style; hover lift/scale is plausible but **not captured** (no DOM hover deltas on thumbnails). Do not infer card tokens from this exemplar.
- **Nav:** corner-anchored mono micro-labels — wordmark `CUBIFLOW` (underlined), `[G]`/`[R]`, `FB IG IN`, `SERVICE APP`, plus `+`/`□` ticks. Hover = underline; the whole nav cluster is **pinned (`position: fixed`)**. `navVisibleLinks: 1` (the rest read as icon/label glyphs, not crawlable links). Focus is honored (`a Cubiflow` focus → `outline: 1px rgb(16,16,16)`).
- **Input:** **none** — no forms, fields, or text inputs on the page (`states` lists no input; `interactive.disclosure: 1` is the only non-link interactive, likely the contact toggle). Nothing to extract.

## Interaction & motion

Cubiflow's static read calls it "near-zero density" — the motion read corrects that hard: this is a **WebGL spectacle stage**, the expressive cousin of teak's Framer choreography but built on a different stack and pointed inward at a single object instead of a scrolling page. Evidence from the capture: libraries **Three.js + GSAP (TweenMax) + Webflow IX2**, scripts `three.min.js` / `gsap.min.js` / `Draggable.min.js` / `TextPlugin.min.js`; **2 `<canvas>` nodes**; **~1439 `requestAnimationFrame` calls in one pass** (a continuous Three.js render loop, not a one-shot reveal); **4 `mousemove` listeners with 0 cursor-followers** (the pointer feeds the sphere's drag/inertia, *not* a custom cursor); **1 IntersectionObserver** (20 targets, threshold `0.35`); **1 scroll listener**; **5 scroll effects — all `pin (fixed)`** (nav, the `[G]`/`[R]` corner HUD, the footer social/`SERVICE APP` cluster, a wrapper div, and the canvas itself); **1 `@keyframes` named `spin`**; 2 CSS animation rules, 16 transition rules. Easing is the lone signature `cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)` @ 900ms over a bare `ease` default. **Critically, `prefers-reduced-motion` is NOT respected** (`prmRules: 0`, `a11y.reduced_motion: false`) — the headline accessibility liability, exactly as expected for an agency spectacle site.

**The frames did not capture the motion.** All sampled stills (`s00`–`s10`, every one ~157KB and pixel-near-identical) show the same resting sphere: because the canvas + HUD are *pinned* and the rotation/zoom happens **inside the WebGL canvas** (and via pointer, not scroll-position), the frame sampler — which diffs DOM at scroll stops — sees no change. Every motion claim below is therefore grounded in the **probe counts + library fingerprint + `spin` keyframe + DOM markup**, not in the frames; nothing is invented from a still. Stack: **Three.js (render loop) · GSAP/TweenMax + Draggable (pointer physics) · CSS `@keyframes spin` · Webflow IX2 (chrome)**.

- **Continuous auto-rotating WebGL sphere** — *Trigger:* load / ambient (autoplay). *Mechanism:* a Fibonacci-distributed point cloud of ~50 thumbnail-textured quads rotates continuously inside a `<canvas>`, driven by a Three.js `requestAnimationFrame` loop (**~1439 rAF calls/pass**) and named `@keyframes spin`. *Reads as:* a living "instrument" — the portfolio is one slowly turning object you watch rather than a grid you scan. *Transfers to:* portfolio / brand-spectacle hero where browsing *is* the experience. *Safety:* **spectacle-only** — a never-idle render loop is a real CPU/GPU/battery cost; decodes ~50 textures (bandwidth); the canvas is not crawlable (raw profile captured zero headings/copy) and not screen-reader traversable; ambient rotation is a vestibular trigger and there is **no reduced-motion stop**. Gate behind capability + `prefers-reduced-motion` and ship a static DOM `<a>` grid fallback. → `webgl-thumbnail-sphere`.
- **Drag-to-orbit pointer inertia** — *Trigger:* drag / pointer (fine-pointer). *Mechanism:* `Draggable.min.js` (GSAP) + **4 `mousemove` listeners** map pointer delta to sphere angular velocity with momentum/inertia; **0 cursor-followers**, so the mousemove work is steering the object, not trailing a custom cursor. *Reads as:* you can grab and spin the instrument — tactile, "this surface is live." *Transfers to:* any single-object 3D/canvas explorer (product viewer, map, globe). *Safety:* **spectacle-only** — pointer-driven inertia does nothing on touch (where the mobile "shake" model substitutes), adds continuous main-thread + rAF work, and has no reduced-motion path. Pair with the auto-rotate fallback above.
- **Sticky-pinned HUD stage** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress (native pin). *Mechanism:* **all 5 captured scroll effects are `position: fixed`** — the wordmark nav, the `[G]`/`[R]` corner controls, the `FB IG IN` / `SERVICE APP` footer cluster, a wrapper, and the canvas all hold position while the page "scrolls," so the chrome frames the viewport as a stable instrument panel and the sphere reads as the live content behind it. *Reads as:* a persistent cockpit/HUD around a viewport — "interactive surface," not a document. *Transfers to:* tools, editors, maps, canvas products, and brand stages wanting an always-present frame. *Safety:* **production-safe** — native `fixed`/sticky, no per-frame JS; the one genuinely cheap, accessible technique on the page. → `monospace-hud-chrome` (the corner labels) / `viewport-frame-ticks` (the `+`/`□` corner ticks).
- **Glitch / scramble text reveal** — *Trigger:* load / interaction. *Mechanism:* GSAP `TextPlugin.min.js` is loaded (its sole job is character-by-character text mutation), corroborating the static read's mobile **"SHAKE TO SEE EMOTION" glitch-text pill**; on desktop it most plausibly scrambles a short label (e.g. the wordmark or a mode tag) into place via the `spin`/expo-out timing. *Reads as:* a terminal/instrument booting — reinforces the cockpit-mono voice. *Transfers to:* short eyebrow/wordmark/mode labels on a system-feel UI (never body copy). *Safety:* **spectacle-only (mild)** — cheap CPU-wise, but rapidly mutating glyphs are an a11y hazard (screen readers re-announce; motion-sensitive users), and with no reduced-motion gate the scramble can't be stilled. Scope to one short label and gate it. *(No corpus recipe — reuse the principle, reduced-motion-gated.)*
- **Hover underline micro-state** — *Trigger:* hover. *Mechanism:* the nav/social links (`[G]`, `[R]`, `FB`, `IG`, `IN`) flip `text-decoration: none → underline` — the only DOM hover delta captured. *Reads as:* minimal, instrument-grade affordance feedback; restraint over flourish. *Transfers to:* any text-link chrome. *Safety:* **production-safe** (pure CSS, no motion-sickness/perf cost). *(No standalone recipe — trivial CSS.)*

## Production-safety note

- **WebGL thumbnail sphere (hero)** — **spectacle-only.** Costs: heavy GPU/CPU and bandwidth (decoding ~50 textures); not crawlable, so SEO sees an empty page (note the raw profile captured **zero headings** and no real copy — search engines get nothing); keyboard/screen-reader users can't traverse a point-cloud of canvas-drawn images; motion can trigger vestibular discomfort. Production-safe fallback: render the same projects as a real DOM `<a>` grid/list (crawlable, focusable, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected), and *layer* the WebGL sphere on top only when the device passes a capability + reduced-motion check — progressive enhancement, not the baseline.
- **Single font / single size / single weight** — **production-safe** as an aesthetic, *with a caveat*: 12px mono as the only size is below comfortable reading size and fails on long-form content; safe here because there is almost no text to read. In a content product, keep the mono "instrument" treatment for chrome/labels but introduce a real type scale for body.
- **Monospace corner HUD labels** (`[G]`, `[R]`, `SERVICE APP`) — **production-safe**, but the cryptic single-letter affordances (`[G]`/`[R]`) trade discoverability for mystique; pair them with tooltips/`aria-label`s in a real product.
- **Rationed single accent `#ff5c00`** — **production-safe** and recommended; just verify contrast when the accent carries meaning (orange on off-white is fine for large/bold marks, borderline for small text).
- **Signature 0.9s expo-out easing** — **production-safe** for hero/showcase moments; 0.9s is too slow for routine UI feedback (buttons, menus) — scope long durations to deliberate reveals and keep functional transitions ≤200ms.
- **Mobile "shake to see emotion" accelerometer interaction** — **spectacle-only.** Requires motion-sensor permission (often denied/unavailable), is undiscoverable, and excludes anyone who can't physically shake a device. Acceptable as a playful Easter egg over a static fallback, never as the only path to content.

- **A11y scorecard (honest, mixed).** Wins: **`img_alt_ratio: 1`** (every `<img>` carries alt — 104 images, all labeled) and **`focus_visible: true`** with a real focus outline (`1px rgb(16,16,16)` on the wordmark) — better than most spectacle sites. Defects: **`reduced_motion: false` / `prmRules: 0`** — the continuous sphere rotation and pointer inertia have **no `prefers-reduced-motion` stop**, the single biggest liability (vestibular risk + never-idle render loop); **12 small tap targets** flagged (the 12px mono HUD labels sit below the 44px touch minimum); and the WebGL canvas is **not crawlable or screen-reader traversable** (the point-cloud of textured quads is invisible to assistive tech, and the raw profile captured zero headings/real copy — SEO/AT get an empty page). Net: expressive site, typical of the class — it nails alt-text and focus but fails reduced-motion and tap-size; the canvas content needs a parallel DOM `<a>` list to be accessible at all.
