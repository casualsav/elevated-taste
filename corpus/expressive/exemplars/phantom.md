## What works
- **The chrome is monochrome so the work supplies all the color.** Canvas is pure black, every interface mark is white (or translucent white), and there is *no* chromatic accent at all — the only saturated color on screen comes from the project thumbnails themselves. This is the cleanest possible solution to the agency-portfolio problem: the UI never competes with the work it frames. Transferable to any gallery, store, or showcase where the content is the hero — strip the chrome to a single neutral and let the media carry the palette.
- **Depth is built from translucency + blur, never shadow.** There is not a single drop-shadow in the system; floating elements (the nav capsule, the metadata chips, the consent card) read as "above" the grid because they are frosted glass — `rgba(white,0.15)` fill, a `rgba(white,0.2)` hairline, `backdrop-filter: blur`. On a dark, busy, image-dense background this layers far more legibly than shadow (shadows vanish against black and against photography), and it ties every floating control into one material language. Transferable to any dark or photographic UI where shadow-based elevation falls apart.
- **Hierarchy by material + position, not by type scale.** Body, nav, and metadata are essentially one size in two families (Helvetica Now ~16px, ballinger-mono ~11px); the page has almost no heading ladder. Importance is signalled by *what something is made of and where it floats* — primary nav is a solid-feeling glass capsule pinned bottom-center; secondary metadata is the same glass at chip size laid over the media; the CTA is the one filled-white pill. Removing scale from the toolkit is what makes a maximally dense grid still read calm and deliberate. Transferable to dashboards/tools that are information-dense and can't afford big type.
- **A flat, edge-to-edge masonry of mixed-ratio plates says "studio," not "search results."** The whole viewport is project tiles — squares and portraits interleaved, no gutters of empty page, no cards or borders — so the eye scans plate-by-plate and the volume of work itself becomes the argument. (See `staggered-masonry-feed` for the layout mechanism; phantom's novelty is the metadata treatment, below.)
- **The brand mark is an illustrated character, not a wordmark — the one warm, human note in a cold system.** A chunky black-on-white cartoon "phantom" figure anchors the top-left. In an otherwise clinical mono/glass interface, one piece of personality-laden illustration carries the entire brand affect and keeps the precision from reading as sterile. Transferable: in a restrained system, spend *all* your warmth in a single deliberate spot (logo, one illustration, one motion) rather than diffusing it.
- **The work/about/careers nav is a persistent floating object, decoupled from the page.** Rather than a top bar that scrolls with content, primary nav is a small capsule that floats over the grid at bottom-center — always reachable, never occupying layout, reading as a piece of hardware sitting on top of the canvas. Transferable to long-scroll experiences and canvas/map UIs that want persistent, thumb-reachable controls.

## Design-system read
- **Type:** Two families, near-zero scale ratio. Helvetica Now (UI/body, weight 500) at ~16px does almost everything; ballinger-mono (weight 400) at ~11px does all the metadata/labels. A few large sizes (48–56px) appear only on the hero line and, ironically, the consent card's Accept/Decline. Hierarchy is *not* typographic — it comes from material (glass vs solid vs illustration) and position. The mono/sans pairing is the only real contrast device: mono = "system metadata / taxonomy," sans = "human voice." **Tracking does not scale with size here** — the capture returns an empty `tracking_by_size` and the h1 reads `letter-spacing: normal`, i.e. default tracking across the (shallow) size range; this is consistent with a site whose hierarchy is material/position, not type, so it never needs the tighten-big-display trick. `font-feature-settings` is effectively off (`"kern" 0` / unset). Type is fluid (`clamp`-based).
- **Color:** Two-value core — black canvas, white ink — plus a translucency ramp of white (`/0.15` fill, `/0.2` hairline, `/0.25` strong, `/0.4` muted text). No accent hue exists; the project thumbnails are the entire chromatic budget. The lone inversion is the consent card (white card / black text), which makes it the highest-contrast object on the page — appropriate for a legally-required interruption, debatable as a hierarchy choice (see safety note).
- **Spacing rhythm:** 8px base. Chip-level padding is extreme-aspect (3px block / 16–40px inline) which is what gives the metadata pills their flat, ticker-like proportion. Section padding jumps to 48px and occasionally 144/180px for the hero void. Grid gutters are tight (8/16/32px) so the masonry stays edge-dense.
- **Layout system:** Full-viewport masonry of mixed-aspect plates, minimal gutters, bleeding to all four edges. Floating controls are absolutely positioned to viewport corners/center, not in document flow. On mobile the grid narrows to ~1–2 columns and the same floating capsule/toggles persist — no separate mobile menu, the identity holds.
- **Motion / easing intent:** Two registers. The everyday grid uses fast neutral `ease`/`ease-in-out` at ~200–300ms for hover/opacity/border transitions (calm, instrument-like). The *authored* moments use the real `.motion.params`: an **expressive ease-out `cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)`** (long soft settle) and a **springy `cubic-bezier(0.93,-0.24,0.4,1.17)`** with negative control points (overshoot/anticipation) for the capsule/label reveal. Durations run **200 / 300 / 700 / 1000 / 1400ms** and there's a single **350ms stagger** delay — the longer 700–1400ms beats are the loader/page-transition and image-reveal sequences, not micro-states. The full choreography (which is largely canvas-driven, see below) is detailed in **Interaction & motion**.
- **Density:** Very high — a full screen of thumbnails, each carrying multiple mono metadata tags. It stays legible because (a) the chrome is monochrome and recedes, (b) metadata is tiny mono and reads as a texture/index rather than competing copy, and (c) whitespace inside chips and between the floating layer and the grid creates breathing room without sacrificing tile count.
- **Hierarchy:** Dominant ordering device is **material + position**: illustrated logo (brand) > filled-white pill (primary CTA) > glass capsule bottom-center (nav) > glass chips on media (metadata) > the plates themselves (content). Scale and color are deliberately *not* the ordering devices.
- **Restraint:** No chromatic accent. No drop-shadows. No heading scale to speak of. No borders on the work tiles. No gradients. No decorative dividers. The system says almost everything with one neutral, two type families, one translucency ramp, and a 14px radius.
- **Grid / container:** A 12-column base that collapses to fewer tracks for the inner clusters — captured columns `[2, 3, 12]` desktop (a 2-up metadata row, a 3-up sub-grid, the 12-col page grid) narrowing to `[2, 1, 3, 8]` at 390px (the masonry drops toward 1–2 columns while the floating chrome stays). **No fixed max-width — the masonry bleeds to all four viewport edges** (the lone ~1920px raw candidate is the full-bleed viewport, not a content column). Gutters are **measured at 8 / 16 / 32px**.
- **Components (from `.craft.states`):** Thin data — the capture recorded `focus_visible: true` globally but returned **empty `states.focus` and `states.disabled` arrays and zero hover deltas**, so per-component hover/focus/disabled transitions were not distilled from the DOM. What's observable: **Button** — two shapes, the filled-white "Let's Talk"/"Accept" pill (highest contrast) and the glass "Filter" chip; hover is the house ~200ms `ease-in-out` opacity/border shift (see *Frosted-glass hover-dim* below), exact deltas uncaptured. **Card (work plate)** — borderless media tile carrying mono metadata chips; the reveal/hover behaviour is canvas-mediated (below), no DOM state delta logged. **Nav** — the `Work/About/Careers` glass capsule, pinned bottom-center, `position: fixed`; item hover is the same opacity/border micro-shift. **Input** — none in the captured surface (no search/text field; "Filter" is a button, not an input). Treat button/card hover as "house glass micro-state," and supply explicit `:focus-visible` rings yourself since none were recorded per-component.

## Interaction & motion

**Honest scope first: this is a canvas-driven spectacle that is *not* DOM-distillable.** Across the 11 scroll frames (`s00→s10`) the masonry grid and every floating control are byte-stable in layout — the page does **not** scroll-translate; the DOM-diff shows **9 `position: fixed` pins** (grid, canvas, nav `ul`, Filter, hero/header, consent card, transition container) and exactly **one** real property animation: the timezone clock icon fading `opacity 0.85→0.40`. Yet the probes report **1 `<canvas>`, ~330 `requestAnimationFrame` callbacks, 13 `mousemove` listeners, 5 scroll listeners, 22 `@keyframes`, 12 animation rules** — so the actual motion lives in a **WebGL/canvas layer and cursor-driven JS that pixel-stills can't show** (adjacent frame hashes differ sub-perceptually = per-pixel shimmer, not visible DOM movement). Keyframe *names* are the richest surviving clue to intent. **Reduced motion is NOT respected** (`prmRules: 0`) — a real defect for a heavy-motion site.

- **Ambient WebGL canvas field (cursor-reactive)** — *Trigger:* load + hover/mousemove (continuous). *Mechanism:* a single pinned full-viewport `<canvas>` driven by a ~330-call `rAF` loop and **13 `mousemove` listeners**; pointer position feeds the shader/scene, producing a low-amplitude living backdrop behind the black grid. *Reads as:* the void is "alive" and reacting to you — the core agency-flex without any chromatic cost. *Transfers to:* dark hero/portfolio canvases that want presence without color. *Safety:* **spectacle-only** — a permanent rAF + 13 mousemove handlers is a sustained main-thread/GPU draw and a motion-sickness/battery cost; **no reduced-motion gate exists here**, so any reuse MUST add `prefers-reduced-motion` → freeze to a static frame, and throttle mousemove. Canvas-driven, so not DOM-distillable; judged from `canvas:1` + `rafCalls:330` + `mousemoveListeners:13`. → no DOM snippet; reuse the *principle* only. (`patterns_to_build: cursor-reactive-canvas-field`)

- **Image-reveal plate entrance** — *Trigger:* in-view (IntersectionObserver, **1 observer over 96 targets**, generous `rootMargin: 200px` so tiles arm before entering). *Mechanism:* `@keyframes styles_image-reveal` clips/fades each work plate in as it crosses the prefetch margin, on the expressive `cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)` ease-out with a **350ms stagger** between tiles. *Reads as:* the masonry "develops" plate-by-plate like film, rather than popping in. *Transfers to:* any gallery/case-study feed that wants an authored arrival. *Safety:* **production-safe IF gated** — clip+opacity are compositor-cheap and fire once, but here it's ungated; ship the resting (revealed) state under reduced motion. Evidence: keyframe name + IO(96 targets) + 350ms stagger param. → `metadata-tagged-thumbnail-plate` (static plate) + `scroll-reveal-in-view` (the in-view mechanic). 

- **Slow continuous rotation** — *Trigger:* load (ambient loop). *Mechanism:* `@keyframes styles_rotation` spins an element continuously (decorative mark / canvas accessory) independent of scroll. *Reads as:* a quiet "system is running" tick, like a radar sweep. *Transfers to:* a single ambient accent (loader, logo orbit, status dial). *Safety:* **production-safe in moderation** — one compositor-only `transform: rotate` loop is cheap; cap to a single element and gate reduced-motion (currently ungated). Evidence: keyframe name only (canvas/decorative, not seen translating in frames). → `scroll-progress-rotate` is the closest citable recipe (rebind from scroll to a capped loop).

- **Springy capsule / label reveal (signature)** — *Trigger:* load / hover. *Mechanism:* the glass capsule and its labels animate in on the negative-control-point `cubic-bezier(0.93,-0.24,0.4,1.17)` (anticipation then overshoot), paired with the `styles_ease-up`/`ease-down` keyframes. *Reads as:* one deliberately playful, springy gesture that injects character into an otherwise instrument-cold UI. *Transfers to:* a single hero CTA, nav, or badge entrance — the "spend all your bounce in one place" move. *Safety:* **spectacle-only-ish / safe if rationed** — overshoot adds perceived latency and reads toy-like if applied broadly; keep it to this one signature beat and gate reduced-motion. Evidence: easing param + `ease-up/down` keyframes. (`patterns_to_build: overshoot-capsule-reveal`)

- **Loader / page-transition skew sequence** — *Trigger:* load + route change. *Mechanism:* a `styles_TransitionElementContainer` (pinned) plus a dense `Loader_*` keyframe set — `move-out-left/right`, `skew-left/right`, `stretch`, `move-up/down` — choreographs an entry/exit wipe with directional skew and stretch over the longer **700–1400ms** durations. *Reads as:* a cinematic between-pages transition that hides load and asserts craft. *Transfers to:* SPA route transitions / intro sequences. *Safety:* **spectacle-only** — full-screen skew/stretch transforms are GPU-heavy and a vestibular trigger; gate reduced-motion (cross-fade instead) and keep them short. Evidence: keyframe family + transition-container pin + long durations. → no current recipe (`patterns_to_build: skew-stretch-page-transition`).

- **Blinking status dot + clock fade (the one DOM-visible micro-motion)** — *Trigger:* load (loop) / scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* `@keyframes Timezones_blinking-dot` pulses a live "online" dot, and the timezone icon's `opacity` eases `0.85→0.40` on scroll (the single non-pin scroll effect the diff actually caught). *Reads as:* a live HUD detail — "real studio, real time." *Transfers to:* footer/HUD presence indicators. *Safety:* **production-safe** — tiny opacity loop, negligible cost; still respect reduced-motion (hold steady). Evidence: keyframe name + the lone captured opacity scroll-effect.

## Production-safety note
- **Monochrome chrome + content-as-color** — **production-safe.** Near-zero cost; arguably *cheaper* and more accessible than a colored system. Caveat: with no accent, interactive affordance must be carried by the glass material, the one filled CTA, and motion — make sure hover/focus states are unmistakable since color can't disambiguate them.
- **Frosted-glass elevation (`backdrop-filter: blur`)** — **production-safe, with a perf + a11y caveat.** Cheap to author and the right elevation model on dark/photographic backgrounds. Cost: `backdrop-filter` is a real compositing expense — animating it, or stacking many blurred layers over moving content, can drop frames on low-end GPUs; keep blurred surfaces few and mostly static. A11y: text on a `/0.15` translucent fill over arbitrary thumbnails can fail contrast — back critical text with a minimum-opacity scrim or a solid plate, never trust the blur alone to guarantee legibility.
- **Hierarchy by material/position instead of type scale** — **production-safe** for tools, galleries, and canvas UIs; **use with care** for content/marketing pages. Mechanism is sound (position orders the eye), but it relies on the user learning the material grammar; on a first-visit marketing page a flat type scale can read as "where do I start?" Provide at least one unambiguous focal point (here, the filled CTA + illustrated logo).
- **Edge-to-edge mixed-ratio masonry** — **production-safe** where reading order is loose (portfolios, galleries). DOM order can diverge from visual order in column-flow masonry; wrong for content where sequence is load-bearing (use explicit grid + row-spans there).
- **Springy overshoot easing (negative-control-point cubic-bezier)** — **spectacle-only-ish / safe in moderation.** One reveal gesture = character; the same spring applied to every transition reads as toy-like and adds perceived latency. Cost is low if rationed to a single signature moment and gated behind `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Persistent floating capsule nav** — **production-safe.** Thumb-reachable and layout-free; ensure it doesn't occlude content at the bottom edge (offset the grid's final row) and that it's keyboard-reachable in a sensible tab order despite being visually decoupled.
- **Illustrated character logo** — **production-safe.** Pure brand upside, no perf/a11y cost beyond providing alt text.
- **Ambient cursor-reactive WebGL canvas + ungated loaders** — **spectacle-only.** A permanent rAF loop, 13 mousemove listeners, and full-screen skew/stretch transitions are a real main-thread/GPU/battery cost and a vestibular risk; the heaviest reuse risk on this site. Lazy-init, throttle mousemove, pause off-tab, and ship a static fallback.
- **A11y, honestly (from `.craft.a11y`):** Mixed. **Good:** `focus_visible: true` (a focus-visible mechanism exists globally), and `img_alt_ratio: 1` — every captured image has alt text (though only **1** `<img>` was present; the work plates are likely canvas/background media that need their own labelling, so don't read 100% as "fully captioned site"). **Defects:** `reduced_motion: false` / `prmRules: 0` — **no `prefers-reduced-motion` support at all**, the single biggest a11y gap given the ambient canvas, rotation loop, and skew page-transitions; and **4 small tap targets** flagged out of 105 interactive elements (the tiny mono chips / sound toggle likely fall under the 44px minimum — enlarge hit areas on touch). No per-component focus ring was distilled (`states.focus` empty), so verify the focus-visible styling is actually visible on the black field.
- **Capture note:** the screenshot shows a live editorial homepage (masonry feed + floating UI), **not** a preloader, WebGL-gate, or splash. A **cookie-consent wall is present** (white Accept/Decline card, bottom-right desktop / bottom mobile) overlaying the page; treat it as a captured overlay, not part of the core design. The hero line ("Phantom is a technology-led creative agency…") and a likely WebGL/canvas layer behind the grid are implied by the raw profile but the grid itself is the dominant captured surface.
