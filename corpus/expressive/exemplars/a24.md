## What works (transferable principles)
- **A two-register type system: display and UI, nothing between.** The stylesheet ships essentially two sizes — a 74px display (weight 500, `line-height: 0.92`, `letter-spacing: -0.04em`) and a 15px workhorse (weight 400, `line-height: 25px`, `-0.005em`) — a ~4.9× jump with no h2/h3 landing in the gap. Every heading on the page, from the hero film titles to a merch tile's name, is the *same* display size. The mechanism: rank comes from **scale + case**, not from a ladder of near-identical heading sizes, so a long catalog reads as a sequence of equally-weighted posters rather than a document outline. Transfers to any index/catalog page where items are peers. See `oversized-title-media-row`.
- **The palette is achromatic so the imagery is the only color.** White ground, black ink, `#888` secondary, `#c2c2c2` dim, `#eee` image plate. There is no brand hue — the orange `#f95936` and green `#b0e9ac` in the computed profile are form error/success tints only. Because nothing on the page competes chromatically, a single film still turns the viewport into a colour event. The mechanism is *withholding*: reserve all saturation for one content type. Transfers to portfolios, editorial, and any brand whose product is photography or video.
- **Full-bleed media as the section break.** The page alternates content rows with edge-to-edge, viewport-tall film stills carrying only an eyebrow (`WATCH NOW`) and a title in the corner. No dividers, no headings, no "Featured" band — the still *is* the chapter marker, and its darkness is what makes the white rows below feel like paper again. Cheaper and more confident than any decorative divider. Transfers to long marketing pages that need pacing without ornament.
- **Chrome that borrows the page's polarity instead of hiding from it.** The header is `position: fixed` and `background: transparent` while it sits over media, with white nav ink and a white burger; a JS-toggled `.sticky` class swaps it to a solid white bar with black ink (`transition: background-color .15s linear`). The burger's colour is a CSS custom property (`--burger-color`) that the same class flips, so one variable keeps the mark legible over both worlds. Transfers to any fixed header over hero media — and it's the honest, no-`mix-blend-mode` version (see `scroll-polarity-invert` for the cheaper scroll-driven variant).
- **Objects photographed, not screenshotted.** Merch and physical goods sit on a flat `#eee` plate with a layered, very low-alpha shadow (`rgba(0,0,0,.024) 2.1px 0 2.2px`, `…0.035 5.1px…`, `…0.043 9.5px…`) — a soft, directional falloff, never a single hard drop shadow. Product photography gets a seamless-backdrop studio treatment; the plate colour is a *system surface*, so a vinyl sleeve, a cap, and a mug all read as one catalogue. Transfers to any DTC or merch grid with heterogeneous product shots.
- **Metadata gets a monospace, and only metadata.** Years (`2026`) and dates are set in a mono companion face at tiny sizes with `letter-spacing: -0.065em`, superscripted beside the title. One secondary face, one job. Transfers as a rule: a second family earns its place only by owning a semantic category, never by decorating.
- **Restraint:** radius ≈ 0 everywhere (the only pills are form buttons), no gradients on content surfaces, no card borders, no `display: grid` at all — the whole layout is a flex utility with a 6px half-gutter — and no CSS `@keyframes` written by the site.

## Design-system read
- **Type:** one family, NB International (a neo-grotesque), plus NB International Mono for metadata; Akzidenz-Grotesk is loaded but not exercised on this page. Scale is breakpoint-swapped, **not fluid**: display 38 → 62 → 74px (`@1024` / `@1300`); body flat at 15px; eyebrow 13 → 15px uppercase with `font-feature-settings: "cpsp"` and `+0.005em`. Tracking is size-proportional and negative at the top (`-0.04em` at 74px, `-0.005em` at 15px). Weights 400 (text) / 500 (display) / 700 (rare). Hierarchy = **scale**, reinforced by **case**; weight never carries rank.
- **Color:** `#ffffff` ground · `#000000` ink · `#888888` eyebrows and secondary · `#c2c2c2` dim/disabled and on-dark secondary · `#eeeeee` image plate · `#000000` inverse surfaces (footer, newsletter modal, off-canvas menu) with `#ffffff` ink. Roles invert wholesale by surface, not by token variant. Semantic-only: `#f95936` (error tint) and `#b0e9ac` (success tint).
- **Spacing rhythm:** base unit **6px**. Section gutter `24px`; header gutter `42px` (`--header-padding-horiz`, → `18px` under 640px); the flex grid uses a `-6px` negative margin against `6px` child padding (12px gaps). Header clearance is a token that steps 108 → 148 → 196px across breakpoints — the page's one deliberately large constant, and it's what makes the hero titles feel dropped into place rather than crammed.
- **Layout system:** no CSS Grid. A single `.grid` flex utility (`flex-flow: wrap`, negative-margin gutters) composes everything; rows split 60/40, 40/60, or 50/50. Breakpoints: 640 · 1024 · 1300 (plus 666/667/1150 for the logo+burger sizing ramp and 1550/2000 for very wide displays). Container widths are `100%` / `80%` — the page has essentially no max-width, so full-bleed is the default and the *text column* is what's constrained.
- **Layering / page architecture:** single flat scroll, no static backdrop, no blend overlay (`has_static_backdrop: false`, `has_blend_overlay: false`). Seven `fixed` layers, all chrome: the header (z 3), the burger (z 4), the off-canvas menu (z 3, `pointer-events: none` until opened), the newsletter aside (z 4), and the cookie SDK's absurd `z-index: 2147483647`. Sections themselves are transparent onto white body — the `transparent_height_pct: 100` reading is the default-transparent artefact, not a reveal effect. Depth comes from **content**, not z-axis: a viewport-filling photograph is the only thing that recedes.
- **Motion / easing intent:** functional, short, and CSS-only. Durations cluster at 120/150/200/250ms for chrome and 600ms for media reveals; easings are the browser keywords plus two hand-cut curves — `cubic-bezier(0, 1, 0.25, 1)` and `cubic-bezier(0, 1, 0.6, 1)` — both "instant start, long glide," used for the burger morph and the off-canvas container slide. Motion says *the interface is responsive*; the one expressive gesture is reserved for photography.
- **Density:** low per-screen, high per-scroll. Each row carries one image, one eyebrow, one title, one link — the 12k-pixel page is a slow reel, not a dense grid.
- **Hierarchy:** scale, absolutely. Position (corner-anchored titles) is the secondary device.
- **Restraint:** no second display face, no accent colour, no rounded cards, no shadows on layout surfaces, no scroll-jacking, no parallax, no cursor follower, no `@keyframes`.

## Interaction & motion (CSS-only; no libraries, no rAF, no keyframes)
Probe: `libraries: []`, `rafCalls: 0`, site-authored `@keyframes: 0` (the two counted are `spin` and OneTrust's fade), `transRules: 102`, one IntersectionObserver at `threshold: [0.5]`, six scroll listeners. Everything below is a class toggle driving a CSS transition.

- **Rack-focus image load.** *Trigger:* load (per-image). *Mechanism:* the `figure` holds `filter: blur(28px) saturate(110%)` + `transform: scale(1.04)` in its `.loading` state and transitions both to rest over `0.6s ease-in`; a separate `opacity 150ms linear` fades the `<img>` in as it decodes. *Reads as:* a lens pulling focus — the still resolves rather than appears, which is exactly the register a film studio wants. *Transfers to:* hero media, portfolio grids, any page where images arrive late and you'd otherwise ship a pop-in. *Safety:* **spectacle-only by default** — a large-radius blur on a viewport-sized image is a real GPU/paint cost and it re-rasterises on resize; gate it to one run per image, cap the radius, and drop to a plain opacity fade under reduced motion. See `rack-focus-image-load`.
- **Transparent-over-media header that solidifies.** *Trigger:* scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* `header.hero:not(.sticky)` is `background-color: transparent` with white nav ink; adding `.sticky` transitions `background-color` to white over `150ms linear` while `--burger-color` flips black. Confirmed by the frames (burger white on the hero and the film-still breaks, black over the white rows) and by `scroll_effects` (`header.home` background `rgba(0,0,0,0) → rgb(255,255,255)`; `button.burger` colour white → black). *Reads as:* the chrome is a lens cap that closes when there's nothing behind it. *Transfers to:* any fixed header over hero media. *Safety:* **production-safe** (colour/background only) — but the site drives it from six scroll listeners; use an IntersectionObserver sentinel or `animation-timeline: scroll()` instead.
- **Featured-title stack with an inverted active marker.** *Trigger:* in-view / timed cycle (class toggle, not `:hover`). *Mechanism:* six film titles stack in the hero at full display size; `.featured-item-title.active h1 { opacity: 0.6 }` — the currently-showing still's title *dims* while the others stay at 1.0. Across the frame sequence the dimmed title migrates (`Onslaught 0.60 → 1.00`, `The Debut 1.00 → 0.60`), so the marker cycles with the backdrop. *Reads as:* a marquee where the film now on screen is "spent" rather than "selected" — an inversion of the usual active-is-brighter convention, and a defensible one because the *image* already announces the selection. *Transfers to:* any list bound to a single media stage (film slates, case-study heroes, tabbed showcases) — but pick a direction and mark it with more than opacity if the list is a real control.
- **Sliding arrow link.** *Trigger:* hover. *Mechanism:* the label `<span>` and an arrow `::before` (a background SVG) both `translate3d` on hover, `200ms ease-in-out` with a `25ms` delay on the label, so the arrow leads and the text follows. *Reads as:* the link is already leaving. *Transfers to:* every "SHOP NOW / LISTEN NOW / READ MORE" affordance; costs nothing. *Safety:* **production-safe** (transform only). Bundled into `oversized-title-media-row`.
- **Off-canvas menu that pushes the page.** *Trigger:* tap/click. *Mechanism:* the panel is `position: fixed` and always present at `opacity: 0; pointer-events: none`; opening translates the entire `.site-container` (`transform 0.25s cubic-bezier(0, 1, 0.6, 1)`) while the burger's two bars rotate to an X on the same curve. *Reads as:* the page slides aside rather than being covered. *Transfers to:* mobile nav on content-heavy sites. *Safety:* **production-safe** (transform/opacity), but you must trap focus and set `inert` on the pushed container — the source leaves it focusable.

**Reduced motion:** **not respected** (`prmRules: 0`). The rack-focus reveal is the one effect here that can genuinely bother a vestibular user (blur + scale on a full-viewport image); any reuse must ship the `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` fallback, which both patterns below do.

## Production-safety note
The type system, achromatic palette, image plate, full-bleed breaks, sliding arrow, and header inversion are all **production-safe** and cheap. Three cautions for reuse:
1. **Rack-focus load is spectacle-only until you gate it.** Blur radius × image area is the cost; run it once, on `decode()`, under `content-visibility`/IO, and never on scroll.
2. **White display type sits directly on the still with no scrim.** A24 gets away with it because the stills are colour-graded dark exactly where the title lands. You will not control your images that tightly — add a bottom-anchored gradient scrim and verify contrast against the *photograph*, not a swatch.
3. **Tap targets.** The probe counts 18 small targets out of 114 interactive elements — mostly the 13px uppercase eyebrow links and the mono year labels. Size the hit area, not the glyph. Focus outlines are present (`1px #101010`) and alt coverage is 100%; keep both.

## Extracted patterns
- `oversized-title-media-row` — alternating catalogue row: object on a flat grey plate, display-size title + eyebrow + sliding arrow link opposite. Reuse for merch grids, podcast/episode indexes, and any list whose items are peers.
- `rack-focus-image-load` — images resolve out of a blur+scale defocus instead of popping in. Reuse for hero and portfolio media; reduced-motion drops to an opacity fade.
