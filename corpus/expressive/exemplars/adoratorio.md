## What works (transferable principles)

- **A graphic made of real text reads as craft, not decoration.** The hero's circular mark is built from the brand words (`SHINE` / `ENNI…`) repeated and placed on a ring in ghost-grey — every node is a letter, not an image. Mechanism: *shape-from-repetition* gives a strong gestalt (a circle) out of many low-contrast meaningful units, so it's a second-read moment (you see the ring, then notice it's words) and the glyphs stay crawler-readable and animatable per-character. Transfers to hero marks, loaders, dividers on any type-led brand — cheaper and more on-brand than bespoke illustration. (Animation is spectacle-only; the static ring is fine.)

- **Scale alone can carry the entire hierarchy.** The page runs essentially two type sizes — ~15px body and a wordmark an order of magnitude larger that bleeds off both edges — in a single weight. Mechanism: when one element is ~10–15× everything else, "this is the title" needs no bold or color, freeing weight/value for other jobs and letting the rest of the system stay flat. Transfers broadly; in product UI cap the ratio nearer 4–6× so mid-level text keeps a legible rung.

- **Overflowing the frame is a confidence signal.** `Adoratorio® Studio` is set bigger than the viewport and clips at the margins. Mechanism: a name *bigger than the page* reads as a brand statement, and clipped edges imply continuation (the eye completes the word), so a static line feels expansive. Bottom-anchoring turns it into a horizon that grounds the floating chrome above. Transfers to agency heroes, footers, splash screens — production-safe if it's a real selectable heading with overflow clipped (no horizontal scrollbar).

- **Margins hold the apparatus, center holds the spectacle.** Identity (top-left), location quips (`Manywhere in Europe`, `(not Milan)`), a live clock (`22:04:55`), and `Contact` sit at the edges; the ring + `scroll down` own the optical center. Mechanism: separating chrome from the hero object lets each be information-dense without crowding, so the page feels minimal yet says a lot. Transfers to dashboards, editorial layouts, any hero needing chrome without clutter.

- **Personality lives in the copy, not the chrome.** The visual system is austere Swiss (one font, two tones), but the words are playful: `Manywhere in Europe`, `(not Milan)`, `Independent Design Studio based in Brescia and Amsterdam.` Mechanism: routing all warmth into microcopy lets the layout stay disciplined and "designed" while the brand still feels human — voice and form do different jobs. Transfers to any minimalist system that risks feeling cold: keep the grid strict, make the text talk.

- **A light canvas is a valid expressive register.** Most spectacle studio sites go near-black; this one is warm light-grey `#e6e6e6` with near-black ink. Mechanism: the same monochrome-plus-one-accent discipline inverted to light reads as gallery/print rather than cinema, and high black-on-grey contrast keeps body text crisp. Transfers as a reminder that "expressive" ≠ "dark" — the system (one hue family, rationed accent, scale-driven hierarchy) is what makes it expressive, not the value.

- **Blend foreign logos into the palette instead of re-drawing them.** The press wall hosts third-party marks (`The New York Times`, `GQ Italia`, `WWD`, `NSS`) — normally a clash of colors and lockups — yet each tile carries `mix-blend-mode: difference` over the `#e6e6e6` canvas. Mechanism: *difference blend* subtracts each pixel from the background, so a light logo resolves toward near-black ink and the whole wall snaps into the site's two tones without editing a single SVG. Transfers to any monochrome brand system that must display client/press logos: one CSS line enforces the palette on assets you don't control — cheaper and more consistent than hand-recoloring, and it degrades to the raw logo where unsupported. (Watch contrast: a mid-grey mark can wash out against a mid-grey ground — test each logo, don't assume.)

## Design-system read

- **Type:** One grotesque — the studio's own **PP Adoratorio** (a Helvetica-class Pangram-Pangram face; the screenshot reads as neutral Swiss grotesque, but the computed family is this named custom font, not literal Helvetica), **weights 400 + a sparse 700**. Effectively two everyday sizes — body ~15px (0.78rem, the dominant node) and labels ~12.5–16.5px — plus a 22.5px sub-head and a display wordmark far off the body scale (it overflows 1440px on one line; sized by viewport, not a fixed token). Leading ~1.0 on display, ~1.2 on body. **Per-size tracking was not captured** (`type_detail.tracking_by_size: []`, `font_features: inherit`, `fluid_type: false`); the screenshot reads near-zero, very slightly negative at display size — treat that as an eyeball estimate, not a measured ramp. Hierarchy is **scale**, never weight.
- **Color:** Two tones in the capture — warm light-grey canvas `#e6e6e6` and near-black ink `#141414`. A ghost grey `#808080` carries the ASCII ring + secondary labels. A single orange accent `#ff752c` is raw-confirmed but **does not appear in the captured viewport** — it's the rationed brand hue on off-capture interactive elements (hover fills, work links, an inverted dark pill at `#141414`). Monochrome-plus-one; no semantic palette.
- **Spacing rhythm:** Small base (~4.5px half-step, ~9px step). Micro padding on the pill button (22.5px inline / 4.5px block) against large structural voids between the corner chrome and the centered ring. Contrast of tight boxes vs open field is the rhythm.
- **Layout system:** Fixed-corners "instrument panel" — masthead top-left, two metadata columns + live clock top-center, `Contact` top-right; the ASCII ring + `scroll down` float in the center; the giant wordmark bleeds across the bottom edge. Mobile keeps the same logic stacked (chrome collapses to two corners, ring centered, wordmark still bleeding).
- **Layering / page architecture:** Flat z-axis with one deliberate compositing exception. The hero is a stack of **5 `fixed` full-viewport layers** — a base `div#app` painting the `#e6e6e6` canvas, the pinned masthead (`z 30`), a transparent `app-stage` / `main`, and a pointer-none `scroll down` overlay — that Locomotive's virtual scroll drives content *beneath*; below it **38 `sticky` layers** pin (the 24 work articles ride one crowded `z 5` plane under a `z 100` `archive-list__head`). **No static backdrop and no full-viewport blend overlay** (`has_static_backdrop` / `has_blend_overlay: false`) — depth here is *ordering*, not parallax planes. The one real blend move is **element-level**: the press wall sets **11 logo tiles to `mix-blend-mode: difference`** (`.press-grid__image`, raw-confirmed) so third-party SVG marks (`The New York Times`, `GQ Italia`, `WWD`, `NSS`…) invert against the light-grey canvas into near-black ink — the mechanism that keeps foreign imagery inside the two-tone system (below the pinned hero, so absent from the captured frames; grounded in CSS + alt text). See **What works** for the transfer.
- **Motion / easing intent:** One house easing — `cubic-bezier(0.215, 0.61, 0.355, 1)` (ease-out-cubic, easeOutCubic) — applied across the full duration ladder `200 / 500 / 750 / 1000 / 1250ms` with a single `150ms` stagger step (all raw-confirmed in `motion.params`; the only other easing seen is the bare `ease` Locomotive uses internally). Short curves = hover/state polish; the long 1–1.25s curves drive the canvas ring and the load assembly. Consistent curve = coherent feel; the variety is in timing, not shape. Full choreography, evidence, and per-technique safety in **Interaction & motion** below.
- **Density:** Looks sparse, reads dense — identity, location, a live clock, contact, a brand mark, and a scroll cue are all present, kept legible by pushing detail to tiny corner type and leaving the center open.
- **Hierarchy:** Dominant device is **scale** (bleeding wordmark vs whisper chrome), with **position** ordering the rest (center = subject, corners = controls).
- **Restraint:** No gradients, no shadows, square corners (one full-round pill aside), one font, two tones in-hero, no decorative color in the capture, no imagery. The wow is one text-built motion object; everything else is deliberately plain.

## Interaction & motion

Adoratorio's motion is **a single sustained spectacle object** — a full-canvas ASCII particle field — wrapped in **Locomotive smooth-scroll** and a staggered load, with a long pinned work-index below the hero. It is the inverse of teak's many-layered Framer choreography: one heavy continuous effect, not dozens of scroll-bound ones. Evidence from the capture: **1 `<canvas>`**, **477 `requestAnimationFrame` calls** in one pass (the per-glyph loop running every frame — the page never sits still even without scrolling), **3 `IntersectionObserver`s over 44 targets** (threshold `0`, fire-on-first-pixel), **1 scroll + 1 mousemove listener** (Locomotive's virtual scroll + a pointer hook for the particles), **9 entrance reveals** with a `150ms` stagger, and **43 `pin` scroll-effects** — the digest collapsed every scroll-effect to "pin", and they map to the hero stage (`div.app-stage`, the masthead, `scroll down`) plus **24 work articles** (`01 Gucci Time Maze` → `24 …`) that pin/scrub as a sticky index *below the captured hero*. Stack is **Locomotive (`data-scroll`)** — **not** hand-rolled; only **3 trivial `@keyframes`** exist (`autofill`, `flicker`, `blink` — the autofill reset + a blinking caret/clock colon), **0 CSS animation rules**, **12 transition rules**. **`prefers-reduced-motion` is NOT respected** (`prmRules: 0`, `a11y.reduced_motion: false`) — the page's biggest production liability, identical to teak.

**The four scroll frames (`corpus/expressive/screenshots/adoratorio-studio/frames/`, s00→s09) did not capture the choreography.** The page is pinned under Locomotive's virtual scroll, so the headless capture couldn't advance it — the giant `Adoratorio® Studio` wordmark, corner chrome, and `scroll down` cue stay locked frame-to-frame, and the 24 pinned work articles never enter view. What the frames *do* prove is the **continuous canvas loop**: the scattered `SHINE` / `ENNI…` glyphs drift to new positions and densities between every still, and the **live clock** ticks exactly `17:45:52 → :56 → 18:00 → :04` (4s/frame). Motion below is therefore grounded in probe counts (rAF / IO / canvas / pin / mousemove), `@keyframes` names, the entrance table, and DOM — not in frame-to-frame visual diffing of the scroll. Frames cited where they corroborate.

- **ASCII particle field (canvas loop)** — *Trigger:* load → continuous, pointer-reactive. *Mechanism:* a `<canvas>` (entrance `opacity 0.00→0.83` at `800ms`) runs a per-glyph rAF loop (477 rAF/pass) that places the brand letters on a ring and continuously drifts/scatters them; the single `mousemove` listener perturbs them toward/away from the pointer. *Reads as:* a logo made of live text — a second-read moment (you see a shape, then read the words). *Transfers to:* hero marks, loaders, dividers on type-led brands. *Safety:* **spectacle-only** — a never-idle main-thread/GPU loop with zero semantic payload; gate behind `prefers-reduced-motion`, render the underlying phrase as real text, pause off-screen. → `ascii-particle-ring`.
- **Staggered load entrance** — *Trigger:* load. *Mechanism:* the corner chrome (identity, `Manywhere in Europe`, `(not Milan)`, the clock, `Contact`) reveals first at `firstChangeAt 150ms`, the canvas fades up at `800ms`, and `scroll down` lands last at `1200ms` — a `150ms`-stepped sequence on `cubic-bezier(0.215,0.61,0.355,1)`. (The `transform 1.00→1.00` deltas in the entrance table are scale-noise — dropped; the *opacity + timing* is the real signal.) *Reads as:* the instrument panel powering on edges-first, then the spectacle. *Transfers to:* any splash/hero that should assemble rather than appear. *Safety:* **production-safe** if the resting state ships in the DOM and the stagger collapses to 0 under reduced-motion; the `1200ms` tail is **spectacle-leaning** (perceived latency before the page settles). → `scroll-reveal-in-view` (group/load variant).
- **Pinned hero + scrubbed work index** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress (43 `pin` effects: `div.app-stage`, masthead, `section "scroll down"`, plus 24 `article` work items `01…24`). *Mechanism:* Locomotive holds the hero stage fixed while the page's virtual scroll drives a sticky work index beneath it — each project article pins/scrubs as it crosses. *Reads as:* a stable stage on which the portfolio advances — scrollytelling for a case list. *Transfers to:* agency/portfolio work indexes, deep-dive feature spines. *Safety:* **production-safe in principle** (native `position: sticky` does this with no JS) but **spectacle as built** — routed through Locomotive's virtual scroll, which hijacks native scrolling, fights reduced-motion, and is a momentum/motion-sickness vector; prefer real sticky + CSS scroll-driven animation over a smooth-scroll library. → `sticky-pin-scrollytelling`.
- **Edge-bleed wordmark (static)** — *Trigger:* none (resting). *Mechanism:* `Adoratorio® Studio` set far larger than the viewport, bottom-anchored, clipped at both margins — confirmed locked across all frames (it does not parallax or scrub in the capture). *Reads as:* a brand statement bigger than the page; the eye completes the clipped word. *Transfers to:* agency heroes, footers, splash screens. *Safety:* **production-safe** — must be a real selectable `<h1>` with overflow clipped (no horizontal scrollbar). → `edge-bleed-wordmark`.
- **Live ticking clock** — *Trigger:* `setInterval` (1s). *Mechanism:* a local-time `HH:MM:SS` readout updates every second (frames confirm `:52→:56→:00→:04`); the `blink`/`flicker` keyframes likely pulse the colon. *Reads as:* a present, "we're live in Europe" signal — ambient personality, not data. *Transfers to:* studio/agency chrome, status bars, monospace HUD furniture. *Safety:* **production-safe** (trivial JS); mark the node `aria-hidden` (decorative, not information) and don't animate the colon under reduced-motion. *(No corpus recipe — see `patterns_to_build`.)*

> **Hover / cursor: not captured.** `hoverEffects: 0` and `cursorFollowers: 0` in the profile — the capture didn't exercise pointer states, and there are no hover deltas or cursor-follower nodes in the data. Given the house micro-durations (`200ms` ease-out-cubic) and the inverted dark pill in the tokens, expect link/CTA hover recolors off-capture, but **none are evidence-backed here** — do not assume them.

## Production-safety note

- **Light monochrome + single rationed accent** — **production-safe.** High black-on-grey contrast, low maintenance. Cost: none significant; just ensure the off-capture orange meets contrast where it carries meaning (links/states), not just decoration.
- **Two-size, scale-driven hierarchy (one weight)** — **production-safe.** Fewer decisions, accessible. Cost: in product UI the extreme ratio leaves mid-level text homeless — add an intermediate rung (sub-nav, captions) so not everything is either body or hero.
- **Edge-bleed wordmark** (`edge-bleed-wordmark`) — **production-safe.** Cost: must be a real selectable `<h1>`, not an image; clip overflow so it never causes horizontal scroll; verify it doesn't push the layout wider than the viewport on small screens.
- **Corner metadata + live clock chrome** — **production-safe.** Cheap text that sets tone; the JS clock is trivial. Cost: keep edge labels in logical DOM/reading order and sized for zoom/contrast, or tiny corner type fails a11y; the clock should be `aria-hidden` (ambient, not information).
- **Measured a11y defects (be honest — this is an expressive site, it fails the usual checks):**
  - **reduced-motion: NOT respected** (`a11y.reduced_motion: false`, `prmRules: 0`) — the continuous canvas loop and Locomotive smooth-scroll both run regardless of the user's setting. The single biggest fix before any production reuse: gate the particle loop + smooth-scroll behind `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` and ship the static ring.
  - **Tap targets: ~19 of 56 interactive elements are under 44px** (`small_tap_targets: 19`) — the tiny corner labels and `View more` work links are below the touch minimum; enlarge hit areas on mobile.
  - **`:focus-visible`: no global system** (`focus_visible: false`) — but the masthead `Contact` link *does* get a `1px #101010` outline, so focus styling is partial, not absent. Add a universal focus-visible ring before shipping.
  - **alt text:** `img_alt_ratio: 1` (all 47 captured `img` nodes carry alt) — good, though on a canvas/text hero most "imagery" is the work-index thumbnails below the fold; verify the alts are descriptive, not filenames.
- **House ease-out-cubic transitions (0.2–0.5s)** — **production-safe.** Exactly the right register for hover/state feedback; curve and short durations port directly to product UI.
- **ASCII particle ring** (`ascii-particle-ring`), the assemble/scatter motion — **spectacle-only.** The animated per-glyph ring is continuous/JS-driven (CPU cost, dozens of animating nodes, no semantic payload). Gate behind `prefers-reduced-motion`, and render the underlying phrase as readable text so it degrades to content. The *static* ring is safe; the *motion* is show.
- **Long 1–1.25s reveal/assembly timings** — **spectacle-only.** Second-plus animations add perceived latency before the page settles; fine on a studio splash, costly where users have a task. Keep skippable and never block first paint or critical content behind the reveal.
