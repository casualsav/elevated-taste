## What works
- **The headline brackets the hero object instead of sitting beside it.** The display sentence is split — "We make hardware" caps the top, "for human creativity." bases the bottom — and a centered 3D sculpture sits *between* the two halves. The reader assembles one sentence by scanning past the object, so the spectacle is threaded *through* the message rather than decorating around it. Transfers to any hero with a strong central asset (product render, 3D scene, photo) where you want copy and image to read as a single unit, not a split-screen.
- **One serif does the entire job — display and body — with zero type pairing.** Hierarchy is built purely from *scale* (a ~3.7× jump, 16px body to ~60px display) and *centered measure width*, never from a second family or a weight contrast. The restraint is the signal: refusing the conventional serif-display/sans-body pairing makes the page read as a printed essay, which is exactly the "heirloom, not gadget" brand claim. Transfers anywhere you want a literary, considered voice — a single well-chosen text face beats a pairing more often than designers assume.
- **Whitespace is the only separator between sections.** No rules, no cards, no surface changes, no background bands — sections are delimited entirely by large vertical voids plus a small bold centered label ("What we make", "What we build for"). At high copy density the page still reads calm because the eye rests in the gaps. Transfers directly to long-form / docs / about pages: spacing rhythm replaces chrome.
- **A claymorphic matte 3D object carries all the visual interest, so the rest can be flat.** The single off-white sculpture is the *only* non-text element above the footer. Because it is desaturated and tonally matched to the bg, it adds form and craft without introducing a competing color or breaking the monochrome calm. Transfers as a strategy: spend your one spectacle budget on a single hero asset and keep everything around it austere, rather than spreading effects thin across the page.
- **Centered narrow measure forces a slow, deliberate read.** Body copy is locked to a single ~50–60ch centered column with generous leading; combined with the serif it sets a magazine cadence. Transfers to manifestos, essays, brand stories — anywhere comprehension and tone matter more than scannability.
- **The brand mark recurs as an ornate glyph, not a logo lockup.** The identity appears top-left as plain tiny text, then returns at the footer as a decorative mandala flourish — a print-style "end mark." Cheap, memorable, and reinforces the artisanal positioning. Transfers as a closing device for editorial pages.

## Design-system read
- **Type:** Single serif family (STK Bureau Serif; Iowan/Baskerville fallbacks). Scale is compact and intentional — body 16px, sub-lead 20px, display 59.6px (≈3.7× range, not the 5–6× of louder heroes). Tracking is negative and **scales with size**: the two measured points are display `59.59px → -1.19px` and sub-lead `20px → -0.2px` (≈-0.02em at both, applied as a raw px value rather than em). So the big display stays optically tight and even the 20px sub-lead carries a faint negative — body 16px is left effectively neutral. Hierarchy = scale + measure width, never weight or family. Weights stay light (400 body, 300/200 for larger optical sizes — weight *drops* as size grows, the inverse of a typical UI ramp, which keeps the big type elegant rather than heavy).
- **Color:** A near-monochrome warm system. Single canvas `#f1ece9` (warm bone), near-black serif ink for all text, no surfaces or borders, no accent color at all. The 3D object is rendered in desaturated off-whites tonally fused to the bg. Color does *zero* hierarchy work — a deliberate choice that makes the type and the one object do everything.
- **Spacing rhythm:** ~8px base; the operative unit is huge block voids between sections (multiple rem of vertical air). Small components (pill button, nav) use tight 6–20px padding. The contrast between micro-tight chrome and macro-loose sections is the whole rhythm.
- **Layout system:** Single centered column on a viewport-wide canvas — content measure ~50–60ch, the hero object slightly wider and centered on the same axis. No multi-column grid anywhere. Everything aligns to one vertical spine. Mobile is the identical structure at narrower width (true one-column design — nothing to reflow), the only change being the headline wrapping to more lines and the sculpture scaling down. The desktop hero brackets the object left/right of nothing — purely top/bottom — so the spine survives every breakpoint untouched.
- **Motion / easing intent:** Restrained. Durations cluster at `75 / 100 / 320 / 520 / 900ms` (micro pill/toggle states at 75–100ms, hover/state at 320ms, the `logo-rise` entrance at the long end). Easings are `ease` (default), `cubic-bezier(0,0,0.2,1)` (decisive ease-out, micro-interactions), and `cubic-bezier(0.2,0.7,0.2,1)` (a soft custom ease-out for the entrance). Stagger delays `60 / 220ms` exist but `staggeredEntrance:false` — only two reveals fire, not a sequence. **Despite that calm token set, the probe found ~6,555 rAF calls/scroll pass with zero canvas and zero libraries** — that load is the two cursor-followers (14 `mousemove` listeners) plus the `audio-bar-pulse` loop, not a 3D scene. The spectacle here is one well-funded *static* object, not animation. Full breakdown in **Interaction & motion** below.
- **Density:** High *textual* density (a full manifesto of copy) kept legible by the narrow measure, generous leading, and large inter-section voids. Visual density is near-zero — one object, all text.
- **Hierarchy:** Dominant ordering device is **scale**, secondarily **position** (centered + vertical order). Color and weight are deliberately removed from the hierarchy toolkit.
- **Restraint:** No second typeface. No accent color. No cards, dividers, shadows, or surface tints. No imagery except the one sculpture and the footer glyph. No bold weights. The page is an exercise in subtraction — its expressiveness comes from *what was removed*, plus one well-funded hero object.

## Interaction & motion

Despite the "agency/spectacle" pedigree, creative.inc is **deliberately the quietest motion site in the expressive set** — the spectacle budget is spent on one *static* claymorphic object, not on animation. Evidence from the capture: only **3 scroll effects, all `pin`** (kind `{pin:3}`); the `scroll_effects` diff yields **0 real transitions** once `from==to` noise is dropped — i.e. **no parallax, no scroll-scale, no scroll-rotate, no recolor on scroll.** The frames corroborate exactly: across s00→s09 the "Team +" pill nav holds its position while the body copy and the 3D sculpture scroll past as ordinary content — the object does **not** idle-spin or parallax between frames (the earlier static read's "likely a subtle scroll rotation" guess is **not supported** — retracted). Only **2 entrance reveals** (`opacity 0→1` on the hero copy block and `.logo-rise`), `staggeredEntrance:false`. **4 `@keyframes`** total: `route-fade-in` / `route-fade-out` (SPA route transitions), `logo-rise` (load), `audio-bar-pulse` (a looping equalizer glyph — the top-left `||` audio icon). The **~6,555 rAF/scroll** with **`canvas:0`, `libraries:[]`** is the cursor-follower + audio-bar loop, **not** WebGL — so the matte sculpture is near-certainly a pre-rendered still/sprite, not a live shader. **`prefers-reduced-motion` is NOT respected** (`prmRules:0`, `respectsReducedMotion:false`) — the one real liability. Stack: **hand-rolled** (`IntersectionObserver` ×2 · CSS `@keyframes` ×4 · rAF cursor/audio loop) — no GSAP/Framer/Lenis/Three.

- **Sticky pill nav over a scrolling manifesto** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress (the 3 `pin` effects). *Mechanism:* the centered "Team +" outline pill (and the top-corner audio/theme icons) hold `position: fixed`/`sticky` while the entire essay column scrolls beneath. *Reads as:* a calm persistent frame — one quiet action stays reachable through a long read, the page feeling like a bound document you page through rather than a deck you fly past. *Transfers to:* any long-form / manifesto / about page that wants a single persistent CTA without a heavy header bar. *Safety:* **production-safe** (native sticky/fixed, no per-frame JS, no reflow). → `sticky-pin-scrollytelling`.
- **In-view fade-up entrance on the hero block** — *Trigger:* in-view / load (2 `IntersectionObserver`s, `rootMargin:200px`; captured `opacity 0.16→1.00` on the "We make hardware…" block, `firstChangeAt:150ms`). *Mechanism:* the bracketed-headline block and wordmark cross-fade in on first paint via a single class flip → CSS `opacity` transition (`logo-rise` keyframe for the mark), fired once; `rootMargin:200px` pre-arms it just before it enters. *Reads as:* the page "settles onto the page" like print ink resolving — a one-beat arrival, not a performance. *Transfers to:* any editorial hero or section that wants a gentle entrance without a stagger cascade. *Safety:* **production-safe** (GPU-cheap opacity, once-only) **but** ships with **no reduced-motion gate** — fix by jumping to the end state under `prefers-reduced-motion`. → `scroll-reveal-in-view`.
- **Outline pill that fills on hover ("Team +")** — *Trigger:* hover. *Mechanism:* the 1px-outline stadium pill inverts on hover — captured `bg rgba(0,0,0,0)→rgba(0,0,0,0.93)`, label `#000→rgb(224,219,217)`, border to match — fill grows inside the hairline oval over ~320ms. *Reads as:* a quiet hairline shape at rest that doesn't compete with the serif, becoming solid and confident on intent. *Transfers to:* low-frequency CTAs / nav actions on a sparse editorial surface. *Safety:* **production-safe** (color/bg only, no layout shift). → `outline-pill-fill-button`.
- **Cursor-follower garnish (fine-pointer only)** — *Trigger:* hover / mousemove (2 `cursor-follower` nodes, 14 `mousemove` listeners; captured as continuously updating `matrix(1,0,0,1,…)` translations tracking the pointer across the viewport). *Mechanism:* small decorative elements lag-follow the cursor via a rAF translate loop. *Reads as:* a faint extra liveliness / craft signature on a desktop pointer; invisible on touch. *Transfers to:* brand-experience garnish on fine-pointer devices only — never load-bearing. *Safety:* **spectacle-only** — does nothing on touch, adds 14 `mousemove` handlers + a perpetual rAF loop (the bulk of the 6.5k rAF count), and is the first flourish to drop under a perf or reduced-motion budget; gate behind `(pointer:fine)` + `(prefers-reduced-motion:no-preference)`. (No corpus recipe — reuse the *principle* sparingly.)
- **Looping audio-bar pulse glyph** — *Trigger:* load (continuous). *Mechanism:* the top-left `||` equalizer icon animates bars via the `audio-bar-pulse` `@keyframes` on an infinite loop. *Reads as:* an "ambient sound on" affordance — a tiny living detail in an otherwise still page. *Transfers to:* a sound-toggle / now-playing indicator. *Safety:* **spectacle-leaning** — a perpetual animation is a (small) battery/main-thread cost and should pause when audio is off and under reduced-motion; cheap if it's a transform/opacity keyframe. (No corpus recipe → see `patterns_to_build`.)

## Components

- **Button / pill (`Team +`):** rest = transparent fill, 1px `#000` border, black label, full radius (`9999px`). Hover = fill `rgba(0,0,0,0.93)`, label/border `rgb(224,219,217)`. - **Nav:** a single link cluster (the top-left wordmark, `desktop_links:1`, `likely_hamburger:false`) plus the centered pill — no nav menu, no dropdowns, no captured hover beyond the wordmark focus outline. **Mobile keeps the same single-link nav (no hamburger).**
- **Input:** **not captured** — the page has no forms or text inputs (`disabled:[]`, no input states).
- **Card:** **not captured** — there are no cards, surfaces, or elevated containers anywhere (whitespace-only separation; `shadows:[]`).

## Production-safety note
- **Headline-bracketed object (top/bottom split around a centered asset)** — **production-safe.** Pure layout (flex column: cap → object → base). Cost: the split sentence must stay readable in DOM source order for screen readers — mark the two halves as one `<h1>` with the object `aria-hidden`. No perf cost if the centerpiece is an image; cost rises only if it's a live 3D canvas (see below).
- **Single-serif manifesto (one face for display + body)** — **production-safe**, and a genuine performance *win*: one font family = one (or two optical) web-font request. Cost: requires a serif that holds up at both 16px body and 60px display — many display serifs are unreadable small; test the actual face at body size before committing.
- **Whitespace-only section separation** — **production-safe.** Zero cost, improves a11y (no decorative noise). Caveat: needs disciplined spacing tokens or sections blur together; pair with the small bold centered labels for orientation.
- **Claymorphic 3D hero sculpture** — **production-safe as shipped here, spectacle-only if rendered live.** The capture shows `canvas:0` / no WebGL, so this site almost certainly serves a **pre-rendered still** (one of its 3 alt-tagged images) — the cheap, correct choice, since the object never animates across the frames. The expensive path (live Three.js) would add shader/geometry payload, GPU draw cost, and a real LCP/INP risk on low-end mobile for **zero visual gain** here. Rule: render this kind of static art object as an optimized still (WebP/AVIF), not a canvas.
- **Decorative footer glyph** — **production-safe.** A single inline SVG; mark `aria-hidden` (it's ornament, not information). Negligible cost.
- **A11y (measured, honest):** `img_alt_ratio:1` (all 3 images carry alt — good); a real **`1px rgb(16,16,16)` focus outline** is present on the wordmark + buttons (`states.focus`), though the probe flags **no dedicated `:focus-visible` ring** (`focus_visible:false`) and **2 of 6 interactive elements are under the 44px tap target** — the icon toggles need larger hit areas. The headline note above stands: the split sentence must be one `<h1>` in source order with the sculpture `aria-hidden`. **Biggest defect: `prefers-reduced-motion` is not respected** (`prmRules:0`) — the entrance fade, cursor-follower, and audio-bar loop all run regardless. For production, gate every one and ship the resting state in the DOM.
