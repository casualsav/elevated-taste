## What works
- **The spectacle is *framed*, not full-bleed.** The tumbling 3D cluster lives inside a rounded-corner dark "viewport" inset into a calm lavender page, with white chrome floating above it. This is the load-bearing decision: a contained canvas reads as a *deliberate object on a page* (legible, scannable, crawlable shell) rather than a WebGL gate that hijacks the whole window. Transfers to any hero where you want motion/3D drama without sacrificing the DOM, SEO, or a clear "this is a website" mental model.
- **Two-surface contrast does the heavy lifting.** A single near-black stage embedded in a near-white page creates maximum figure/ground separation for the 3D objects, while keeping all *text* on the light field where it's easy to read. The dark is rationed to exactly one rectangle. Transfers anywhere you need a "screen within a page" — product viewers, video embeds, demo panels — without committing the whole UI to dark mode.
- **Body-vs-huge type with no middle.** Hierarchy is built from a deliberate scale *gap*: ~16-21px running text jumps straight to a ~108-144px display headline, with the mid-scale empty. One family (Aeonik), one-to-two weights, negative tracking on the display. Drama comes from the size ratio and whitespace, not from weight, color, or a second face. Directly reusable in product UI to make one number/title dominate without bolding everything.
- **Chrome as a thin HUD, content as the event.** Wordmark, menu, and a single accent-adjacent CTA pill sit as small, evenly-weighted labels at the viewport edges; the center is reserved for the one moving thing. Restraint in the frame is what lets the spectacle land. Transfers to any landing page: keep navigation quiet so the hero can be loud.
- **One rationed accent.** Electric blue appears only in a few of the 3D primitives and the CTA region — roughly 1-2% of pixels. Because everything else is achromatic (white / slate / black), that single hue carries all the "brand energy" without a gradient. Transfers as a discipline: pick one saturated color, spend it in <5% of the surface.

## Design-system read
- **Type:** Single family (Aeonik), weights 400 (+ a thin 500 for chrome labels). Scale is bimodal at roughly a 1.5 ratio: body 1-1.35rem, then a jump to a 2.7rem sub-display and a 6.75-9rem (108-144px) hero. Hierarchy = scale gap + whitespace, not weight. **Tracking varies by size** (it is *not* a single global value): the structured `tracking_by_size` came back empty and the computed 36px h1 reads `normal`, but the giant hero display carries heavy negative tracking (raw `-2.3px`/`-2.88px` ≈ **-0.04 to -0.05em**) so the big glyphs stay optically tight — apply tightening only at display sizes, leave small UI text near-normal. Running copy sits near 1.5 leading, display near 1.0-1.15.
- **Color:** Five roles. `bg` cool lavender-white (#f0f1fa), `surface` pure white (chrome/cards), `fg` near-black (#121416), a dark slate `stage` (#2b2e3a -> #121416) used *only* inside the canvas, and one electric-blue `accent` (#1a2ffb, deeper #0016ec for fills). No mid-grays: secondary text is the same ink at smaller size. Relationship is achromatic page + one rationed hue + one dark inset.
- **Spacing rhythm:** 8px base, bimodal. A tight 8 / 14.4 / 28.8 micro-scale governs chrome gaps and label spacing; a coarse 50 / 72 / 168 macro-scale governs section padding and the stage inset margin. Intermediate steps are deliberately absent, which keeps the layout reading as "edges + one big object."
- **Layout system:** Edge-pinned HUD chrome (corners + top-center) around a single centered stage that nearly fills the viewport with a uniform inset margin. 1440-wide capture. On mobile the chrome collapses to a top wordmark + two corner icons and a bottom "SCROLL TO EXPLORE" bar; the stage becomes a tall portrait rectangle but keeps its rounded frame and inset — the art direction survives the breakpoint rather than reflowing to a generic stack.
- **Motion / easing intent:** Real `.motion.params` — durations cluster at **300 / 400 / 500ms** for UI transitions with a slow **3000ms** loop for the ambient scene and **100-250ms** micro-states; easings are a family of fast-out S-curves: `cubic-bezier(0.4,0,0.1,1)` (primary), `cubic-bezier(0.35,0,0,1)`, `cubic-bezier(0.1,0,0.1,1)`, `cubic-bezier(0.4,0,0,1)`, with bare `ease` as the lazy fallback. Stagger steps are bimodal: a fast **20/60/80ms** per-glyph cadence and a slow **200/300/400ms** for grouped reveals. The continuous tumble of the extruded-cross primitives (WebGL physics/materials, **not** in the computed-style tokens) is the ambient "this page is live" signal; the chrome barely moves. Full choreography in **Interaction & motion** below.
- **Density:** Very low above the fold — one sentence of copy, one CTA, one object. Legibility is trivially preserved because text never overlaps the busy canvas; the canvas is a separate rectangle. Information arrives on scroll, not at once.
- **Hierarchy:** Dominant ordering device is **position + scale**: the centered stage owns the optical center, the display headline owns the type scale, everything else is small and edge-pinned.
- **Restraint:** No gradients, no drop shadows on the dark stage, no second typeface, no decorative color beyond one blue, no full-screen takeover. The dark is confined to one rectangle; the accent is confined to a few elements. The spectacle is loud but the *system* is austere.

## Interaction & motion

Lusion's motion is **canvas-first spectacle on a near-inert DOM** — the opposite of Attio's hand-rolled IO reveals. Evidence from the capture: **362 `requestAnimationFrame` callbacks**, **3 `<canvas>`** nodes, exactly **1 `mousemove` listener**, and — tellingly — **0 IntersectionObservers, 0 scroll listeners, 0 hover effects, 0 cursor followers, no GSAP/Lenis/Framer/Three globals exposed**, only **2 CSS `@keyframes`** (`text-animation`, `arrow-animation`) and 3 animation rules over 61 transition rules. So nearly all the drama is *inside the WebGL stage and not DOM-distillable*. The frame deltas confirm it: across s00→s10 the top chrome strip (headline + nav) delta saturates at ~3.6 by s01 and then **never changes** (headline and nav are frozen), while the center-stage delta climbs monotonically 0→40 with a steady ~8-17/frame churn — a scene that tumbles continuously and never settles. **`prefers-reduced-motion` is NOT respected** (0 PRM rules). Stack: `<canvas>` + rAF (WebGL, library hidden) · CSS `@keyframes` · CSS transitions. (The 26 "scroll effects" the DOM scraper reports — 4 pins, 15 per-word `transform`/parallax, 7 per-word `rotate` — live in markup that the scroll≈0 frames don't reach, so those are judged from the markup, not motion I watched animate.)

- **Ambient WebGL tumble (contained stage)** — *Trigger:* load (continuous rAF, never settles). *Mechanism:* a cluster of extruded-cross / plumbing-joint primitives rotates and dilates inside the dark inset viewport, driven by a hidden WebGL loop (362 rAF, 3 canvas) — property is the 3D scene transform, input is time, not scroll. *Reads as:* the page is alive, a physical object spinning under studio lighting. *Transfers to:* any 3D/product-viewer hero where the canvas is one framed rectangle, not a full-bleed takeover. *Safety:* **spectacle-only** — persistent GPU/CPU load + battery drain on mobile, invisible to crawlers/SR, and here it ignores reduced-motion entirely. Gate it: lazy-mount, pause off-screen, ship a static poster under reduced-motion / no-WebGL. → reuse the `inset-canvas-stage` framing; the loop itself is a `canvas-loop` (no distillable snippet).

- **Cursor-reactive scene parallax** — *Trigger:* hover/pointer move (the single `mousemove` listener). *Mechanism:* pointer position feeds the WebGL camera/light so the cluster subtly shifts toward the cursor — input `mousemove` → output canvas camera transform. *Reads as:* the object acknowledges you; tactile, "I can touch this." *Transfers to:* hero canvases, 3D product shots where you want presence without click. *Safety:* **spectacle-only** — fine-pointer only (no touch payoff), adds to the rAF budget, no reduced-motion fallback observed. Degrade to a still scene on coarse pointers.

- **Per-word entrance with rotational skew** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress / in-view (markup evidence; not seen firing in the scroll≈0 frames). *Mechanism:* each headline word is its own `div` carrying an individual `transform` — the running-copy words translate (parallax), and the emphasis run "that / help / brands / stand / out" carries a *graduated* baked rotation `-1.3° → -2° → -2.9° → -4.3° → -6.3°`, so the line fans as it resolves. *Reads as:* a hand-set, kinetic headline rather than a static block; the skew gives it gesture. *Transfers to:* display headlines you want to feel composed/animated. *Safety:* **production-safe with care** — compositor-only `transform`; cap the angle and ship the resting (upright, settled) state in the DOM. → `parallax-drift` + `scroll-progress-rotate`.

- **Pinned chrome + stage during scroll** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* the `<canvas>`, the `header`, and the slide-over menu overlay are `position: fixed`/pinned so the stage and HUD hold while content scrolls beneath (4 pins). *Reads as:* the viewport-instrument stays put; the website scrolls *around* the spectacle. *Transfers to:* scrollytelling where one media stage anchors several copy beats. *Safety:* **production-safe** (native fixed/sticky, no JS scroll loop). → `sticky-pin-scrollytelling`.

- **Scroll-cue micro-loop** — *Trigger:* load (CSS keyframe). *Mechanism:* the bottom-center "SCROLL TO EXPLORE" arrow runs the `arrow-animation` keyframe (a small looping translate/opacity nudge); the bottom band is the only chrome region that micro-ramps in the frames (delta 0→12). *Reads as:* a quiet "there's more below" without a separate band. *Transfers to:* any single-screen hero that needs a scroll affordance. *Safety:* **production-safe** (one tiny looping transform; pair with `viewport-frame-ticks`).

**Components.** `.craft.states` came back essentially empty (`focus: []`, `disabled: []`, **0 hover effects** in the probe) — the DOM is near-inert, so these are read from CSS transitions + frames, not captured state deltas, and the honest note is *the interactivity lives in the canvas, not in styled component states*.
- **Button (CTA "LET'S TALK" / "MENU" pills):** stadium pill, `radius 100px`, accent-blue or white fill, small 500-weight label. *Hover:* a `cubic-bezier(0.4,0,0.1,1)` background/transform transition over ~300ms (transition rules present); exact hover delta **not captured** — implement as a fill-darken (`accent #1a2ffb → #0016ec`) or invert. *Focus/disabled:* **none captured** — must be added (see a11y).
- **Card (the stage / inset viewport):** `radius 15px`, dark `stage-bg #2b2e3a → #121416`, edge vignette `linear-gradient(270deg, rgba(11,11,18,0.5), rgba(11,11,18,0))`, single soft ambient shadow `0 6px 10px rgba(0,0,0,.04), 0 2px 4px rgba(0,0,0,.04)` on white floating chrome only (the stage itself is shadowless — separation is by inset + frame). No hover state (it's a stage, not a control).
- **Nav (edge-pinned HUD labels, 6 links):** small static text labels at the corners; no captured hover recolor, no underline animation — they rely on position + the one accent pill for emphasis. Mobile keeps all 6 links (`likely_hamburger: false`).
- **Input:** none present (no form on the captured hero).

**Grid / container.** Desktop is a **12-column** grid with **3** and **6**-column child spans `[12, 3, 6]` and a **measured ~28.8px gutter**, collapsing to **`[6, 3]`** on mobile (390vw); the stage occupies a near-full-bleed centered span with a uniform inset margin (**no fixed max-width** — the lone ~486px raw candidate is a child component, not the content column). Columns and gutter are measured, not frame-estimated.

**A11y (honest, with defects).** `focus_visible: false` — **no visible focus ring** (keyboard users are stranded; must add). `reduced_motion: false` — **`prefers-reduced-motion` is NOT respected**, and the heaviest thing on the page is an unbounded WebGL loop, so this is the most serious defect: ship a static poster fallback. **9 small tap targets** out of 40 interactive elements fall under 44px (the corner HUD icons/pills — enlarge on mobile). `img_count: 0 → img_alt_ratio: null` — there are **no raster images at all** (the hero is canvas + live text), so alt-text is N/A here, but that also means **the entire hero message is invisible to crawlers/SR unless the headline text is real DOM** (it is — good — but the spectacle itself conveys nothing to AT).

## Production-safety note
- **Inset framed canvas (the dark viewport on a light page)** — *production-safe.* The framing is plain CSS (rounded rect, inset margin, two-tone surfaces); only the *contents* are WebGL. Cost: low. Because the canvas is one element you can lazy-mount it, swap in a static poster image for reduced-motion / no-WebGL / crawler cases, and the surrounding page stays fully functional. This is the safe way to ship "3D hero" energy.
- **Continuously tumbling 3D primitive cluster (physics + materials)** — *spectacle-only.* Cost: high. Persistent GPU/CPU load and battery drain on mobile, invisible to crawlers and screen readers, and ignores `prefers-reduced-motion` unless explicitly gated. Treat as decoration layered over a real DOM; never put content or navigation *inside* the simulation, and provide a static fallback.
- **Edge-pinned HUD chrome** — *production-safe.* Fixed-position small labels; cost low. A11y caveat: ensure the floating white pills keep AA contrast over both the light page and the dark stage edge, and that tap targets meet 44px on mobile.
- **Body-vs-huge single-family type scale** — *production-safe.* Pure CSS; cost low. The only caveat is responsive clamping so the 144px display doesn't overflow narrow viewports (use `clamp()`), which the mobile capture confirms they handle.
- **One rationed accent on an achromatic field** — *production-safe, recommended.* Zero cost, improves clarity. The discipline (one saturated hue under ~5% coverage) is the most directly portable lesson here.

## Extracted patterns
- `inset-canvas-stage` — a dark, rounded "viewport" inset into a light page that holds an interactive/3D scene as a contained object; reuse for any 3D/video/demo hero that must keep a legible, crawlable DOM shell instead of a full-bleed WebGL takeover.
- `viewport-frame-ticks` — corner registration ticks + edge labels + a bottom-centered scroll cue that frame a media stage like a camera viewfinder; reuse to make an embedded canvas/video feel like an instrument and to signal "scroll" without a separate band.
