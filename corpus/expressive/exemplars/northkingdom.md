## What works (transferable principles)
- **One typeface, one weight, two roles.** A single grotesk (FK Grotesk Neue, 400) carries everything; a display cut of the *same* family (FK Display) does only the giant wordmark. There is no bold, no second family, no italic — hierarchy is built entirely from **scale** (a ~5× jump from 24px body to 115px display) and negative tracking. Transfers to any brand that wants editorial calm: pick one family, lock the weight, and let size do the ordering.
- **Negative tracking is the load-bearing display detail.** Body runs ~-0.01em; the display wordmark is pulled to ~-0.05em (-5.75px at 115px). At poster sizes default spacing looks gappy and amateur, so tightening is what makes the size read as intentional rather than just big. Universal rule for any oversized headline.
- **Light *is* the composition, not the geometry.** The hero is a near-black indigo void (`#050311`) with a single colored rim-glow blooming from behind the central object (center samples to a steel-blue `~#0f1d2f` while the corners stay flat black). The page is essentially empty; the only "design" is one object and the light on it. Transfers anywhere you want a premium dark hero without building a busy scene — spend the budget on lighting one focal element, not on filling the frame.
- **The brand mark is threaded into the wordmark.** "North ⬡ Kingdom" splits the two words to the left and right viewport edges and seats the hexagonal shield emblem dead-center between them on a single baseline. The logo is not a separate lockup off to the side — it *is* the punctuation of the headline. Makes the identity inseparable from the type and gives the hero a strong central axis for the 3D object/play target to occupy.
- **Spectacle is gated, not forced.** The centerpiece is a 3D-rendered shield that doubles as a play button for the "20 year showreel." Nothing animates or autoplays in the first frame except the ambient glow; the heavy media is one click away. This is the safe shape of a video/WebGL hero — a poster-state object with an explicit affordance, so crawlers, keyboards, and reduced-motion users get a complete, legible page.
- **A single signature easing unifies all motion.** One curve — `cubic-bezier(0.215,0.61,0.355,1)` at 0.6s (easeOutCubic) — appears on 42 elements, far more than any other transition. Reusing one ease/duration across hovers, reveals, and nav makes unrelated interactions feel like one hand made them; the brand "feel" lives in the curve.

## Design-system read
- **Type:** One grotesk family in two optical cuts. Text role: FK Grotesk Neue Regular, ~24px, line-height ~1.15, tracking -0.01em. Display role: FK Display, ~115px, line-height ~0.95, tracking -0.05em. Scale ratio body→display ≈ 4.7×. Nav/labels sit at ~13px. **Hierarchy = scale + tracking, never weight** (everything is 400).
- **Color:** Monochrome-on-void. Ground `#050311` (near-black warm indigo, *not* navy). Foreground pure white; secondary text `rgba(255,255,255,0.6)`; a single muted steel-blue `#4a5464` for tertiary marks. The only chroma is the blue **rim-light** rendered into the background, not declared as a fill. Roles: bg = void, fg = white, muted = white@60%, accent = the glow (no solid accent color).
- **Spacing rhythm:** Generous; the hero is mostly empty. Edge gutters ~20px on chrome, with the wordmark pushed fully to the bleed. Section padding is large (the showreel block uses very tall block padding). Whitespace does all the separating — there are no dividers or cards in the hero.
- **Layout system:** Full-bleed dark canvas. Top bar = small shield logo flush-left, text nav (Work / About / Contact) flush-right. Hero = three-zone horizontal axis (word — emblem/object — word). Mobile collapses the inline split into a stacked wordmark below a circular hamburger; the 3D object + play button stays center-stage. Breakpoint behavior preserves the *object-centric* composition even though the inline wordmark reflows to two stacked lines.
- **Motion / easing intent:** Captured params: durations cluster at **600ms** (the signature) with a 200/250/300/350/400ms band for small states and a ~1000ms outlier; the dominant easing is **`cubic-bezier(0.215,0.61,0.355,1)`** (easeOutCubic — soft, decelerating, premium), backed by `cubic-bezier(0.25,0.46,0.45,0.94)` (easeOutQuad), the house `cubic-bezier(0.2,0,0,1)`, `cubic-bezier(0,0,0.6,1)`, and bare `ease`/`ease-in-out`. Entrance is **staggered** (per-item delays 39 / 78 / 108 / 117 / 147ms). Intent: nothing snappy or playful; everything settles. The full scroll choreography (pinned chrome, a sticky image-marquee, in-view fade-ups) is detailed in **Interaction & motion** below — and unlike the static read implies, this page does NOT respect reduced motion.
- **Density:** Very low visual density, high *brand* density. One headline, one object, four nav words — yet it reads unmistakably as a confident studio because every element is maximal (giant type, full void, one perfect light).
- **Hierarchy:** Scale first, position second, color last. The eye goes display wordmark → glowing object/play → nav. No weight or color contrast is used to rank anything.
- **Restraint:** No second font family, no second weight, no gradient *fills*, no shadows on type, no decorative chrome, no autoplay, no scroll-jacking visible in capture. Color is withheld to a single ambient glow. The discipline is the point — one void, one object, one curve.
- **Tracking (measured):** two captured sizes, both pulled negative and **scaled to size** — body `24.35px → -0.243px` (**-0.01em**) and the display wordmark `115px → -5.75px` (**-0.05em**, i.e. 5× tighter at ~5× the size). Confirms the "tighten as it grows" rule; only these two type sizes were sampled (no mid-ramp data). Font-features: `tabular-nums`/`"tnum"` appear (number alignment), `fluid_type: false` — sizes step at the breakpoint, they do not `clamp()`.
- **Grid / container:** capture reports a **2-column** grid (`gridCols: ["2"]`, `grid.columns: ["2"]`) at 1440px — read as a two-up case-study layout below the single-axis hero (the hero itself is a 3-zone flex row, not this grid). On mobile (390px) the grid collapses to none (`grid_collapse.mobile: []`) — single column. Thin data: only column *count* was captured, no gutter/max-width/row values, so treat "2-up desktop → 1-up mobile" as the reliable claim and don't over-specify track sizing.
- **Components (from `craft.states` — mostly NOT captured):** **Nav** — text links (Work / About / Careers / Contact) with a real `focus-visible` outline `1px rgb(16,16,16)`; resting/hover deltas **not captured** (`hover: []`). **Button** — pill geometry inferred from tokens (radius 26px/100px) and the same `1px rgb(16,16,16)` focus outline; fill/hover states **not captured**. **Card** — case-study tiles + work thumbnails exist in the marquee, but no card state (border/shadow/hover) was recorded; the only elevation token is a faint `0 1px 2px rgba(0,0,0,0.15)` (7 nodes) and an image scrim `linear-gradient(rgba(0,0,0,0)→rgba(0,0,0,0.75))`. **Input** — **none on the page** (no form captured); no input states. Honest gap: this site exposes almost no component state machinery beyond the focus ring — components are inferred, not measured.
- **A11y (measured, incl. defects):** **Good** — `focus_visible: true` (real 1px outlines on all interactive nodes), `img_alt_ratio: 1.0` (all 52 images carry alt). **Defects** — `reduced_motion: false` / `prmRules: 0` (the marquee, fade-ups, and ambient glow all run regardless of the user's reduced-motion preference — the headline a11y failure, typical of expressive sites); **12 small tap targets** out of 26 interactive elements (~46% under the 44px minimum — the text nav + footer links are tight on touch); the steel-blue tertiary `#4a5464` on `#050311` is decorative-only (do not use for body — it would fail contrast). The focus outline `rgb(16,16,16)` is near-black, so on the dark void it relies on the link text itself for visibility — verify the ring is actually perceptible against `#050311`.

## Interaction & motion

North Kingdom's motion sits **much closer to Attio than to teak**: despite being an agency/spectacle site, it is **hand-rolled** (`libraries: []`, no GSAP/Lenis/Framer; 0 script hits) and modest on the hot path — **594 `requestAnimationFrame`** calls in one scroll pass (vs teak's ~5191), **10 `@keyframes`**, 7 CSS animation rules, 40 transition rules, **14 `IntersectionObserver`s over 43 targets**. The heavy media is gated: **1 `<canvas>`, 8 `<video>`**, and the hero object stays a poster + play affordance in the first frame (the showreel is one click away). The real signature is a **sticky image-marquee** (`keyframes ImageMarquee_scroll`, **95 marquee nodes**), not a parallax storm. **Frames did not capture the motion** — all 11 scroll stills are byte-identical (smooth-scroll / pinned-overlay capture: the hero overlay holds `position: fixed` so the viewport never repaints across stops), so every claim below is grounded in probe counts + `@keyframes` names + the DOM classes in `motion.scroll_effects`, not in watched frames. Stack: `IntersectionObserver` · CSS `@keyframes` · sticky/fixed pins · `<canvas>`/`<video>` (lazy). **Critically: `prefers-reduced-motion` is NOT respected** (`prmRules: 0`, `a11y.reduced_motion: false`) — the single biggest production liability here, same failure mode as teak.

- **Pinned chrome over a scrolling stage** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress (5 `pin` effects: `header "WorkAboutCareersContact"` and a duplicate nav block hold `position: fixed`; `HomeHero_homeHero__overlay` holds `fixed`; the marquee `stickyContainer` and the `footer` hold `position: sticky`). *Mechanism:* the nav + hero overlay stay fixed while sections travel underneath; no per-frame JS — native sticky/fixed. *Reads as:* a persistent stage on which the work passes by; the action (nav) is always reachable. *Transfers to:* persistent nav/CTA on a long agency scroll, or a section heading pinned over evolving case-study evidence. *Safety:* **production-safe** (native sticky/fixed, zero hot-path cost). → `sticky-pin-scrollytelling`.
- **Sticky image-marquee with scroll-linked drift** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress (`ImageMarquee_stickyContainer` pins while `rowMarqueeContent` gets a `transform` parallax; `@keyframes ImageMarquee_scroll`; **95 marquee nodes**). *Mechanism:* a row of work thumbnails ticks horizontally on a CSS keyframe loop while the container is pinned, and the row's translate is additionally nudged by scroll progress so the crawl speed couples to the read. *Reads as:* the studio's portfolio streaming past as an ambient band — "look how much we've shipped" absorbed, not studied. *Transfers to:* a work/logo/press thumbnail strip on any agency or portfolio page. *Safety:* **production-safe if budgeted** — translate-only and GPU-cheap, but it must pause on hover/focus, `aria-hidden` the duplicated run, and stop under reduced-motion (this site does none of that). → `announcement-ticker-marquee` (same mechanism, image payload instead of text — add `loading="lazy"`/`srcset` for the thumbs).
- **In-view staggered fade-up reveal** — *Trigger:* in-view (`@keyframes fade-up`, `image-fade-up`, `featured-case-fade-up`, `fade`; 14 IO over 43 targets at thresholds `0.25` / `0.5` / `0`, plus a `rootMargin: 200px` prefetch observer; staggered entrance, delays 39–147ms). *Mechanism:* one class flip on intersect → CSS transitions `opacity` + a small `translateY`, sequenced per item; fires once. *Reads as:* a long dark page that "settles in" case by case rather than popping. *Transfers to:* any tall portfolio/marketing page wanting entrance rhythm without scroll-jank. *Safety:* **production-safe** (GPU-cheap, once-only) — *except* it is NOT gated here, so motion-sensitive users get every reveal. → `scroll-reveal-in-view`.
- **Subtle scroll parallax on the marquee row** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress (the single `transform/parallax` effect, on `ImageMarquee_rowMarqueeContent`). *Mechanism:* the thumbnail row translates at a rate offset from the page scroll, so the band reads as a depth plane separate from the pinned frame around it. *Reads as:* quiet dimensionality — the work passes *behind* the chrome. *Transfers to:* a single hero/strip layer you want to feel layered, not pasted-on. *Safety:* **production-safe only if the range is small** (tens of px); North Kingdom keeps it to one layer, so it stays calm — unlike teak's 14-layer storm. → `parallax-drift`.
- **Sticky footer reveal** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress (`footer "A member of the NoA family ↑ Back to north"` holds `position: sticky`; pairs with the fixed hero overlay above it). *Mechanism:* the footer is painted and pinned beneath the content; as the last section scrolls off, the footer is uncovered like a panel peeling away — no reflow, transform/position only. *Reads as:* a clean hand-off from the work into the studio's sign-off / back-to-top. *Transfers to:* a closing CTA or footer revealed under the last content block. *Safety:* **production-safe** (two stacked sticky layers, compositor-only). → `scroll-reveal-panel`.

> *Not corroborated as a real technique:* the lone `background-shift` (`Media_loading-overlay rgb(32,32,32)→rgb(38,38,38)`) is a **media-loading shimmer** (`@keyframes Media_load-shimmer-*`), a ~6-unit near-`from==to` delta on a placeholder — loader noise, not a scroll recolor; dropped. The 27 `mousemove` listeners register **no** `cursor-follower`/`hover` effects in the capture (`hover: []`, `cursor: []`) — likely video-scrub / play-hit-area handlers, not a custom cursor; no magnetic/cursor-follow technique claimed.

## Production-safety note
- **Split-wordmark-emblem hero (the inline word—glyph—word lockup)** — *production-safe.* Pure HTML/CSS flex; the heading is one accessible string and the emblem is an inline SVG/`img` with proper `alt`. Cost: near-zero. Watch only that the two words don't collide with the centerpiece at narrow widths — fall back to a vertical stack (mobile already does).
- **Rim-lit void stage (colored radial glow on near-black)** — *production-safe.* A CSS `radial-gradient`/`conic-gradient` background plus one focal element; no JS, no canvas required for the lighting itself. Cost: low. A11y caveat: ensure foreground text keeps ≥4.5:1 against the *darkest* part of the glow, and don't rely on the glow to convey meaning. A subtly *animated* glow should respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **3D-rendered shield as the play target / WebGL hero** — *spectacle-only.* The animated/WebGL render and the showreel itself are heavy (GPU, large payload) and invisible to crawlers/keyboards if done in canvas. Cost: bandwidth + main-thread + a11y risk. Mitigation (which this site follows): ship a **static poster** of the object with a real `<button>` play affordance in the DOM, lazy-mount any WebGL/video on interaction, and gate motion behind reduced-motion. The page must be complete with the spectacle stripped.
- **Single signature easing across all transitions** — *production-safe* and recommended. Define one `--ease`/`--dur` token and reuse it; it costs nothing and is what makes the motion feel authored.

## Extracted patterns
- split-wordmark-emblem-hero — a display wordmark split to the left/right viewport edges with the brand glyph (or a centerpiece object) seated between the words on one baseline; reuse for identity-forward agency/brand heroes that want the logo fused into the headline.
- rim-lit-void-stage — a single hero object lit by a colored radial glow against a near-black field, doubling as a poster/play gate; reuse for premium dark heroes (showreel, product, 3D) where you want drama from lighting one element instead of filling the frame.
