## What works (transferable principles)

- **Invert which typeface is "expressive": make the *small* text the characterful serif italic and the *huge* text a neutral sans.** The 250px display word "Digital" is a plain light geometric sans; the personality lives in the tiny italic Times intro paragraph and the italic "*ape*" in the wordmark. The eye reads the giant sans as architecture and the italic serif as a human voice annotating it. Mechanism: scale already supplies the hierarchy, so the serif is freed to carry *tone* instead of rank. Transfers to any editorial/agency hero where you want warmth without the display type getting fussy — let size shout in a calm voice and let a small italic whisper with an accent.

- **Let a single cinematic photograph be the entire color system; keep the UI achromatic.** There is no brand accent hue anywhere — the palette is ink `#0d0e13`, white, and a warm paper `#e0ccbb`. All chroma comes from one blue-hour photo of Venice. Because the chrome has *no* color of its own, the image reads as the subject and never competes with buttons or labels. The transferable rule: if your hero is photographic, spend zero color budget on the UI; a neutral interface makes the photo look more expensive, and swapping the photo re-themes the whole page for free.

- **Overlay display type directly on the image and use the photo's own dark zones as the "scrim."** No translucent card, no gradient overlay box — the white headline sits on the naturally dark lower-left of the twilight image. This keeps the composition uninterrupted (the picture is whole) and ties type and image into one plane. Transferable *only* with art-directed images that guarantee a dark, low-detail region behind the text; otherwise add a real gradient. The principle: legibility can come from *choosing/shooting* the image, not just from layering UI on top of it.

- **Straddle the headline across the black/photo seam so scrolling feels like the image is rising into the words.** On mobile the page opens on a solid `#0d0e13` block; "Digital / Design" sit on the black while "Experience" lands on the photograph bleeding up from below. The shared ink-vs-image boundary cuts through one type block, so the reveal reads as the picture emerging *through* the headline rather than a section change. Mechanism: aligning a content edge to a tonal edge makes two zones feel like one continuous surface. Transferable to any hero→media transition you want to feel seamless rather than stacked.

- **A two-part logotype (sans + serif italic) is a whole identity in two words.** "exo" (sans) + "*ape*" (serif italic) encodes the brand's "technical + expressive" duality in the wordmark itself, then that exact pairing recurs as the page's type system. Reusing the logo's contrast as the site's contrast makes everything feel authored by one hand. Transferable: derive your type pairing *from* the wordmark so brand and UI share the same tension.

- **Ultralight weight at massive size reads as luxury; heavy negative tracking keeps it from looking flimsy.** Display is a 300-weight sans at ~250px with ~-0.05em tracking. Thin strokes at huge scale feel refined (a fashion-house move); pulling the letters tight stops the thin forms from drifting apart into weakness. Mechanism: at display size, optical weight and letter-fit must be tuned *opposite* to body text — lighter weight, tighter tracking. Transferable to any oversized hero word, but only above ~80px where thin strokes stay legible.

## Design-system read

- **Type:** Two families doing two jobs. A **light geometric sans** (Lausanne-300/400/500) carries display + UI; **Times New Roman** (a generic/fallback alias for a licensed serif) is reserved almost exclusively for **italic** — wordmark accent and the hero intro copy. Hierarchy is extreme **scale contrast**: body 16/14px jumps to a ~144–250px display (a ~9–16× ratio in a single sans), so whitespace and size — not weight — do all the ordering. **Tracking scales with size** (`.craft.type_detail.tracking_by_size`): the 250px display sits at **-11.5px (≈-0.046em)**, the 144px display at **-5px (≈-0.035em)**, and 14px UI text at **+0.1px (≈+0.007em, essentially zero)** — heavy negative on the giants, neutral-to-faintly-positive on small text. No bold weights at all.
- **Color:** Achromatic UI + photographic color. `bg` ink `#0d0e13` (the dominant computed color), `fg` white, with a **warm paper** surface `#e0ccbb` / `#e4e0db` on lower light sections. There is **no chromatic accent token** — `accent` aliases ink; all hue is supplied by the full-bleed imagery. Mutes are just opacity drops of ink or white (`rgba(13,14,19,0.6)`, `rgba(255,255,255,0.6)`).
- **Spacing rhythm:** 8px-ish base, but the operative move is *enormous* section padding (raw shows 200px / 440px paddingBlock) framing the hero copy in air. Chrome margins are tight (~22px inline); section gaps are large (32px+). Bimodal: tight for labels, vast for sections.
- **Layout system:** Full-bleed single-image hero, no visible column grid in the hero — type is corner/edge-anchored (wordmark TL, nav TR, intro mid-left, display word bottom-left). Below the fold (per raw headings: Featured Projects, Work in motion, Play Reel, In the media) it becomes a more conventional stacked editorial layout. Breakpoints of consequence: desktop (type beside/over a landscape photo) and mobile (type stacked over a portrait crop, headline straddling the black seam).
- **Motion / easing intent:** The capture exposes one tuned param set — `.motion.params`: duration **500ms**, easing **`ease`** (bare), with **no** staggered entrance, no IO thresholds/root-margins, and **zero `@keyframes`** (`keyframeCount: 0`, `cssAnimationRules: 0`). So the *authored* motion is not CSS keyframes at all — it's a **rAF-driven inertia engine** (1746 rAF callbacks in one capture, 1 mousemove listener, 1 cursor follower, 0 scroll listeners) layered over native scroll, plus 8 `<video>` elements for the reel. No GSAP/Locomotive/Lenis global was detected (`libraries: []`) — either hand-rolled or bundled opaque. Intent: slow, weighty, filmic — a camera move, not a UI tween; the single `500ms`/`ease` token is for hover/UI only. Full behavior is mapped in **Interaction & motion** below.
- **Density:** Very low surface density in the hero — one photo, ~30 words, one giant word. Latent density (a full project portfolio, press, reel) is deferred below the fold. The hero buys attention with emptiness.
- **Hierarchy:** Dominant ordering device is **scale** (16px → 250px), reinforced by **position** (edge anchoring) and the photo's tonal map. Weight and color are deliberately *not* hierarchy tools here.
- **Restraint:** No accent color, no gradients on chrome, no shadows/elevation, no bold weights, no scrim card over the image, no decorative UI. The serif is rationed to italic only. Everything loud is the photo and the one display word; everything else is quiet.

## Interaction & motion

Honest evidence note first: the **scroll-frame sequence (s00→s10) is byte-identical** (all 11 frames share one MD5) — the capture caught the hero *holding* while page-level rAF ran, so there is **no frame-delta choreography to read here**. Everything below is grounded in the probe counts and the structural fingerprint, **not** in motion I saw between frames; where the visible beat is static I say so. Profile: **1746 rAF** callbacks, **1 mousemove** + **1 cursor follower**, **0 scroll listeners**, **1 IntersectionObserver over 21 targets**, **4 fixed-position pins**, **8 `<video>`**, **0 `<canvas>`**, **0 `@keyframes`**, **0 detected libraries** (no GSAP/Locomotive/Lenis global), **15 transition rules**, and — critically — **`respectsReducedMotion: false`** with **0 `prefers-reduced-motion` blocks**. Stack: hand-rolled (or bundle-opaque) **rAF inertia engine** · `IntersectionObserver` · HTML `<video>` · native CSS `transition` for hover. This is a spectacle/agency site whose loud layer is invisible to a static capture — so most of it is **spectacle-only** by construction.

- **Custom cursor follower** — *Trigger:* hover/mousemove (`mousemoveListeners: 1`). *Mechanism:* a DOM cursor element's `transform: translate()` is lerped toward the pointer every frame (capture shows it traveling `matrix(…,151,141)` → `matrix(…,730,479)`), part of the 1746-rAF budget. *Reads as:* a bespoke "instrument," the page asserting it's a crafted experience not a document. *Transfers to:* agency / portfolio / brand-experience heroes (fine-pointer only). *Safety:* **spectacle-only** — pure decoration, invisible to touch + keyboard + reduced-motion, and a steady rAF/main-thread cost; must no-op on coarse pointers and under `prefers-reduced-motion`. **No recipe file exists** → see `patterns_to_build`.

- **Smooth-scroll inertia (rAF-virtualized scroll)** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress, but driven off rAF (`scrollListeners: 0`, `rafCalls: 1746`), the classic Locomotive/Lenis signature even though no library global was found. *Mechanism:* native scroll position is read each frame and content `translateY` is eased toward it, so scrolling decelerates with weight rather than snapping. *Reads as:* the "filmic, heavy camera" feel the brand is after; the whole page glides. *Transfers to:* cinematic agency sites that want momentum — but it **hijacks native scroll**. *Safety:* **spectacle-only** — jank on low-end devices, a motion-sickness + a11y hazard (it overrides the user's scroll model and assistive scrolling), invisible to crawlers; here it is **not** gated for reduced motion, which is a defect. **No recipe file exists** → `patterns_to_build`.

- **Pinned chrome over a moving page** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* four elements hold via `position: fixed` — the menu/`Menu Close` button, the `Work Studio News Contact` nav, and the `Global digital design studio…` intro line — staying placed while sections travel beneath (`scroll_effects` kind = `pin (fixed)` ×4). *Reads as:* a stable HUD framing the work; the intro line staying put as the photo scrolls makes the copy feel like a fixed plate the imagery passes behind. *Transfers to:* any scrollytelling frame or persistent agency nav. *Safety:* **production-safe** — `position: fixed`/`sticky` is free, no JS, no reflow; the resting layout is correct. (Note: exoape used `fixed`; the cheaper, more contained primitive is native `sticky`.) → `sticky-pin-scrollytelling`.

- **In-view media reveal (IO-gated)** — *Trigger:* in-view (1 IntersectionObserver, **21 targets**). *Mechanism:* elements (project plates, the reel, press rows below the fold) flip a class on intersect to fade/rise once as they enter — though note `entrance` deltas in the capture came back `from==1.00 to==1.00` (dropped as noise), so the *reveal amount* is unconfirmed; the IO wiring is real, the magnitude is not. *Reads as:* the long editorial body settling in section by section beneath the spectacle hero. *Transfers to:* any tall portfolio/editorial page. *Safety:* **production-safe** (GPU-cheap opacity/transform, once-only) **iff** a resting state ships and reduced-motion is honored — which this site does **not** do. → `scroll-reveal-in-view`.

- **Headline ↔ photo scroll parallax** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress (rAF). *Mechanism:* the brand's stated signature is differential drift between the giant "Digital" word and the Venice photo as you scroll past the hero, layers translating at different rates. *Reads as:* depth — the image rising "through" the type (the same seam idea as the static `full-bleed-photo-headline` mobile straddle, made temporal). *Transfers to:* cinematic photo heroes wanting depth on scroll. *Safety:* **production-safe when the drift is small (tens of px); spectacle-only when large** — big ranges induce motion sickness and amplify jank. **Evidence caveat:** the byte-identical frames do **not** corroborate this beat — it's inferred from the rAF engine + the brand's intent, *not* observed; treat as likely-but-unconfirmed. → `parallax-drift`.

- **Looping reel video** — *Trigger:* load / in-view. *Mechanism:* 8 `<video>` elements (the "Work in motion" / Play Reel sections) autoplay-loop as ambient motion; `interactive.canvas: 0`, so the moving imagery is plain video, not WebGL. *Reads as:* "the studio's work is alive." *Transfers to:* showreel / case-study sections. *Safety:* **spectacle-leaning, productionizable** — budget it: `preload="none"` + poster, lazy-mount on in-view, `muted`/`playsinline`, and pause under reduced motion (none of which is evidenced here). No recipe (bespoke to content); reuse the principle — gated, pausable, poster-fallback video.

## Production-safety note

- **Full-bleed photographic hero with type overlaid on the image's dark zone** — **production-safe** *with discipline.* Cost: legibility depends entirely on the chosen image; a lighter or busier photo behind white text fails WCAG instantly. Safe pattern: pair the overlay with a subtle gradient scrim (or `text-shadow`) as a fallback, set a solid bg color behind the image for slow connections, and `loading`/`fetchpriority` the LCP image. Spectacle risk is low; the a11y risk is contrast, so test contrast against the *actual* art, not an average.
- **Serif-italic counterpoint (small italic Times against a giant sans)** — **production-safe** and recommended. Negligible perf cost (one extra family). Only caveat: italic serif at small sizes is lower-legibility than upright sans, so keep it to short intro/voice copy, never long-form body or UI labels; ensure the serif has a real licensed cut (don't ship literal "Times New Roman").
- **250px ultralight display with negative tracking** — **production-safe** on desktop, **needs a responsive clamp** to avoid horizontal overflow and to keep thin strokes legible when scaled down (thin + small = invisible). Use `clamp()` so the weight/tracking only apply above a size threshold; below it, step up the weight.
- **Headline straddling the black/photo tonal seam** — **production-safe**; it's pure layout (a colored block above an image, with type crossing the boundary). Just ensure the portion of text on each side keeps contrast against *that* side's tone.
- **Scroll-driven parallax between text and image / smooth-scroll inertia / custom cursor** — **spectacle-only.** Cost: jank on low-end devices, hijacks native scroll (accessibility + motion-sickness concern), steady main-thread rAF load (1746 callbacks in one capture), and invisibility to crawlers/touch/keyboard/reduced-motion users. **Defect, observed:** exoape ships **`respectsReducedMotion: false` with 0 `prefers-reduced-motion` blocks** — the inertia + cursor are *not* gated, so a reduced-motion user still gets the hijacked scroll. The right pattern is to gate behind `prefers-reduced-motion` *and* a coarse-pointer / capability check and let the page read perfectly as a normal-scroll static layout; this site does not, and that's the headline a11y failure to *avoid* copying.
- **Achromatic UI + photo-as-color** — **production-safe** and a strong default; the only risk is that semantic states (error/success) have no reserved hue, so a real product must introduce minimal semantic colors that don't break the achromatic mood.

- **Accessibility scorecard (honest, from `.craft.a11y`)** — mixed, with two real defects expected of a spectacle site:
  - **Image alt text: `img_alt_ratio: 1.0`** — *passes*; all 8 images carry alt. Good and worth copying.
  - **Focus-visible: `focus_visible: false`** at the summary level, yet `.craft.states.focus` shows every nav anchor *does* get `outline: 1px rgb(16,16,16)`. Read as: real focus outlines exist on the primary nav, but coverage is incomplete / not a consistent `:focus-visible` ring across all 30 interactive elements — verify the non-nav controls before trusting keyboard nav.
  - **Reduced motion: `reduced_motion: false`** — *fails.* No `prefers-reduced-motion` handling; the rAF inertia + custom cursor run regardless. This is the headline defect (see production-safety note) — expressive sites routinely skip this; do **not** copy it.
  - **Tap targets: 23 small targets out of 30 interactive** — *fails* the 44px guideline for most controls; the corner-anchored, small-label chrome that looks elegant on desktop is under-sized for touch. Enlarge hit areas on mobile.

## Extracted patterns

- **serif-italic-counterpoint** — pair a neutral sans (display + UI) with a serif reserved for *italic* voice copy + logotype accent; reuse for editorial/agency heroes wanting warmth without fussy display type (production-safe).
- **full-bleed-photo-headline** — a full-bleed art-directed photo with the display headline overlaid on its dark zone (and, on mobile, straddling a solid-color → image seam); reuse for cinematic agency/brand heroes (production-safe with a contrast/gradient fallback).
