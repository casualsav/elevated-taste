## What works
- **The work IS the layout — a wall-to-wall thumbnail contact sheet replaces a hero.** There is no headline-led hero, no copy-led pitch: the page opens directly into a dense modular grid of project thumbnails snapped to a row-height module (1×1, 2×1, 1×2 spans) on one tight ~12px gutter. The studio argues by *volume of evidence* rather than by claims. Transfers to any portfolio/agency where the strongest proof is breadth of output; the mechanism (image-first density, taxonomy/copy deferred) beats the standard "big claim + three featured cards."
- **Saturation is rationed to content, never to the system.** The chrome and the paper are strictly monochrome (near-black ink on warm near-white); every saturated color on screen — the reds, acid-yellows, electric blues — lives *inside* the artwork. Because the frame adds zero hue, each thumbnail's own palette pops at full strength and the page never competes with the work. Transferable rule: when content is the hero and it is already colorful, make the system achromatic so the content supplies all the color.
- **A single tight gutter does all the separating — no cards, borders, radius, or shadow.** Tiles butt together on one consistent small gap, reading as a *printer's contact sheet / lookbook*, not a grid of UI cards. Removing every container chrome element lets a very high item count stay calm and gallery-like. Transferable to any image-dense feed; the discipline is "one gutter value, zero box decoration."
- **A display-serif line is used as a caesura, not a banner.** Mid-scroll the image wall stops for a full-bleed band of whitespace carrying one centered old-style serif phrase ("Moving Missions Forward"), then resumes. The serif's job is *rhythm and voice inside density* — it lets the eye breathe and states the mission once, rather than introducing a section. Transferable: punctuate long dense feeds with a void + one line of voice instead of a heading/rule.
- **Two-register type system: engineered mono chrome vs. literary serif content.** All wayfinding (nav, footer ticker, indices, copyright) is small tracked monospace = "catalog / system metadata"; the one human, expressive moment is the serif line = "studio voice." The tension reads as *technical studio with editorial taste* and, because both faces recur in fixed roles, the page feels authored by one hand. Transferable pairing logic for any agency/editorial surface.
- **Corner-anchored chrome frees the whole canvas for work.** Identity glyph top-left, comma-joined nav cluster (`Work, Info, News`) center-right, `Contact` top-right; footer carries `IG / LI` · `Studio Freight` · `©2026 / Terms`. Hierarchy is by *position*, not size — the margins hold the interface so the center can be wall-to-wall imagery.
- **Chrome stays legible over anything via a `difference` blend, not a scrim.** The fixed corner nav and footer ticker carry no background of their own — `mix-blend-mode: difference` makes the single ink invert against whatever scrolls beneath, so it reads near-black over the warm paper and flips to light over a dark or saturated tile. That buys persistent high-contrast wayfinding on top of a full-bleed media wall *without* a plate, blur, or second color — the achromatic system extended into the z-axis. Transfers to any fixed nav/caption over unpredictable media; the cost is extra compositing (the blended layer forces its own stacking context) and it can go muddy over mid-grey, so it suits content that's either dark or bright, not mid-tone.

## Design-system read
- **Type:** Two families, two jobs. Display = jjannon-regular (Jannon/Garamond old-style serif) at ~2.6–3.5rem for the single interstitial line only; chrome = publico-text-mono at ~0.875rem, regular + one semibold, often comma-joined or slash-joined. **Tracking is gently positive on both faces, not tightened** (`tracking_by_size`: mono 14px → 0.14px ≈ +0.01em; serif 56px → +1% ≈ +0.014em) — note this *inverts* the usual "bigger = tighter" display rule: the serif holds a hair of positive tracking even at display size, which suits an old-style face and the catalog/metadata voice. Type is `fluid_type:true` (clamps), and the H1 downscales 42 → 26px on mobile. Scale range is deliberately *narrow* (~3.5× body→display) because the images, not type, carry the visual weight. (Raw's Times New Roman 16px top count is the un-fonted UA fallback, not a real role — absent from the screenshot.)
- **Color:** Achromatic system. Paper bg `#fefdfc` (warm near-white), ink `#1a1a1a`; no surface tier, no system accent. All hue is delegated to thumbnail artwork. One ink does fg + chrome; muted = same ink (no second grey tier on screen).
- **Spacing rhythm:** A single tight 12px gutter governs the entire mosaic — that uniform gap *is* the rhythm. The only large space is the interstitial whitespace band (~5.5rem / 88px block padding) that hosts the serif line. Base unit reads as 12px, not the house 8px.
- **Layout system:** Modular row-height grid; tiles span integer numbers of cells (1×1, 2×1, 1×2) so aspect variety comes from *span size*, not from column offset (this is what distinguishes it from a gutter-offset masonry). Mobile collapses to ~2 columns, same tight gutter, same span logic; chrome stacks to top bar + bottom ticker.
- **Layering / page architecture:** Not a flat scroll — a **thin fixed frame + blend-composited chrome** over one moving plane. Five `position:fixed` layers (header nav z50, footer ticker z99, the `main` mosaic wrapper, a nav drawer, a `#page-transition` veil z9999) hold the corners while the contact sheet streams through; depth is otherwise minimal — one opaque paper surface, `0` translucent blocks, `0` backdrop-filters (no glass). The achromatic frame is achromatic *by compositing, not by luck*: header and footer sit on transparent backgrounds (`architecture.layers`: 2× fixed chrome, `bg alpha:0`) with **`mix-blend-mode: difference`**, so the one ink (`#1a1a1a`) self-inverts against any tile beneath it (see What works). A **second, dormant blend system** explains the raw profile's blend spike: each media wraps a hidden white-fill overlay grid — **125 `mix-blend-mode: exclusion` cells at `visibility:hidden`** — that animates visible as an *invert/develop* transition on route/hover change. It's why the raw census counts ~130 blended elements while the stills stay full-color and pixel-identical: at rest the exclusion grid is off and the difference chrome is simply black on white. **Safety:** both blends force extra compositing/isolation layers; the develop-reveal is spectacle-only and, like the rest of the motion here, ships with no reduced-motion gate.
- **Motion / easing intent:** The signature is a **hand-rolled rAF momentum/smooth-scroll** (Studio Freight authored Lenis): the probe caught a continuous `requestAnimationFrame` loop (`raf:1993` in one pass) with `0` detected libraries / script-hits, `0 @keyframes`, only `3` CSS animation rules and `19` transition rules — i.e. the page is JS-transform-driven, not keyframe- or library-driven. Captured easing is the bare `"ease"` default with **no explicit durations exposed** (the rAF scroller drives per-frame); the token snapshot infers the house curve `cubic-bezier(0.2,0,0,1)` at fast/base (200/320ms) for the discrete hover/reveal transitions, which is safe to port. Beyond the scroll, motion is deliberately minimal: dimmed-tile hover-reveal, IntersectionObserver lazy-load, one subtle cursor element. Full choreography, evidence, and per-technique safety in **Interaction & motion** below. **The frame sequence did not capture any of it** — see that section.
- **Density:** Very high item count, yet calm — because separation is whitespace-of-one-gutter + monochrome frame, and the serif band gives one rest stop. Density never tips into "search results" because there are no card borders to multiply visual noise.
- **Hierarchy:** Dominant ordering device is **position + size of tile** (bigger span = more important work), reinforced by the achromatic frame. Type hierarchy is secondary and minimal.
- **Restraint:** No hero, no marketing copy above the fold, no accent color, no cards/shadows/radius, no second grey, no decorative dividers (the serif band replaces them), and no oversized wordmark — the identity mark is a small corner glyph. Almost everything that a conventional agency landing page front-loads has been deliberately removed.

- **Components:** Captured states are thin — the craft probe found **no explicit `:focus` or `:disabled` rule sets** (`states.focus:[]`, `states.disabled:[]`), consistent with a near-stateless content wall. *Button:* not a real role here — there are no filled CTAs; the only "actions" are text links (`Contact`, nav words), so there is no button surface/hover/active to report. *Card:* deliberately **none** — tiles are bare images on one gutter, zero container chrome (`radius:0`, `shadow:none`, `elevation.shadows:[]`), so "card" states don't exist by design. *Nav:* small mono text links, comma-joined; the captured interaction is the dimmed→full **opacity hover** (below), not a bg/border state; **no `:focus-visible` style was captured** (`focus_visible:false`) — a real defect for keyboard users. *Input:* **not captured / none on page** (no search or forms in the index). Treat button/input specs as *not captured*, not as "minimal by choice."

- **Grid / container:** Desktop runs a **12-column** track with mixed sub-spans — the craft probe reports column counts `[3, 12, 2, 7]` (the 12 is the master mosaic grid; 3/2/7 are sub-regions like the corner-chrome rows and the interstitial band). Frame estimate at 1440px: the widest mosaic rows read as **~12 tile columns** on the single 12px gutter, with tiles spanning integer cells. Mobile collapses to `[2, 6, 3]` — i.e. **~2 columns** for the mosaic, same gutter, same span logic; body stays 16px, H1 drops to 26px. Container is effectively edge-to-edge (wall-to-wall imagery), not a centered max-width column — the margins only hold corner chrome.

## Interaction & motion

The four frames (`s00 / s03 / s06 / s09`) are **pixel-identical** — same mosaic, same centered "Moving Missions Forward" serif band, same corner chrome — so **the frame capture did not record the motion.** That is itself the tell: Studio Freight authored **Lenis**, and the page is driven by a continuous **rAF momentum/smooth-scroll** that transforms a single wrapper, so per-stop stills diff to nothing. Everything below is therefore grounded in **probe counts + hover deltas + IO configs + DOM markup**, not in what the frames show. Evidence: `raf:1993` (continuous loop) · `scrollListeners:3` · `mousemoveListeners:1` · `IntersectionObservers:27` over `64` targets (all `threshold:0`, `rootMargin:0px 0px 100px 0px`) · `hoverEffects:12` · `cursorFollowers:1` · `scroll_effects: 3× pin(fixed)` · **`keyframeCount:0`, `cssAnimationRules:3`, `libraries:[]`, `scriptHits:[]`** (hand-rolled, not GSAP/Framer) · `entranceReveals:0` (no staggered load) · `interactive`: 0 carousels/tabs/disclosure/video/canvas/marquee/draggable. **`prefers-reduced-motion` is NOT respected** (`prmRules:0`, `respectsReducedMotion:false`, `a11y.reduced_motion:false`) — the headline a11y defect. Stack: **hand-rolled rAF smooth-scroll (Lenis-class)** · `IntersectionObserver` (lazy-load) · CSS transitions.

- **rAF momentum smooth-scroll** — *Trigger:* scroll (continuous rAF). *Mechanism:* native scroll is intercepted and a damped target position is interpolated each frame, applied as one `transform: translate3d(...)` on a wrapper (`raf:1993` in a single pass; `0` libraries / `0 @keyframes` → JS, not a library/keyframe). *Reads as:* the whole contact-sheet glides with weight and inertia — the "expensive, art-directed studio" feel before any element animates. *Transfers to:* portfolio / brand-experience sites where scroll *feel* is the product. *Safety:* **spectacle-only** — hijacks the platform scroll: continuous main-thread rAF (battery/jank on low-end), can fight trackpad/AT expectations, and (here) has **no reduced-motion off-ramp** so motion-sensitive users get the full inertia. If used: gate behind `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`, keep keyboard/anchor jumps instant, and never block native scroll fallback. → *no corpus recipe* (see `patterns_to_build`).
- **Dimmed-tile hover-reveal** — *Trigger:* hover. *Mechanism:* thumbnails rest **partially or fully transparent** and resolve to full strength on pointer-over — the probe caught 12 `a.a-div` opacity transitions `0 → 1` (and `0.066 → 1`, `0.266 → 1`), i.e. a few tiles sit dimmed and most sit near-invisible until hovered. *Reads as:* a contact sheet that "develops" under the cursor — you light up the work you point at; reinforces *image-is-everything, chrome-is-nothing*. *Transfers to:* any image-dense index/gallery where you want focus to follow the pointer without borders or captions. *Safety:* **production-safe** (opacity-only, GPU-cheap) **with a hard caveat**: tiles that start at `opacity:0` are **invisible and unreachable on touch / keyboard** — ship a visible resting state (e.g. `0.6→1`, never `0→1`) and don't hide content behind hover on no-hover devices. → `scroll-reveal-in-view` is the closest gated-opacity recipe; the pure hover variant has no dedicated file.
- **Pinned interstitial + corner chrome** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* three `position:fixed` pins — `header` (top corner nav), the `main` carrying "Moving Missions Forward", and the `footer` ticker — so the chrome and the one serif line **hold position while the mosaic scrolls past** (`scroll_effects: 3× pin(fixed)`). *Reads as:* a stable editorial frame / stage on which the work streams by; the mission line reads as a persistent caption rather than a section you scroll into. *Transfers to:* persistent corner nav + a single fixed "voice" line over a long media feed. *Safety:* **production-safe** (native `position:fixed`, no per-frame JS) — but verify the fixed serif line doesn't overlap tiles at short viewports, and keep the fixed nav out of the way of focus order. → `sticky-pin-scrollytelling`.
- **IntersectionObserver lazy-load reveal** — *Trigger:* in-view (pre-emptive). *Mechanism:* 27 IOs over 64 targets, every one `threshold:0` with `rootMargin:0px 0px 100px 0px`, so each tile is decoded/faded in ~100px **before** it enters the viewport; `entranceReveals:0` confirms there is **no** staggered entrance choreography — it's purely load management for a heavy image grid. *Reads as:* nothing, ideally — tiles are already present as you arrive (the point of the +100px margin). *Transfers to:* any long image-first feed needing CLS-safe lazy loading. *Safety:* **production-safe** (the cheapest, most correct use of IO; pair with `aspect-ratio` placeholders). → `scroll-reveal-in-view`.
- **Subtle cursor element** — *Trigger:* mousemove (1 listener). *Mechanism:* a single `cursor-follower` node tracks the pointer with a small scale (`matrix(1.02,0,0,1.02,0,0)`), resetting to `none` off-target. *Reads as:* a faint bespoke-cursor touch of craft on fine-pointer devices. *Transfers to:* brand-experience garnish only. *Safety:* **spectacle-only (mild)** — pointer-only (inert on touch), adds `mousemove` work, first thing to drop under a perf/reduced-motion budget; here it ships without a reduced-motion gate. → *no corpus recipe* (`magnetic-cta` is a different, magnetism effect; a plain cursor-follow recipe doesn't exist — see `patterns_to_build`).

## Production-safety note
- **Tight modular thumbnail mosaic — production-safe.** Pure CSS grid with `grid-auto-rows` + integer `grid-row/column` spans; cheap, responsive, accessible. Cost: needs curated aspect ratios per item or art-directed crops, and DOM order should equal reading order (use an explicit grid, not column-flow, so it does). Image-weight is the real cost — lazy-load + `aspect-ratio` placeholders to avoid CLS.
- **Achromatic system + content-only color — production-safe.** Zero cost, strong a11y (high text contrast guaranteed by monochrome ink). The only caveat: on-image text/labels (if added) need their own contrast handling since the system gives no chip color.
- **Serif interstitial divider — production-safe.** A whitespace band + one line of type; trivially cheap, improves scannability of long feeds. Cost: the chosen display serif is a web-font request; subset it. No motion required.
- **Monospace corner-chrome — production-safe.** Pure text at edges; recolors tone for free, fully responsive (each corner collapses independently). Cost: cryptic comma/slash-joined labels (`IG / LI`, `Work, Info, News`) can hurt first-time wayfinding — keep destinations guessable and ensure real link semantics + focus states.
- **Dimmed-tile hover-reveal — production-safe with a caveat.** The opacity hover IS present in the capture (12 `0→1` transitions); it's GPU-cheap, but tiles resting at `opacity:0` are invisible/unreachable on touch + keyboard — ship a visible resting state (`~0.6→1`) and never gate content visibility on hover.
- **IntersectionObserver lazy-load — production-safe.** Present and correctly configured (`threshold:0`, `rootMargin:+100px`); the right way to load a heavy image grid. Pair with `aspect-ratio` placeholders to hold CLS.
- **rAF smooth-scroll + cursor-follower — spectacle-only.** The momentum scroll is the page's signature but hijacks the platform scroll (continuous main-thread rAF, fights AT/trackpad) and the cursor-follower is pointer-only flourish; both ship here **without** a reduced-motion gate.
- **A11y defects (be honest — expressive site, fails the motion bar):** `prefers-reduced-motion` is **NOT respected** (`reduced_motion:false`, `prmRules:0`) — motion-sensitive users get the full inertia + cursor follow; **no `:focus-visible` styling captured** (`focus_visible:false`) — keyboard wayfinding through the cryptic mono links is poor; **7 of 41 interactive targets are below tap-size** (`small_tap_targets:7`) — the small corner mono links are hard to hit on touch. On the credit side, **image alt coverage is 100%** (`img_alt_ratio:1` over 26 images) and the achromatic ink guarantees text contrast. Net: a11y is good on contrast/alt, **failing on motion + focus + tap-size** — the typical expressive-site profile.
- No cookie wall, preloader, or WebGL gate observed — this is a genuine content-first capture.

## Extracted patterns
- tight-thumbnail-mosaic — a borderless, single-tight-gutter modular grid where thumbnails span integer row/column cells; reuse for image-first portfolio/agency indexes that argue by volume of work.
- serif-interstitial-divider — a full-bleed whitespace band carrying one centered display-serif line, used to punctuate a dense feed with rhythm + voice instead of a heading or rule.
- scroll-reveal-in-view *(motion, reused)* — IntersectionObserver-gated opacity reveal; here it's the closest existing recipe for both the lazy-load fade-in and the gated-opacity discipline the hover-reveal needs (ship a visible resting state).
- sticky-pin-scrollytelling *(motion, reused)* — native `position:fixed/sticky` pin; here it's the corner-chrome + fixed "Moving Missions Forward" voice line holding position while the mosaic scrolls past.

### patterns_to_build (genuinely-new techniques, not yet corpus files — do NOT cite as existing patterns)
- **rAF-momentum-smooth-scroll** — a Lenis-class hand-rolled inertial scroll (damped target position interpolated per `requestAnimationFrame`, applied as one wrapper `translate3d`). Spectacle-only; the recipe must ship a hard `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference` gate, keep keyboard/anchor jumps instant, and never break native-scroll fallback. No corpus file exists (`scroll-scrub` is the nearest keyword but has no recipe and is a different effect).
- **cursor-follow** *(plain)* — a single decorative element lagging the pointer with a small scale, fine-pointer-gated. Spectacle-only; distinct from `magnetic-cta` (which pulls a target toward the cursor). No corpus recipe exists for the plain follower.
