## What works (transferable principles)

- **Hierarchy from scale + color, not weight.** The entire page uses only two font weights (300 light, 400 regular) of one family (Söhne). A headline reads as a headline because it is ~3-3.5× body size, not because it is bold. This keeps text optically calm at high information density and removes the "shouty" feel of heavy display weights — directly transferable to any content-dense product/marketing page that needs gravitas without aggression.
- **Two-tone running text encodes a sentence's structure.** Both headlines and body paragraphs are split into an ink-colored lead clause and a muted-slate continuation ("**Flexible solutions for every business model.** *Grow your business with…*"). The color shift does the work of a visual hierarchy *inside* a single type size, letting you front-load the claim and demote the explanation without a size change or a line break. Reusable anywhere you have claim-then-elaboration copy.
- **A single saturated accent against a slate-not-black neutral ramp.** One violet (`#533afd`) carries every link, primary button, icon, and inline emphasis; everything else is a desaturated blue-slate ramp (ink `#061b31` → slate `#50617a` → `#64748d`). Because the neutrals lean blue rather than pure gray, the violet reads as "more of the same family" instead of a foreign highlight — the page feels cohesive while still having exactly one call-to-action color. Transferable rule: pick neutrals that share the accent's hue family.
- **Bento card grid with full-bleed media and a fixed text plinth.** Feature/case-study cards are equal-radius tiles whose top region is filled by product imagery or gradient and whose bottom is a short text block ending in a colored "Read X story ›" link. Mixed column spans (a 4-up row, then asymmetric 2-up promos) create rhythm without leaving the grid. This is the workhorse layout for "many parallel offerings" and survives any framework.
- **Diagonal gradient as a brand signature, contained and non-blocking.** The hero's orange→pink→violet→blue ribbon sweeps diagonally behind/beside the text but text sits on plain white — the gradient never sits under body copy, so contrast is never at risk. The same gradient idiom returns as numerals on the dark stats band. Transferable: use an expressive gradient as a recurring motif but quarantine it from text-bearing surfaces.
- **Whitespace as the primary separator between sections.** Section padding is large (≈64-96px block) and there are almost no dividers, boxes, or background color changes between content groups except the two intentional dark bands. Generous vertical rhythm lets a very long page (14k+ px) stay scannable. Transferable density lesson: buy legibility with space, not with borders.
- **Restraint on chrome — near-zero shadow, small radii.** Card radius is ~4-6px and box-shadow appears on only a handful of floating elements (the demo card, dropdowns). Flatness keeps focus on content and gradient; shadow is reserved to signal "this thing floats above the page," giving it real meaning when it does appear.

## Design-system read

- **Type:** One superfamily (Söhne / `sohne-var`, SF Pro Display fallback). Effective scale ≈ 14 / 16 / 20-22 / 26-32 / 48-56px — roughly a 1.25-1.5 step, ~3.5× body-to-hero range. Only weights 300 + 400. Negative tracking scales *with* size but stays subtle — measured per-size: ~**-0.01em** through the 20-26px range (20px→-0.2px, 26px→-0.26px), tightening to ~**-0.02em** at 32-48px (32px→-0.64px, 48px→-0.96px) and ~**-0.025em** at the 56px display (-1.4px). So it's a gentle size-proportional tightening, not a fixed em and not the aggressive -0.04/-0.05em of more expressive sites. Hierarchy = size + color, never weight.
- **Color:** Accent violet `#533afd` (links/CTA/emphasis), legacy link blue `#0000ee` on a few native links. Ink `#061b31`; muted slate ramp `#50617a` / `#64748d` / `#7d8ba4`; lavender support `#7f7dfc` / `#7389ff`. Surfaces: white `#ffffff`, faint `#f8fafd`, pale blue `#e5edf5`. Dark sections ≈ `#0c1128` navy. Brand orange `#ff6118` only in gradients/illustration. **Recurring brand-gradient motif (static):** an orange→pink→violet→blue diagonal color field recurs as a *distinct, per-section static* treatment — a fanned ribbon beside the hero headline (s00), card swatch tiles (s05), a full-bleed top band on a later section (s06), and a soft violet/orange aurora on the navy band (s07). These are four separate static color fields, not one gradient repositioned by scroll (motion unproven — the capture has no scroll/background effect). Always kept off text-bearing surfaces. Semantic colors are not exposed on the marketing page.
- **Spacing rhythm:** Clean 4/8px base. Common gaps 8 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 64 / 80px; section block padding 64 / 96px. Button padding ~14.5px block / 16-24px inline. The big jumps (32→64→96) create the airy section separation.
- **Layout system:** Centered ~1080px max content column. 12-col feel realized as 4-up card rows that recombine into 2-up asymmetric promo rows. Strong left-alignment for prose; centered only for the stats band and pull-quotes. Mobile collapses to a single column with the gradient ribbon shrinking to the upper-right corner and CTAs going full-width and stacked.
- **Motion / easing intent:** Captured durations cluster at **150 / 240 / 250 / 300ms** (micro / hover) and **800 / 1000 / 1200ms** (reveal / count-up / cinematic), plus a 400ms mid. Easings: hover settles on `cubic-bezier(0.25,1,0.5,1)` (easeOutQuart — quick decisive stop) and `cubic-bezier(0.3,0,0.2,1)`; long scroll/reveal choreography uses `cubic-bezier(0.165,0.84,0.44,1)` and `cubic-bezier(0.65,0,0.35,1)`; a symmetric `cubic-bezier(0.45,0.05,0.55,0.95)` and a bare `ease` also appear (the latter is a smell — see a11y note). No staggered-entrance delays were captured (group reveals fire together, not in sequence). Intent: snappy on interaction, cinematic on entrance — full choreography in **Interaction & motion** below.
- **Components:** *Primary button* (Get started / Contact sales): violet `#533afd` fill, white label, ~14.5px×16-24px padding, ~4-6px radius; **hover** darkens the fill (`#533afd`→`#4032c8`/`#4a36e4`, no lift, no shadow); **focus** 2px violet `#533afd` outline; **disabled** is transparent bg + 30%-opacity ink text (`rgba(16,16,16,0.3)`) with `cursor:default` (a *quiet, low-contrast* disabled — fails AA, flag for forms). *Link* (`hds-link`): violet, **hover** darkens to `#2e2b8c` **and drops to ~0.63 opacity** (double-encodes the press). *Nav item* (Solutions/Developers/Pricing): slate `#64748d`, **hover** resolves to ink `#061b31`; 2px violet focus outline. *Card*: equal-radius (4-6px) flat tile, near-zero shadow at rest — elevation is reserved for genuinely-floating elements (demo card, dropdowns) via `0 30px 45px -30px rgba(50,50,93,.25)`-class shadows. *Input*: no marketing-page form fields captured — values would need a product-surface capture.
- **Grid / container:** centered ~1080px max content column on a 4/8 base. Card rows run **4-up → 2-up → 3-up → 1-up** spans (captured `grid.columns: [4,2,3,1]`) that recombine within one grid for rhythm; gaps 8/16/24/32px, section block padding 64/96px. **Mobile (390px)** collapses the same rows toward single-column (reorder `[1,4,3,2]`), CTAs go full-width + stacked, nav drops 8→5 links behind a likely hamburger, and h1 steps 48px→34px.
- **Density:** High — many parallel offerings, stats, logos, case studies — kept legible by single-family type, large inter-section whitespace, and the two-tone trick that compresses claim+detail into one size.
- **Hierarchy:** Dominant ordering device is **scale**, with **color** as the secondary device (ink vs slate vs accent). Weight is deliberately not a lever.
- **Restraint:** No bold weights; no heavy shadows; tiny radii; no per-section background boxes; one accent color; gradients kept off text surfaces. The expressiveness is concentrated in two places (hero ribbon, dark stats band) and the rest is disciplined neutral.

## Interaction & motion

Stripe's motion is **scroll-driven and largely ambient** — pervasive in-view reveals plus layered parallax on the product graphics, with the "wow" concentrated in the dark stats band and its count-up numerals. Evidence from the capture: **74 IntersectionObservers over 74 targets**, **18 scroll listeners**, **2095 rAF callbacks** in one scroll pass, **6 `<canvas>`** + **2 marquees**, **78 draggable** + **17 disclosure** nodes. Notably **0 `@keyframes` / 0 CSS-animation rules and 0 libraries detected** (sheets were CORS-blocked, so the engine is JS-driven rAF rather than declarative CSS), and **`prefers-reduced-motion` is NOT respected (0 PRM rules)** — the single biggest a11y gap. Note: the captured `scroll_effects` are **entirely `transform`/`opacity`/`color` on product graphics** — there is **no scroll-driven background or gradient effect** in the data; the recurring brand gradient is a **static per-section motif** (see the Design-system read), not scroll-anchored. Stack (inferred): `IntersectionObserver` + `requestAnimationFrame` + CSS `transform`; no GSAP/Lenis/Framer global surfaced.

- **Layered parallax on product graphics** — *Trigger:* in-view. *Mechanism:* sibling layers of a product graphic translate at different rates as the block enters — captured on `billing-plan-graphic` (background vs usage-progress vs wrapper) and three stacked "Order Summary" pricing cards, each a separate `transform` track. *Reads as:* shallow depth / "the diagram is assembling itself" as it scrolls in. *Transfers to:* any feature illustration or stacked-card promo. *Safety:* **production-safe when subtle** (compositor-only transforms), trends spectacle if the drift is large; gate on in-view and cap displacement. → `parallax-drift`.

- **In-view fade-rise reveal** — *Trigger:* in-view (74 IO targets, threshold ~0–0.01, generous `rootMargin` 500-1000px to pre-arm). *Mechanism:* one class/opacity flip on intersect → `opacity` + small `translateY`, fired once. *Reads as:* a very long page that "settles in" section by section instead of popping. *Transfers to:* any tall marketing/docs page. *Safety:* **production-safe** (GPU-cheap, once-only) — but here it's **not reduced-motion-gated**, so add the PRM fallback when you reuse. → `scroll-reveal-in-view`.

- **Scroll-out scale-dim** — *Trigger:* in-view / scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* elements leaving the viewport shrink and fade rather than just clipping — a `$999.00` figure runs `opacity 1→0` **and** `scale 1→0.4` together, and a `connect-platform` connection-ring scales `1→0`. *Reads as:* the previous step "recedes" as the next arrives — a soft scene change. *Transfers to:* sequential storytelling panels, before/after swaps, stacked-card hand-offs. *Safety:* **production-safe** (transform+opacity only); ensure the end state isn't the only way critical content is reachable. → `scroll-scale` / `card-stack-scale-dim`.

- **Count-up stat figures** — *Trigger:* in-view. *Mechanism:* per-digit `span`s ("6","9","0","8" … "%") on the dark stats band tick up to their final value when the band enters (first change ~150ms in). Pairs with the brand-gradient numerals called out in the static read. *Reads as:* the proof-points feel measured and earned, not static. *Transfers to:* any metrics/social-proof band. *Safety:* **production-safe** (cheap), but expose the final number in the DOM so screen readers and reduced-motion users get the value without the animation. → `count-up-stat`.

- **Auto-scrolling logo marquee** — *Trigger:* load (continuous). *Mechanism:* `logo-carousel__marquee` ticks customer logos horizontally on a loop (2 marquees on the page). *Reads as:* an always-fresh logo wall that implies breadth without a static grid. *Transfers to:* logo walls, "trusted by", press strips. *Safety:* **production-safe** *if* it pauses on hover/focus and respects reduced-motion — **this capture does neither**, so add both on reuse. → `announcement-ticker-marquee`.

- **Hover micro-states** — *Trigger:* hover. *Mechanism:* links darken violet `#533afd→#2e2b8c` **and drop to ~0.63 opacity**; nav text slate→ink `#061b31`; primary buttons darken their violet fill — all over ~150-250ms easeOutQuart, no transform/lift. *Reads as:* crisp, restrained, "rendered with intent." *Transfers to:* all interactive chrome. *Safety:* **production-safe** (don't let opacity alone carry the affordance for low-vision users — the color shift backstops it here).

## Production-safety note

- Two-weight single-family type, slate-hued neutral ramp, single accent, two-tone running text, low-shadow flat cards, large-whitespace sectioning: all **production-safe** and cheap — they improve a11y (contrast preserved because gradient is off text) and perf (one font family, few shadows).
- The diagonal brand gradient is **static here and production-safe** (a per-section CSS color field, off text). It is *decorative only* — keep it off text-bearing surfaces. If you ever choose to *animate* it (Stripe does not, in this capture), that variant would trend **spectacle-only** (GPU/battery cost, paint churn) and must be `prefers-reduced-motion`-gated.
- Inline accent-colored emphasis words inside headlines are **production-safe** but must keep AA contrast (the violet on white is fine) and should not be the *only* signal of importance for screen-reader users — it's purely visual.
- **A11y, measured (honest, with defects):** **Image alt is exemplary** — `img_alt_ratio: 1` (all 52 images carry alt text). **Focus rings are present but inconsistent** — nav buttons and links expose a clean 2px violet (`#533afd`) outline, yet the `:focus-visible` probe came back `false`, so coverage isn't uniform across all interactive elements; audit non-nav controls before trusting it. **Two real defects to copy *carefully around*:** (1) **`prefers-reduced-motion` is not respected at all** (0 PRM rules) while the page runs continuous marquee + parallax + in-view motion — anyone reusing this motion **must** add PRM gating. (2) **Tap targets are small** — 137 of 215 interactive elements (~64%) fall under the 44px minimum, a mobile/touch hazard. The disabled-button treatment (30%-opacity ink, ~`rgba(16,16,16,0.3)`) also fails AA contrast — fine as decoration on marketing, not for real form controls.
