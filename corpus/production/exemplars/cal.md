## What works
- **The page is drawn on a ruled canvas, not stacked in a column.** A pair of 1px hairlines runs the full page height at the content edges, horizontal hairlines cross them at every section joint, and a small `+` crosshair is painted at each intersection (and at card corners). The device costs nothing — two vertical rules and a repeated glyph — but it converts a 9-section scroll into something that reads as *drafted*: every card is registered to a visible grid rather than floating in undifferentiated whitespace. Transfers to any long marketing page that wants structure without adding boxes, dividers, or background bands. See `patterns/hairline-grid-canvas.md`.
- **The "product screenshot" is live DOM, not an image.** Every feature card contains a real, styled fragment of the app — a month grid, a duration segmented control, a `Minimum notice` select, an availability toggle, a cancellation toast — built from the same 71 assets and elements as the rest of the page (0 `<video>`, 0 `<canvas>`, 0 `<iframe>`). Because it's markup, it can animate on scroll: the calendar's selected day walks from the 16th to the 24th, duration chips light up, the toggle flips. A screenshot can only *claim* the product works; a DOM replica *performs* it, at a fraction of the bytes, and it stays crisp on every DPR. Transfers to any tool whose UI is worth showing — and it is the direct antidote to the fake-dashboard-mockup cliché.
- **A grayscale page so the product's color reads as data.** The entire chrome — background, cards, ink, borders, primary CTA — is neutral gray-to-black. The only saturated pixels on the page belong to *other people's brands* (Google, Zapier, Salesforce, Zoom, Trustpilot's green, the review stars) and to the app's own event chips. Removing brand color from the frame means every hue that survives is carrying information: an integration, a rating, a booked slot. Transfers to any product page whose screenshots are already colorful; the discipline is to let the screenshot be the palette.
- **Hierarchy is pure scale in one weight.** Display 64px, section headings 48px, card headings 24px, body 16px — a 4× jump from body to display with nothing in between fighting for rank, all in a single geometric sans at 400. Weight is used only to separate a card title from its paragraph; color only to separate ink from muted. Because scale does the ordering, the page holds nine sections without a single all-caps kicker doing hierarchy work. Transfers everywhere; it's the cheapest way to make a dense page read calm.
- **The eyebrow is a pill, and it is the section's only label.** Each chapter opens with a tiny icon+text capsule (`Benefits`, `FAQ`, `App store`) on a white pill, then the headline, then one muted sentence, then two pill CTAs — dark primary, white secondary. Repeated verbatim nine times. A section header that never varies is a promise to the reader that the page's shape is knowable; the variety lives inside the cards, where it earns something.
- **Borders are shadows.** Cards carry no `border` property. Elevation is a three-part box-shadow: a hairline ring (`0 0 0 1px rgba(34,42,53,0.08)`), a 4/8px ambient shadow at 5% and a −4px-spread contact line. The ring behaves as a border but doesn't affect layout, doesn't compound at corners, and scales with the elevation rather than fighting it. Transfers to any card system — one shadow token buys the outline and the lift together.
- **Trust is placed at the fold, not the footer.** Trustpilot / Product Hunt / G2 star rows sit *inside* the hero card, directly under the calendar widget, and the customer-logo strip is the first thing below it. The hero's job is not to be big; it is to be believed.

## Design-system read
- **Type:** Cal Sans (a geometric grotesque, single 400 weight) for display and headings; a neutral UI sans (Inter/Matter) for body and controls. Sizes: 64px/70.4 (`1.1`) h1 → 48px/52.8 (`1.1`) h2 → 24px/31.2 (`1.3`) h3 → 16px/24 (`1.5`) body → 14px/21 small. Ratio ≈ 1.33–1.5 through the UI tiers, then a hard 4× leap to display. Tracking is `-0.2px` (≈ −0.0125em) at 14–18px and `normal` at 64px — the *opposite* of the usual ramp, because the display face already has tight sidebearings. No fluid type (`clamp()` absent); the display steps 64 → 40px at mobile while body stays 16px.
- **Color:** One neutral ramp, no brand hue. Canvas `#f4f4f4`, card `#ffffff`, ink `#111111`, secondary ink `#242424`, muted `#898989`, hairline `#e1e2e3`, strong border `#d3d3d3`. The primary CTA is a near-black gradient (`#292929 → #242424`) with a `rgba(255,255,255,0.15) 0 2px 0 inset` top bevel — the one place the page uses light to imply a physical surface. Semantic color exists only inside the embedded app UI (lavender event chips, a dark "today" cell). Roles are pure luminance: ink ranks text, muted demotes it, hairline separates.
- **Spacing rhythm:** Base 4px. Component padding 4/8/12/16/24; the workhorse gap is 10px (367 uses) inside controls and 24/32/48px between blocks; section padding reaches 80–96px. Cards are 24px-padded and the page column is 1224px with a 640px measure for headline+subhead — the sub never runs wider than ~65 characters even in a 1224px section.
- **Layout system:** Flex-dominant (1242 flex containers vs 5 grids) — the grids are the ones that must be true grids: 4-up feature tiles, 3-up steps, the 2-up benefit cards. Breakpoints at 640 / 810 / 1024 / 1200. Desktop 4/2/3-column groups collapse to 2/1/4 on mobile (the 4-up icon tiles *gain* columns because they shrink to icon+label). Hero is a 2-up split: copy + booking widget.
- **Layering / page architecture:** Effectively a **single flat scroll**. `layers_summary` reports `fixed: 2, sticky: 0, has_static_backdrop: false, has_blend_overlay: false`; zero `mix-blend-mode`, zero `backdrop-filter`. The only fixed element that matters is the nav. Depth is manufactured with three cheap tricks instead of a z-stack: the shadow ring, a `radial-gradient(50% 50%, transparent 87%, #fff 100%)` vignette that dissolves the orbit rings into the card edge, and small transform offsets on floating chips (`cal.com/peer`) that let them break a card's bounding box.
- **Motion / easing intent:** No declared durations or custom curves in CSS (`durations_ms: []`, easings: `ease`); one `@keyframes` (`pulse`) in the entire sheet. All motion is JS-driven (Framer, 117 IntersectionObservers, 1010 rAF calls) and scroll-parameterized rather than time-parameterized — nothing plays on a clock, everything plays as a function of where you are. The intent is *demonstration*: motion exists to run the product's own flows.
- **Density:** Low on the page, high inside the cards. 1 h1, 9 h2, 20 h3, 109 inline SVGs, 72 images. Each card is one claim plus one working control.
- **Hierarchy:** Scale first, position second (eyebrow → headline → sub → CTA, always), luminance third. Weight and color rank nothing above the card level.
- **Restraint:** No brand accent color. No gradient mesh, no glass, no dark mode (`supportsDark: false`), no hero video, no canvas, no fake dashboard PNG, no autoplaying carousel, no cursor follower, no full-bleed color band anywhere on a 8,600px page.

## Interaction & motion
Evidence: 11 frames, 39 scroll effects (22 background-shift, 8 transform/parallax, 4 rotate, 3 opacity, 1 scale, 1 pin), 117 IntersectionObservers, 4 scroll + 6 mousemove listeners, 1010 rAF calls, 1 `@keyframes` (`pulse`), library: Framer. `prmRules: 0`.

- **Nav pill that materializes on scroll** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* a fixed 80%-width bar (z 9) sits transparent over the hero, then transitions `background: rgba(255,255,255,0) → #fff` plus its ring-shadow and full pill radius as you leave the fold; frames s00 → s03 show the same links first floating on the canvas, then riding a detached white capsule inset from the page edges. *Reads as:* the nav becomes an object only once it has something to sit on top of. *Transfers to:* any page whose hero already supplies contrast. *Safety:* **production-safe** — one compositor-friendly background/box-shadow swap on one element; do it with an IntersectionObserver sentinel at the fold, not a scroll handler.
- **Scroll-progress orbit with counter-rotated icons** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* two concentric rings in the "Connect your calendar" card rotate as a function of the section's scroll progress (probe: `+105.5°` / `−105.5°` on one pair, `−117.8°` on the other, all `trigger: scroll-progress`); each mounted app icon is rotated by the *negative* of its ring's angle so it stays upright while it orbits. A radial-gradient vignette fades the rings into the card's edge so they never collide with the border. *Reads as:* "everything revolves around your calendar," stated by geometry instead of a headline. *Transfers to:* integration/ecosystem sections, in place of a static logo cloud. *Safety:* **production-safe** (transform-only, two elements per icon) — see `patterns/scroll-progress-orbit-counterrotate.md`, which builds it on `animation-timeline: view()` with a JS fallback and a reduced-motion opt-out.
- **The embedded app UI runs its own flow as you scroll it into view** — *Trigger:* in-view. *Mechanism:* background-color transitions on individual calendar day cells (`16` goes dark→light, `24` light→dark: the selection *moves*), on duration chips (`15m` → `30m` → `45m` gain and lose the white "selected" fill), and opacity steps on availability rows. Twenty-two of the 39 recorded effects are these background shifts. *Reads as:* a product demo that costs zero video bytes and can't fall out of sync with the real UI. *Transfers to:* any feature card showing a control — animate the control's own state, don't animate the card. *Safety:* **production-safe**, with one caveat: `background-color` is a paint, not a composite. At this scale (a dozen 40px cells) it's free; a hundred cells would not be. Prefer transitioning `opacity` on a pseudo-element fill if the grid is large.
- **Floating chips that break the card frame** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress / in-view. *Mechanism:* small labels (`cal.com/peer`, `MS Teams`, `Europe/Amsterdam`) get a `translateY` offset driven off scroll position, overhanging the card's rounded edge. *Reads as:* parallax depth on a page with no z-stack at all. *Transfers to:* any card that would otherwise be a flat rectangle. *Safety:* **production-safe** (transform only); keep the offsets under ~24px or the chips read as broken layout on short viewports.
- **Center-focus testimonial row** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* three quote cards; the centered one is opaque white with full-ink text, its neighbours are washed toward the canvas (reduced opacity, no blur, no scale). Frame s05 shows the two flanks at roughly half contrast. *Reads as:* one testimonial at a time, with peripheral vision confirming there are more. *Safety:* **production-safe**; opacity only. Note the flanking cards are still in the tab order and still read by AT — dim ≠ hidden, which is correct here but must be deliberate.
- **Hover is a fade, never a lift.** Nav links and social handles drop to `opacity: 0.8` or `color: rgba(17,17,17,0.5)`; nothing translates, scales, or shadows on hover. The interactive vocabulary is deliberately quieter than the scroll vocabulary.

**Reduced-motion: NOT respected.** `prmRules: 0`, `respectsReducedMotion: false` — the orbit, the parallax chips and the in-view state machines all run regardless of preference. Any reuse must gate them; the patterns below do.

## Production-safety note
Every technique here is production-safe *in kind* — transform, opacity, and background swaps on a handful of elements. Three defects to fix on reuse: (1) no `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback anywhere, despite four scroll-driven rotations; (2) the effects are hung off Framer's rAF loop (1010 calls) rather than CSS scroll-driven animation, which is more main-thread work than the result needs; (3) **94 of 105 interactive elements fall under the 44px tap-target minimum** — the footer's six link columns and the in-card app controls are the offenders, while the pill CTAs are exemplary. Focus rings are present (`1px solid #101010`) and alt coverage is 100% across 71 images.

## Extracted patterns
- `hairline-grid-canvas` — full-height gutter rules with crosshair joints and white cards on a gray canvas; reuse to give a long marketing page visible structure without dividers, bands, or borders.
- `scroll-progress-orbit-counterrotate` — concentric rings whose rotation tracks scroll progress while their mounted icons counter-rotate to stay upright; reuse for integration/ecosystem sections, built on `animation-timeline: view()` with a reduced-motion opt-out.
