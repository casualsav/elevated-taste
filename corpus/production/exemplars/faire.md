## What works
- **Two typefaces split by *job*, not by size.** A serif (Nantes) speaks; a sans (Graphik) works. Every editorial voice — the hero promise, each section title ("Featured brands", "Shop by your values", "Explore categories") — is serif. Every functional word — nav, search, filter chips, brand names, ratings, prices, buttons — is sans, at 14–16px. You can tell a claim from a control by the letterforms alone, before reading either. This is stronger than the usual weight-based hierarchy because it survives at identical size: a 16px serif line still reads as *editorial* next to a 16px sans one. Transfers to any surface where merchandising copy and operational UI share a page — ecommerce, docs-plus-console, dashboards with narrative headers.
- **The photograph is the card; there is no card.** 165 images and exactly **three box-shadows on the entire page**. Product and brand tiles carry no border, no elevation, no radius beyond a 4px softening of the image corner. Metadata sits *below* the image on the page ground, ranked purely by type (14px ink title → 14px grey vendor → 12px rating → underlined link). Card chrome exists to compensate for weak imagery; when the imagery is the product, chrome only adds visual noise per unit of catalog. Transfers to any grid whose cells are photographs.
- **Brand color is a separate token tier from UI color.** The system exposes `expressive-surface-*` (olive `#595604`, chartreuse `#f1f29f`, peach `#ffd0b2`, plum, earth, sage) *beside* — never mixed into — `action-*`, `surface-*`, `text-*`. The expressive hues only ever paint full-bleed bands and their inverse text; buttons and inputs are drawn exclusively from the near-neutral action ramp (`#333` → `#000` on active). The result: a merchandiser can repaint a whole band without any risk of a peach primary button. The transferable move is namespacing the brand voice as its own tier, so "which colors may touch a control" is answered by the token name, not by review.
- **A third tier for *programs*, so features never invent hex.** `program-surface-insider`, `program-surface-faire_direct`, `program-text-top_shop`, `program-icon-faire_pay`, each with an `-inverse` sibling. Any badge, ribbon, or promotional row for a named initiative is a token lookup. Any product that ships tiers, partner programs, or labeled campaigns accumulates one-off hexes without this; with it, a new program is a token PR.
- **Full-bleed ground color is the only chapter divider.** The page has no `<hr>`, no section borders, no container shadows. It runs cream → olive → cream → chartreuse → cream → peach, and each colored band is a *marketing* band (a promise, a proof, a CTA) while every cream band is *catalog*. Ground color therefore encodes the register — sell vs. browse — not just visual rhythm. Reusable rule: pick one property to signal "you are now in a different kind of section," and let it be the ground, not a line.
- **Secondary text recedes by weight and hue, never by size.** Body and micro copy both sit at 14–16px; what changes is weight (a genuine 200/light for supporting text against 400 for primary) and color (`#757575` against `#333`). Nothing on the page is smaller than 12px. Contrast this with the usual failure — shrinking captions to 11px to signal "less important" — which costs legibility to buy hierarchy that weight would have given free.
- **One tracking value, applied everywhere.** `letter-spacing: 0.15px` on 1,439 elements and no other value anywhere. Not a tracking *ramp*, not per-size tuning: a single, nearly invisible loosening that makes the sans breathe at small sizes and is imperceptible at large ones. A system with one number for a property is a system nobody can get wrong.
- **The legibility scrim is a corner, not a wash.** White hero text over autoplaying shop footage is protected by `linear-gradient(22.18deg, rgba(0,0,0,.5) 1.86%, transparent 31.23%)` — a diagonal that darkens only the lower-left third where the words are. The photograph keeps its exposure; the copy keeps its contrast. Beats the default full-frame `rgba(0,0,0,.4)` overlay that flattens the image you paid to shoot.

## Design-system read
- **Type:** Nantes (transitional serif) for display/section titles; Graphik (neo-grotesque) for everything else. Scale is stepwise, not ratio-clean: 12 / 14 / 16 / 18 / 22 / 30 / 38 / 52 / 72px, with 52/64 the hero and 16/24 the body. Line-height is fixed in px, not unitless — 24px on 16px (1.5), 20px on 14px (1.43), 64px on 52px (1.23): tightens as size grows. Weights 400 (primary), 200 (supporting), 500 (medium/labels); no bold anywhere on the page. Tracking is a single 0.15px. No fluid type — sizes step at breakpoints.
- **Color:** Ground is warm off-white `#fbf8f6` with white `#fff` for the header/footer/search plane and `#f7f7f7` for tinted surfaces. Ink `#333` (never black), secondary `#757575`, tertiary `#6c6a6a`, hairline `#dadada` and `#3333331a`. Action is a neutral ramp — `#333` fill → `#000` on hover/active — so the primary CTA never competes with product photography. Brand color lives entirely in the `expressive` tier (olive `#595604`, chartreuse `#f1f29f`, peach `#ffd0b2`, plum `#41252a`, sage `#d9d8cd`, earth `#453200`) and the `program` tier. Semantics: focus `#275ec5`, critical `#921100`. Crucially, **no accent is used for text ranking** — grey does that.
- **Spacing rhythm:** Base 4px (`--spacing: .25rem`); dimension tokens are strict multiples (4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 40, 52, 56, 68). Section rhythm is coarser: 48px block padding, 56px row gaps, 24px page gutters. Card metadata packs at 2–4px. The contrast between 4px inside a tile and 56px between rows is what lets a very dense page breathe.
- **Layout system:** Six-column full-width product/brand rows (`gridCols: 6`) that collapse to two on mobile; 2-up split bands for the marketing interstitials; a mega-nav that is a four-column link matrix with a featured image rail. Content is full-bleed at the row level — tiles run edge to edge behind a 24px gutter — rather than centered in a max-width shell. Breakpoints are unusually granular (390/425/480/500/600/768/896/1023), because catalog rows re-count columns rather than reflow. Twenty visible nav links on desktop; seven and a hamburger on mobile.
- **Layering / page architecture:** **Single flat scroll.** `layers_summary`: `has_static_backdrop: false`, `has_blend_overlay: false`; `blend_glass` is empty on all three axes — zero `mix-blend-mode`, zero `backdrop-filter`. The only real fixed layer is the opaque white header (z 100); the rest of the z-stack is consent-widget noise at z 2147483647. Depth exists in exactly two places, both *painted*: the hero's diagonal scrim, and the closing peach band where product photos are collaged at staggered offsets. A commerce page has nothing to gain from translucent planes — every one of them costs paint over 165 images.
- **Motion / easing intent:** One transition dominates: `1.2s cubic-bezier(0.17, 0.67, 0.24, 1)` on 127 elements — a fast-out, long-settle curve used for image opacity as tiles decode. Chrome uses `0.2s cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1)`. Motion here means "content arrived," not "look at me."
- **Density:** Very high — 1,341 interactive elements, 165 images, 93 `<picture>` elements, a nav with hundreds of links. It stays legible because the page never draws a box: separation is whitespace, alignment, and ground color only.
- **Hierarchy:** Typeface first (serif = voice, sans = function), then scale, then ground color (which register am I in), then grey. Weight ranks *within* a tile; it never ranks sections.
- **Restraint:** No dark mode (the probe's `supportsDark` flag is a false positive — the "dark" sample is the light palette verbatim). No gradients except two utility ones. No glass, no blend modes, no shadows on cards, no fake dashboard, no three-feature-card grid. No accent-colored buttons. Sixty of 165 images lazy-load; the entrance-reveal count is **zero**.

## Interaction & motion
Evidence: 11 scroll frames, 35 `@keyframes` (all but `fadeInOut` and two obfuscated ones belong to toast/consent vendors), **0 detected animation libraries**, `entranceReveals: 0`, `prmRules: 0`. The 74 IntersectionObservers over 405 targets with `rootMargin: 200px` are doing **lazy-loading and impression telemetry, not reveal animation** — an important read: a huge IO count is not evidence of choreography. This page is deliberately near-static, and its one piece of real animation is in the closing headline.

- **Rotating noun in a serif headline** — *Trigger:* load (timed loop). *Mechanism:* the sentence "The perfect ___ for your store, right this way" holds a fixed-width slot in which five nouns (drinkware · planter · moisturizer · coffee · candle) crossfade and slide on a `fadeInOut` keyframe; stagger delays of 500/1000/1500/2000/2500ms inside a ~5s cycle, `opacity` + `translateY` only, the active word underlined so the swap reads as a link, not a glitch. The profile logs these as `transform/parallax` on `scroll-progress` — that is an artifact of sampling a *time* loop at scroll stops; the frames show the slot in the same place at every depth with a different word. *Reads as:* one abstract marketplace promise made concrete five times, without five headlines. *Transfers to:* any product whose value is "many things" — marketplaces, integrations, template galleries. *Safety:* **production-safe** (opacity + transform, one element). Costs: the DOM must contain all five words for screen readers, and a perpetual loop beside a CTA needs a reduced-motion opt-out this page does not ship. See `patterns/rotating-noun-headline.md`.
- **Ambient hero video under a diagonal scrim** — *Trigger:* load (autoplay, muted, looping). *Mechanism:* a single `<video>` of a retailer in her shop fills the hero; a 22° `rgba(0,0,0,.5)→transparent` gradient darkens only the copy's corner. *Reads as:* the customer, not the software. *Transfers to:* any brand whose users are photogenic and whose product is not. *Safety:* **production-safe** for paint; the real cost is bytes and battery — gate on `prefers-reduced-motion` (swap in the poster frame) and never autoplay with sound.
- **Decode-fade on catalog imagery** — *Trigger:* in-view (IntersectionObserver, `rootMargin: 200px`, `threshold: 0`). *Mechanism:* lazily-loaded tiles transition `opacity 0→1` over `1.2s cubic-bezier(0.17, 0.67, 0.24, 1)` when the image decodes. There is no `translateY`, no stagger, no per-card delay — the grid does not "reveal," it just stops being blank. *Reads as:* the page is fast and the content is real. *Transfers to:* every image grid. *Safety:* **production-safe** and cheaper than a reveal animation; the 200px root margin means the fade usually completes before the tile is on screen.
- **Surface-tint hover on tiles, ink-darken hover on controls** — *Trigger:* hover/focus. *Mechanism:* two rules for the whole page. Content chips move `rgba(247,247,247,.5) → #f1f1f1` (background only); buttons and links move `#333 → #000` on `color`/`border-color`/`background`; category links grow a bottom border. Nothing lifts, scales, or shadows. *Reads as:* affordance without theater. *Transfers to:* dense UIs where a hover-lift on 1,300 targets would make the page shimmer. *Safety:* **production-safe**; note that `background-color` hover is compositor-cheap only because nothing else animates.
- **Filter chips as the merchandising axis** — *Trigger:* tap. *Mechanism:* 40px-radius pills ("Women owned", "Not on Amazon", "Gives back") swap the section's featured brand + quote in place; the selected chip inverts to the ink fill. No motion, no accordion. *Reads as:* browsing, not filtering. *Transfers to:* any catalog whose taxonomy is a value or attitude rather than a category.

**Reduced-motion: NOT respected** (`respectsReducedMotion: false`, `prmRules: 0`). The exposure is small — a looping word swap and an autoplaying hero video — but both are exactly the cases the media query exists for. The patterns below gate them.

## Production-safety note
Everything here is **production-safe** in kind; the defects are omissions. (1) No `prefers-reduced-motion` rule anywhere: the noun rotator and hero autoplay both run regardless. (2) **381 of 1,341 interactive elements fall under the 44px tap-target minimum** — the mega-nav link matrix and footer columns, mostly; the pill chips and buttons are fine. (3) `img_alt_ratio: 0.75` — a quarter of 165 images ship without alt text, which on a catalog page means a quarter of the products are invisible to a screen reader. (4) Zero `<h1>` on the document (7 `h2`, 23 `h5`) — the heading outline is decorative, not structural. Focus is handled well: a visible 1px–2px `#101010` outline on every probed control, plus a `#275ec5` focus-ring token.

## Extracted patterns
- `photo-first-catalog-row` — a borderless, shadowless product/brand row where the photograph *is* the card and metadata is ranked by weight and grey; reuse for any grid of six or more images with attribution.
- `rotating-noun-headline` — a serif headline with one word slot that crossfades through a list of concrete nouns; reuse when a product's value proposition is "many things," with the full list in the DOM and a reduced-motion static fallback.
