## What works
- **One hue, four values, zero dividers.** The entire 15,000px page is a single olive field. Every `<section>` is `background: transparent` (7/7 transparent blocks in the architecture probe); the ground is painted once on `<body>` (`#3d521e`) and a handful of bands re-declare it one or two steps darker (`#36471c`, `#34451c`, `#2d3f13`) or lighter (`#445b23`). There is not a single `<hr>`, card border, or shadowed container doing section separation. Chunking a long page by *value steps inside one hue* — rather than by hue changes, rules, or cards — is why it reads as one continuous document instead of a stack of unrelated modules. Transfers to any long marketing page; it is strictly cheaper than a card system and never dates.
- **A serif Light at 7vw against a sans at 12px, and nothing between.** Display is GT Alpina at weight **300 only** — one face, no bold, sizes 3.5–7vw (50–101px at 1440). Labels are Atlas Grotesk 12px uppercase. The intermediate tier is deliberately thin: there is no 24px semibold heading competing for attention. Hierarchy comes from **scale × family × case**, never from weight — the display face ships exactly one weight, so it *cannot* be bolded. Constraining the display family to a single light weight is a forcing function: it makes you solve hierarchy with size and whitespace.
- **Tracking declared in the same unit as the size.** `h1 { font-size: 7vw; letter-spacing: -0.2vw }`, `h2 { 5vw / -0.17vw }`, `h4 { 3vw / -0.05vw }`. Because both scale with the viewport, the optical ratio (≈ -0.029em, -0.034em, -0.017em) holds at every width — a px tracking value would go loose on wide screens and crush on narrow ones. The general rule: **express tracking in the unit your size uses**, and let the tighter-as-larger ramp fall out of it.
- **The product screenshot is real, oversized, and cropped by the viewport edge.** Content lives in a 940px column; the app shots sit in an 1800px rail that runs past the right edge of a 1440px window. Nothing is a stylized "dashboard mockup" — it's the actual dense GIS UI (5,000 plotted points, a legend, a histogram, a live comment thread), deliberately too big to fit. The crop is the argument: *the app does not end where the page does*. Transfers to any tool whose credibility lives in its density. See `patterns/edge-bleed-product-crop.md`.
- **Capabilities are a typographic spec sheet, not feature cards.** The "Spatial superpowers" section is a 4-column grid of uppercase 14px labels (GEOCODING · DATA EXPORTS · IMAGE EXPORTS · PERMISSION CONTROL …) with two lines of body under each, separated only by `rgba(255,255,255,0.15)` hairlines. Eight capabilities, no icons, no boxes, no shadows. When claims are *enumerable and comparable*, a rules-and-labels table outperforms a card grid — cards imply each item is a story, a spec sheet implies each item is a fact.
- **The brand's subject matter is the page's only ornament.** The decorative elements are coastlines: pale continent silhouettes at 44% opacity behind the logo row, a landmass bleeding off the hero, a fixed full-viewport map-outline backdrop, and a fixed ticker printing the company's literal lat/long. No gradient mesh, no glass, no glow. Where a product has an inherent visual language (maps, waveforms, molecules, schematics), that language *is* the decoration budget — anything imported on top reads as generic.
- **Square buttons with an internal arrow, pushed apart.** `.button { display: flex; justify-content: space-between; padding: 20px; border-radius: 0 }` — the uppercase label sits left, a solid ▶ triangle sits hard right, and the gap between them stretches with the button. Sharp corners plus a filled triangle read as *instrument*, not *app chrome*, and the `space-between` construction means a wide button never centers into a lonely puddle of orange. One accent (`#dc8c46`) fills it; the only other button is a translucent white.

## Design-system read
- **Type:** Two families, opposed on every axis. **GT Alpina Standard** (transitional serif, high contrast, one registered weight: 300) for all display — `h1` 7vw/90%/-0.2vw, `h2` 5vw/100%/-0.17vw, `h4` 3vw. **Atlas Grotesk** (neutral grotesque, 400/500/700) for everything a person operates: nav 16px/500/uppercase, buttons 18px/500/uppercase, eyebrows 12px/uppercase/+0.4px, body ~18–20px/1.5. Display-to-body ratio is ≈ **5.5×** in a page whose UI tier spans only 12→20px. Hierarchy is scale + family + case; weight is not a hierarchy device at all. Caveat worth stealing *against*: the vw sizes carry no `clamp()`, so a 24px mobile override is bolted on at the 479px breakpoint — and it fakes weight 500 from a font that only ships 300.
- **Color:** A monochrome green field with exactly one warm accent. Ground `#3d521e`; bands step to `#36471c` / `#34451c` / `#2d3f13` (darker chapters) and `#445b23` (lighter); ink `#212f0c`; one tan band `#ecebe5` inverts the page. Text is `#eee` (never pure white for body) with `rgba(255,255,255,0.8)` as muted and `#fff` for the wordmark. Accent **peru `#dc8c46`** does CTA fill, hover recolor of dropdown text, and the footer ground; its hover is a *darker* sibling `#aa5e1b`. Interaction surfaces are pure alpha-white — `rgba(255,255,255,0.2)` hover fill, `0.15` hairlines — so hover states cost no new hue. A lime `#edf1a3` and a `#7b9c48` mid-green exist only inside illustrations. The map thumbnails are the page's *only* saturated color, which is exactly right: the data is the color.
- **Spacing rhythm:** 4px base, but the page rhythm is coarse and few-valued: 20px is the universal inset (button padding, gutters, nav), then 32 / 64 / 72px gaps and ~180px section padding. Three container widths do all the work — **640px** (prose measure), **940px** (content column), **1800px** (media rail). Whitespace, not chrome, is what makes eight dense sections legible.
- **Layout system:** Flex-dominant (113 flex containers vs 5 grids). The archetype is a **center-stack**: centered eyebrow → centered display heading → centered 640px subhead → one wide artifact, repeated eight times. Grid appears only where content is genuinely tabular (4-col spec sheet, 2-col map gallery, 3-col security cards, 6-col logo row). Breakpoints 991 / 767 / 479, desktop-first, no `min-width` queries; 4-col → 2-col → 1-col, and the fixed ticker is `display: none` below 767px — the right call, a marquee on a phone is pure tax.
- **Layering / page architecture:** Three fixed layers and one sticky. Above: the nav (z 100, transparent background — it floats *on* the field, never gains a bar) and a full-width ticker (z 4) that runs 128% of viewport width. Below: `.continent-pattern`, a `position: fixed; inset: 0; width: 100vw; height: 100vh; z-index: -10` map-outline image — a genuine static backdrop the page scrolls over. **Honest note:** its base state is `opacity: 0` and it never lit during capture; the coastlines you *see* in the frames are section-local `position: absolute` silhouettes (`.shape-1`, `.hero-shape-bottom`, `.logo-continent-pattern` at 0.44 opacity) parallaxing inside their own sections. So the backdrop is armed, not fired. No `mix-blend-mode`, no `backdrop-filter`, one box-shadow on the whole page (`0 2px 5px rgba(0,0,0,0.2)`). Depth is painted, not stacked.
- **Motion / easing intent:** One curve and effectively one duration: `0.2s ease` on 57 rules, with `0.075s` / `0.125s` micro-states and a single `0.3s`. Zero custom cubic-béziers. Motion here is *acknowledgement* — a hover fill, a color swap — plus a slow ambient drift of scenery. Nothing gates reading, nothing announces.
- **Density:** Low on the page, extreme inside the artifacts. Eight `h2` chapters over 15,000px, each carrying one screenshot that is itself packed. The page never crowds itself to prove the product is capable.
- **Hierarchy:** Scale first, then which tonal band you're standing in, then position. Color ranks nothing except the CTA.
- **Restraint:** No dark-mode toggle, no gradients (one 10%-black overlay on the whole site), no glassmorphism, no icon set on the feature list, no three-card grid, no fake dashboard, no second accent, no bold display weight, no rounded buttons.

## Interaction & motion
Evidence: 11 scroll frames, 20 scroll effects (11 `transform/parallax`, 3 `pin`, 5 opacity — of which 4 are `0.00→0.00` noise and dropped), 6 scroll listeners, 1 IntersectionObserver over 3 targets, 3,440 rAF calls, GSAP + ScrollTrigger + Webflow IX2, and exactly **one** `@keyframes` (`spin`, a vendor lightbox spinner — it drives nothing on this page). `prmRules: 0`.

- **Duplicated-group infinite ticker** — *Trigger:* load (time loop). *Mechanism:* a `position: fixed; top: 0; z-index: 4` strip whose inner span is driven to `translate3d(-N%, 0, 0)` by JS with `will-change: transform`; ~9 identical marquee groups are duplicated in the DOM so the wrap is seamless. Content is the office's literal coordinates alternating with "CLOUD-NATIVE GIS HAS ARRIVED". *Reads as:* an instrument readout — it signals *live system* rather than *announcement*. *Transfers to:* status strips, changelog banners, brand tickers. *Safety:* **production-safe** (transform-only, compositor) — but perpetual motion above body copy is a reading tax: it must pause on hover/focus, mark duplicate copies `aria-hidden`, and stop under reduced motion. This page does none of the three; it does correctly `display: none` the strip below 767px. Reuse via the existing `patterns/announcement-ticker-marquee.md`.
- **Section-local scenery parallax** — *Trigger:* in-view / scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* absolutely-positioned continent SVGs (`z-index: -10 … 0`, `max-width: 25–40%`, hung off section corners with negative insets) translate at a rate offset from the scroll, so the coastlines drift behind the type as each section crosses the viewport. Frames s00 → s03 show the landmass migrating from behind the hero headline to the section's right shoulder. *Reads as:* the page moving over a world rather than the world moving. *Transfers to:* any brand with an inherent visual language to use as scenery. *Safety:* **production-safe** in kind (transform + opacity only); this implementation pays for it with 6 scroll listeners and 3,440 rAF callbacks — `animation-timeline: view()` gets the same result off the main thread.
- **Scripted page-ground recolor** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* sections are transparent; the `<body>` carries `class="animated-body"`, `data-w-id`, and `style="will-change: background"`, so the *ground itself* is the animated element and section boundaries are value steps in one hue. *Reads as:* chapters in a single document, not a stack of slabs. *Transfers to:* any long-scroll page that would otherwise reach for full-bleed colored `<section>`s. *Safety:* **production-safe** — `background-color` is paint-only (no layout, no composite), and one root-level repaint per band is cheap. See `patterns/tonal-band-scroll-field.md`. Cost: `background-color` cannot be animated off the main thread; keep it to the root and never to a scrolling list.
- **Armed fixed map backdrop** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress (scripted). *Mechanism:* a 100vw × 100vh `position: fixed` coastline image at `z-index: -10`, `opacity: 0`, ready to be faded in behind a transparent page. Named here because the *layering* is the lesson even though the fade never fired during capture: a still world at negative z, with the content scrolling over it, buys depth with one element and no blend modes. *Safety:* **production-safe**.
- **Alpha-only hover states** — *Trigger:* hover. *Mechanism:* nav items fill with `rgba(255,255,255,0.2)` over `0.2s ease`; dropdown contents recolor from `#fff` to the accent `#dc8c46` (text *and* border together); the primary button darkens `#dc8c46 → #aa5e1b`. *Reads as:* the field lighting up, not new chrome appearing. *Transfers to:* dark-ground navigation anywhere — an alpha-white fill works over *any* band, so a recoloring page needs no per-band hover variants. *Safety:* **production-safe**.
- **Composited annotation layers over the product shot** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress / in-view. *Mechanism:* the map screenshot's comment pins and speech bubbles (`home-map-comment-1`, `…-bubble`, `home-map-comment-2`) are separate images translated independently of the screenshot beneath them, so collaboration UI floats slightly off the surface. *Reads as:* people working *on* the map. *Safety:* **production-safe**. *Honest:* the probe's opacity deltas for these are `0.00 → 0.00` — representation noise; only the transforms are corroborated, and the frames never caught them mid-reveal.

**Reduced-motion: NOT respected** (`prmRules: 0`, `respectsReducedMotion: false`). A perpetual ticker, scenery parallax, and a scripted ground recolor all run regardless of preference. The patterns below gate all three.

## Production-safety note
Every technique is production-safe *in kind* — transform, opacity, and one root background-color. Four defects to fix on reuse:
1. **No `prefers-reduced-motion` rule exists anywhere** in 4,000 lines of CSS. The ticker is the worst offender: perpetual horizontal motion pinned above the fold.
2. **No focus ring.** The framework's base CSS actively suppresses outlines (`.w-dropdown-toggle:focus { outline: 0 }`, same for nav/inputs) and nothing replaces them. A page with 131 interactive elements is unusable by keyboard.
3. **The primary CTA fails contrast.** White label on peru `#dc8c46` measures ≈ **2.7:1** — below AA even for large text (3:1). Darkening to the existing hover value `#aa5e1b` (≈ 4.7:1 with white) would fix it without adding a color. The `#eee`-on-`#3d521e` body text, by contrast, is ~9:1.
4. **48 of 131 interactive elements fall under the 44px tap-target minimum** (footer link columns, dropdown rows). The 20px-padded buttons are exemplary; the small print is not.
Alt coverage is 100% (70/70 images), and 61 of 70 are lazy-loaded with 20 carrying `srcset` — the asset discipline is better than the a11y discipline.

## Extracted patterns
- `tonal-band-scroll-field` — chunk a long page into chapters using lightness steps of one hue on a transparent-section field, with an optional scroll-driven ground recolor; reuse instead of full-bleed colored sections, `<hr>`s, or card borders.
- `edge-bleed-product-crop` — a real, oversized product screenshot in a wide rail that is cropped by the viewport edge while the copy stays in a narrow column; reuse when the product's density *is* the proof.
