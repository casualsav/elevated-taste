## What works
- **Hairline rules do the work cards do — and cost nothing.** The entire page ships **zero box-shadows** (`craft.elevation.shadows: []`), one degenerate gradient, and a maximum border-radius of 5px. Separation comes from 1px *dotted* rules drawn in the same warm ink as the body text (`rgb(75,74,71)`, 750 occurrences of that border color) plus the column gutters. Because the rule is the same hue as the type, it reads as typography rather than as chrome, and it never introduces a second "surface" plane the reader has to interpret. Transfers to any dense information page — a card is a rule plus a shadow plus a radius plus a fill; three of those four are usually unearned.
- **One family carries headline, body, and navigation; the sans is quarantined to metadata.** A single serif (a Feature-family flat-serif) accounts for ~1,540 of the ~1,700 typed elements. Helvetica appears only on timestamps, section kickers, buttons, and the standing-head labels. That split is not decorative — it *encodes* a distinction: serif = things a journalist wrote, sans = things the system knows (time, category, state). Any product with editorial content and system chrome can borrow the rule verbatim, and it survives a font swap because it's a role assignment, not a typeface choice.
- **Hierarchy is a variable-weight ramp, never color.** Observed weights are 180 / 300 / 370 / 395 / 400 / 495 — micro-steps off a variable axis, not a 400/700 binary. A 31px headline at 395 outranks a 16px lede at 400 through *size plus a half-step of weight*, so the page can nest five levels of importance inside one hue. Colored text is reserved for signals (see below), which means it stays available as a signal. Transfers anywhere you have more hierarchy levels than a two-weight family can express.
- **Uniform 1.2 leading typesets the whole page as one block.** 16px body sits at 19.2px, the 31px headline at 37.2px, the 42px display at 50.4px — the same ratio top to bottom, and 820 of ~1,100 measured elements share it. The single exception is the feature deck at 1.5, and that exception is exactly what marks "this is the paragraph to read." When every block breathes identically, a *deviation* becomes a hierarchy device for free.
- **Accent economy: three colors, three jobs, no decoration.** Red (`#af1f1b`) means liveness — the "Updated 11:42pm UTC" dot, breaking flags. Teal (`#3a6d78`) means "this is a link or a kicker," used 11 times on the page. Yellow (`#ffff00`) appears on exactly one element, the newsletter email field. Nothing is tinted for mood. On a page carrying 64 headlines, that discipline is what makes a single red dot legible from across the viewport.
- **The masthead is a working instrument, not a logo lockup.** Six bureau dials (D.C. / BXL / LAGOS / RIYADH / BEIJING / SG) flank the wordmark, hands sweeping continuously. It is the only motion the site authors, and it argues the brand's premise ("global, live") more cheaply than any tagline. The generalizable move: when your product's claim is a *property* (live, global, distributed, always-on), express it as a small always-running instrument in the chrome rather than as a sentence in a hero.
- **Line art is composited onto the paper, not pasted on top of it.** The globe/world-map illustrations use `mix-blend-mode: multiply`, so the cream ground shows through the drawing instead of the drawing carrying a white box. Two elements, whole-page effect. Transfers to any tinted or textured ground where you'd otherwise be exporting per-background PNGs.
- **Layout stability is treated as a design constraint.** 66 elements declare `aspect-ratio`, 37 images ship `srcset`, 38 are lazy, and the leaderboard slot reserves its height even when the ad never fills (the ~270px cream void at the top of every frame). Nothing on this page can jump. On an ad-funded editorial product that is the difference between a broadsheet and a slot machine.

## Design-system read
- **Type:** One flat serif family + Helvetica for meta. Scale (px): 9 · 10 · 12 · 13 · 15 · **16** · 20 · 24 · 31 · 42 — a ~1.25–1.3 ratio through the middle with a 2.6× jump from body to display. No fluid type (`fluid_type: false`); the scale is stepped, not clamped. Weights ride a variable axis (180 → 495). Tracking is essentially zero (one entry: 0.32px on 32px, i.e. 0.01em). Hierarchy = **size × micro-weight**, with case and family assigning *role* rather than rank.
- **Color:** A cream **paper** ground (`#f8f5d7`, also the `theme-color`) with two lighter tints for insets (`#fcfae4`) and one darker (`#ece9d2`); ink is a warm near-black (`#1f1d1a`), body copy a warm grey (`#4b4a47`) that is *also* the rule color; rules soften to `#c3c1ae` / `#9c9a92` where a divider must recede. Semantics: red `#af1f1b` = live/breaking, teal `#3a6d78` = link/kicker, navy `#1f2b6d` and rust `#bc4d2c` are section marks, yellow `#ffff00` is a single input highlight. Inverted blocks (nav drawer, primary button) flip to ink ground with paper text. No dark mode (`supportsDark: false`) — and none is invented here.
- **Spacing rhythm:** Base is **5px**, not 8 — the padding and gap ladders read 4/5 · 10 · 12 · 18/20 · 30 · 50. That five-unit rhythm is a print inheritance, and it's why the page can be tight without feeling cramped: the smallest legible step is smaller than an 8px system allows. Whitespace is spent almost entirely on *column gutters* and the space above section rules; inside a column, blocks stack close.
- **Layout system:** A 1248px shell over a 12-column grid, split 3 / 5 / 3-ish — standing-head rail (~322px, "The World at a Glance"), feature well (~540px), news rail (~290px). Article measure caps at 878px, forms at 486–540px. 184 flex containers to 21 grids: the grid sets the page skeleton, flex sets everything inside it. One breakpoint, **768px**, below which all three columns become one and 38 nav links collapse to 5 plus a hamburger. Density is high (64 `h2`s, 237 interactive elements) and stays legible because each column is capped near 40–55ch.
- **Layering / page architecture:** **Single flat scroll.** `layers_summary` reports 8 fixed elements, **0 sticky**, `has_static_backdrop: false`, `has_blend_overlay: false`, no `backdrop-filter`. Every fixed layer is a modal or drawer — the newsletter gate (z 900/901: a transparent positioner plus a paper panel) and the nav overlay (z 3/4: a `rgba(0,0,0,0.5)` scrim plus a 25vw paper drawer). Notably the masthead does **not** pin; the chrome scrolls away and the reader gets the whole viewport. Depth exists only where a dialog is open. The only blend in the document is `mix-blend-mode: multiply` on two illustrations.
- **Motion / easing intent:** Two durations and two curves for the entire site: **175ms** and **300ms**, `ease-in-out` and `ease` — plus one **60,000ms** loop (the clock hands). There are no entrance reveals (`entranceReveals: 0`), no hover transforms (`hoverEffects: 0`), no parallax, no cursor followers, no carousels, no canvas. Motion here means "a link changed color in 175ms." The intent is that nothing on a news page should move except the news.
- **Density:** Very high and deliberately so — three simultaneous columns, a numbered 5-item briefing rail, 64 headlines, 38 nav links above the fold. It stays readable because measure is capped, leading is uniform, and *nothing is boxed*: the eye follows column edges, not container edges.
- **Hierarchy:** Position first (which column am I in), then scale, then micro-weight. Color ranks nothing.
- **Restraint:** No shadows. No dark mode. No sticky header. No gradients. No radius above 5px (dials excepted, at 50%). No hero image treatment. No scroll animation. No hover choreography. No second display face. The page's one indulgence is six 20px clocks.

## Interaction & motion
Evidence: 11 scroll frames that are **pixel-identical except the six clock hands** (the newsletter overlay scroll-locks the document on first load, which is itself the finding — the full-page `desktop.png` is mostly the locked overlay). `keyframeCount: 10`, of which 8 are vendor hashes (Emotion `go…`, Termly `rotate`/`dash`); the site's own is one 60s rotation. `entranceReveals: 0`, `hoverEffects: 0`, `libraries: []`, `rafCalls: 1`.

- **Phase-offset perpetual rotation** — *Trigger:* load (infinite loop). *Mechanism:* N identical elements share one `@keyframes` rotation at `60000ms`, and each is seeded to a different point in the cycle with a **negative `animation-delay`** (`-38000ms` observed) rather than N bespoke keyframes or any JS. The probe's lone `scroll-progress rotate 49.2°` on `_clockHand_` is this loop sampled at two capture times, not a scroll effect — the frames prove the page never scrolled. *Reads as:* a live instrument; six dials that are visibly *not* synchronized read as six *places*. *Transfers to:* status dials, spinners in a row, staggered ambient loops, any "N of the same thing, out of phase." *Safety:* **production-safe** — `transform` on six 20px elements, no layout, no JS. The cost is that a perpetual loop beside body copy is a reading tax for motion-sensitive readers and this implementation does not gate it. See `patterns/phase-offset-instrument-row.md`, which freezes each hand at a *meaningful* static angle under reduced motion instead of stopping it at a random one.
- **Fixed newsletter gate over a scroll-locked document** — *Trigger:* load (delayed). *Mechanism:* a full-viewport fixed positioner (z 900, `pointer-events: auto`, transparent) holds a paper panel (z 901, 100vw × 57vh); the document behind it is scroll-locked. Fades on `opacity` over 175ms. *Reads as:* a paywall's cousin. *Transfers to:* nowhere, as built — the mechanism (fixed positioner + opacity + inert background) is standard, but a modal that blocks the first scroll of a news homepage converts against the product's own premise. *Safety:* mechanically **production-safe**; a usability defect. If you ship it: return focus, close on `Esc`, and never lock scroll before first interaction.
- **Slide-over nav drawer with scrim** — *Trigger:* tap. *Mechanism:* a 25vw × 100vh paper drawer (z 4) over a `rgba(0,0,0,0.5)` scrim (z 3), toggled by class; 300ms `ease`. Standard, and the right amount of machinery for 38 links. *Safety:* **production-safe**.
- **IntersectionObserver as loader, not as animator** — *Mechanism:* **367 observers / 373 targets**, nearly all at `threshold: 0` with `rootMargin: 2250px 0px 2250px 0px` (and a few at `200%`). A 2250px root margin fires ~2.5 viewports early: this is image prefetch, ad-slot warmup, and impression counting — not entrance choreography (which the frames and `entranceReveals: 0` confirm doesn't exist). *Worth naming* because the same API is usually assumed to mean "fade things in." Pre-loading with a fat root margin is why a 67-image page can feel instant with 38 of those images lazy.
- **175ms link/state transitions** — *Trigger:* hover/focus. *Mechanism:* `color`/`border-color` transitions at 175ms `ease-in-out`, and nothing else. No transform, no scale, no underline slide.

**Reduced motion: unknown/effectively unguarded.** The probe reports `respectsReducedMotion: true`, but the document contains exactly **one** `prefers-reduced-motion` rule across 48 stylesheets, and it almost certainly belongs to the third-party consent widget (`termly-styles-module-rotate`). The site's own 60s clock loop shows no guard. Treated as unguarded here; the extracted pattern gates it.

## Production-safety note
Everything Semafor authors is production-safe by construction — two durations, two easings, one transform-only loop, zero scroll handlers of its own (the 15 scroll listeners belong to ad/analytics vendors: DoubleClick, DoubleVerify, Parse.ly, Sentry, HubSpot, LinkedIn, Meta). The defects to fix on reuse: (1) **no visible focus ring** (`focus_visible: false` — inexcusable on a page with 237 interactive elements and 38 nav links); (2) **78 of 237 interactive targets fall under the 44px minimum**, the nav row and rail links worst; (3) the 60s loop needs a `prefers-reduced-motion` stop; (4) the load-time modal scroll-lock. Alt coverage is exemplary at 99% across 67 images.

## Extracted patterns
- `dotted-rule-editorial-grid` — a three-column front page on a tinted paper ground where dotted hairlines and gutters replace every card, shadow, and border-radius; reuse for news, changelogs, dashboards-of-text, or any page where cards would fragment a continuous read.
- `phase-offset-instrument-row` — a row of identical dials/indicators driven by one keyframe loop, desynchronized with negative `animation-delay` and frozen at meaningful angles under reduced motion; reuse for status rows, world clocks, and ambient "we're live" chrome.
