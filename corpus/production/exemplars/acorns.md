## What works
- **A character system replaces stock photography and fake dashboards.** Every module is carried by the same photoreal-composite mascot (a suited squirrel) staged inside collaged textures — cut-out clouds, real oak leaves, halftone bar charts. Nothing on the page is a generic office photo or an invented "product screenshot" glow. Where the category is abstract and anxiety-adjacent (investing, insurance, health, legal), a consistent character does the emotional work that a UI screenshot cannot, and it costs nothing in trust because the *real* app screens still appear verbatim beside it. The rule that transfers: pick one visual protagonist and let it recur in every section, rather than sourcing a fresh metaphor per section.
- **Tiny tracked eyebrow, huge headline, no middle.** Section labels are 13px uppercase at 2.6px tracking (~0.2em); headlines jump straight to 40–72px semibold. The ~4× scale gap means there is no ambiguous "medium" tier competing for the eye, so a 12,000px page still reads as a sequence of discrete chapters. The eyebrow — not a larger heading — is what does the labeling. Transfers to any long marketing page; the eyebrow is the cheapest hierarchy device that exists.
- **Color chunks the page; rules and cards do not.** The spine is white/ecru. The two sections that must feel heaviest — the compounding calculator and the customer testimonial — are full-bleed forest-green and near-black bands. There are no divider lines, no card borders between sections, and almost no shadows (seven distinct box-shadows on the entire page). Ground color alone signals "new chapter, different emotional register." Transfers anywhere you'd otherwise reach for a `<hr>` or a boxed section.
- **Proof is computed by the visitor, not asserted by the page.** The pivotal section is a live compounding calculator — initial deposit, contribution + cadence, a years slider, an assumed return — driving a real bar chart whose headline number ($49,901) updates as you drag. The visitor manufactures their own reason to sign up. Then the disclosure sits *directly under the chart*, in the same band, not buried in the footer. For any product whose value is a projection (savings, ROI, time saved, cost per seat), an honest interactive model outperforms a testimonial and a claim, and shipping the caveat inline is what makes it credible rather than salesy.
- **One accent hue as ground, action, and mark — with a single spark color.** Acorn-green fills the primary pill CTA, the logo, the app screens, and eventually an entire full-bleed closing card. Exactly one secondary hue (electric purple) appears, and only on the Kids tile and the carousel's active dot. Brand recognition comes from the accent covering *surfaces*, not from adding more hues. Transfers to any single-brand product page.
- **Two radii, applied without exception.** Interactive things are full pills (50px on a 56px-tall button); containers are 20–30px squircles; imagery is masked into organic circles and rounded squircles rather than rectangles. Two radius tiers consistently applied read as a house style. A third would read as decoration.
- **The dense thing is inside the phone, not on the page.** The page is sparse; the app screens it shows are genuinely busy (live balance, sparkline, round-up controls). That contrast makes the product look capable while the marketing stays calm — the opposite of a page that proves capability by crowding itself.

## Design-system read
- **Type:** Avenir Next as a near-solo family (a geometric humanist sans — round bowls, low contrast, no sharp corners; it is doing the same "friendly, not childish" job the illustrations do). Display 72px/78px, tracking ~-0.0125em; H2 40/48; H3 32/36; body 16/26–30 at 400; eyebrow 13px/600/uppercase at 0.2em. Ratio ≈1.25 through the UI sizes with a hard 4.5× jump to display. Hierarchy = **scale + case + tracking**, and only two weights (400 body, 600 heading) — 700 is rare. Desktop→mobile display compresses 72→40px (1.8×), far more than the 1.0× body, so mobile stays legible without re-flowing the scale.
- **Color:** Ground is white with ecru (#faf9f1) and ivory (#f5f5f5) as the only light surfaces. Ink is a soft near-black (#191919), never pure. Accent acorn-green (#74c947) does triple duty as CTA fill, brand mark, and section ground; fern (#529f46) and moss (#bceaa3) are its darker/lighter siblings for charts. Emerald (#0a250d) and near-black (#212322) are the two dark bands. Purple (#6944ff) is the single non-green spark. Semantic color is essentially absent from the marketing page — the chart earns its two greens (contribution vs. return) rather than reaching for a second hue.
- **Spacing rhythm:** Base 4/8px, but the *page* rhythm is coarse: 40 / 48 / 60 / 100 / 120px gaps and section padding. Content sits in a 1167px wrapper (20px gutters) inside a 1440px max shell; full-bleed bands break out of it. The generosity is what lets the sparse page carry 10 sections without feeling like a scroll marathon.
- **Layout system:** Flexbox throughout — zero CSS Grid on the page (116 flex containers, 0 grid). The dominant device is a 2-up alternating text/image row (five of them), each image masked into an organic shape. Breakpoints are 767/768 and 1024/1025; everything collapses to a single centered column below 768px, and the alternation collapses with it (image always follows its heading on mobile — the reading order is fixed, not mirrored).
- **Layering / page architecture:** Essentially a **single flat scroll** — `architecture.layers_summary` reports `has_static_backdrop: false`, `has_blend_overlay: false`, no `backdrop-filter`, no `mix-blend-mode`. The only fixed layers are chrome: an opaque white header (z 998, 100% × 10vh), a persistent app-install/CTA bar pinned to the bottom, and third-party consent/a11y widgets. Depth comes entirely from *painted* collage — masked shapes overlapping a phone, tiles at different scroll rates — not from z-stacking translucent planes. Worth naming: a page can feel spatial without a single fixed backdrop.
- **Motion / easing intent:** Durations cluster at 250/300/400/500ms for chrome and 1.08–1.3s for the big crossfades. Two signature curves: `cubic-bezier(0.64, 0.04, 0, 1)` (a hard-out, long-settle ease) on nav submenus and hover borders, and `cubic-bezier(0.56, 0.02, 0.32, 1)` on link recolors. The intent is *nudging*, not choreography — nothing blocks reading, nothing announces itself.
- **Density:** Low on the page, high inside the artwork. 134 images (86 of them SVG), one h1, ten h2s — the page is a stack of ten single-idea chapters.
- **Hierarchy:** Scale first, ground color second (which band am I in), position third. Weight is barely used and color is never used to rank text.
- **Restraint:** No gradient mesh, no glassmorphism, no dark mode, no second display face on the home page (Knockout is loaded but unused here), no three-feature-card grid, no fake dashboard mockup, no shadow system to speak of. The one radial gradient on the page is inside an illustration.

## Interaction & motion
Evidence: 11 scroll frames, 24 scroll effects (10 `transform/parallax`, 6 `pin`, 6 opacity), 17 scroll listeners + 66 rAF calls and **zero** detected animation libraries — the motion is hand-rolled. Four `@keyframes` total (`full_rotation`, `border-train`, plus two vendor). `prmRules: 0`.

- **Crossfade hero carousel pinned to the viewport** — *Trigger:* load (autoplay) + tap. *Mechanism:* a `position: sticky`, 100vw × 100vh hero wrapper holds five stacked product panels; only `opacity` crossfades between them (`1→0` on the outgoing, `0→1` on the incoming, ~1.08–1.3s, `cubic-bezier(0.32, 0, 0.67, 0)`), with arrow controls and a five-dash pagination row. Frames confirm five distinct headlines occupying the identical box. *Reads as:* one product with five faces, not five heroes fighting for the fold. *Transfers to:* any multi-product company that refuses to pick one hero message. *Safety:* **production-safe** (opacity only, no layout) — but autoplay demands pause-on-hover/focus and a reduced-motion opt-out, neither of which this page ships.
- **Per-tile scroll parallax on an exploded product collage** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* a centered phone is surrounded by six feature tiles (Investing, Retirement, Kids, Banking, Learning, Bonus); each tile's `transform: translateY()` is driven off scroll position at a *different* rate, so the cluster fans apart as it crosses the viewport (frames s02→s03: tiles that overlap the phone at the top of the section have separated by the bottom). *Reads as:* "these are all one product, currently unpacking themselves." *Transfers to:* any suite/bundle section where a grid of cards would flatten the relationship. *Safety:* **production-safe** if transform-only; this implementation drives it from 17 scroll listeners + 66 rAF callbacks, which is more main-thread work than the effect needs. See `patterns/scroll-parallax-tile-collage.md` (CSS `animation-timeline: view()` first, JS fallback second).
- **Scroll-linked marquee headline as a chapter divider** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* an `h2` whose text is the section name repeated ten times ("Why Acorns - Why Acorns - …") in oversized pale grey, translated horizontally as a function of scroll progress rather than on a time loop, so it only moves while you do. *Reads as:* a chapter title, and the motion confirms your scroll rather than competing with it. *Transfers to:* section breaks on long pages, in place of a rule or a centered label. *Safety:* **production-safe** (transform on one element); a *time-based* marquee of the same thing would be spectacle-only, because perpetual motion beside body copy is a reading tax.
- **Slider-driven projection chart** — *Trigger:* drag/tap. *Mechanism:* range input + radio group + number fields recompute a 21-bar stacked chart and a headline currency figure on `input`, with the disclosure text rendered in the same band. *Reads as:* a calculator, not a marketing graphic. *Transfers to:* pricing, ROI, and savings pages. *Safety:* **production-safe**; the cost is correctness, not frames — the numbers must be honestly caveated inline, as here.
- **Testimonial crossfade with dot pagination** — *Trigger:* in-view / tap. *Mechanism:* stacked quote+portrait slides crossfaded on opacity (probe shows `0.86→1.00` / `0.14→0.00` mid-transition), ten dots below. *Safety:* **production-safe**.
- **Nav submenu drop and hover underline** — *Trigger:* hover/focus. *Mechanism:* fixed submenu panels transition `height` + `opacity` over 400–500ms on `cubic-bezier(0.64, 0.04, 0, 1)`; nav items grow a bottom border on the same curve. *Note:* transitioning `height` reflows — animate `max-height`/`transform: scaleY` or `grid-template-rows` instead. *Safety:* **production-safe** at this size, sloppy at scale.

**Reduced-motion: NOT respected** (`prmRules: 0`, `respectsReducedMotion: false`). An autoplaying hero crossfade, a scroll marquee, and six parallax tiles all run regardless of preference. Any reuse must gate them; the patterns below do.

## Production-safety note
Every technique here is **production-safe** in kind — opacity, transform, and a form that recomputes a chart. The three defects to fix on reuse: (1) no `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback anywhere; (2) parallax driven by scroll listeners rather than `animation-timeline: view()` or an rAF-throttled handler; (3) 54 of 145 interactive elements fall under the 44px tap-target minimum (footer link columns, carousel dots) — the pill CTAs are exemplary at 56px, the small print is not. Focus rings are present and visible (1px solid), alt coverage is 99%.

## Extracted patterns
- `organic-mask-alternating-rows` — alternating 2-up text/image feature rows where the imagery is masked into circles and squircles instead of boxed into cards; reuse for 4–6 sequential feature claims that a three-card grid would flatten.
- `scroll-parallax-tile-collage` — a centered product anchor with satellite tiles that drift apart at different scroll rates; reuse for suite/bundle sections, built on CSS scroll-driven animation with a reduced-motion opt-out.
