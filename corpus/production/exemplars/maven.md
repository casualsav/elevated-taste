## What works
- **The homepage is an index, not a pitch.** Below a 3-line hero sits a 24-cell grid of actual courses — real titles, real start dates, real instructor faces. There is no "three feature cards" tier, no invented dashboard, no benefit copy. The marketplace's inventory *is* the marketing, so the fold-to-conversion path is "see a thing you want" rather than "read a claim, then hunt for the thing." The rule that transfers: when the product is a catalog (courses, templates, listings, plugins, components), lead with density of real items; a curated grid of 24 concrete rows outperforms three abstract cards, and it never goes stale.
- **A dark page ground with white "sheets" laid on it.** The page body is a deep navy (`#080c28`); the catalog and other content blocks are full-bleed white rectangles stacked on top of it, edge-to-edge, with the navy showing between them. Chaptering is done by *ground inversion*, not by section padding or dividers. This is far more durable than alternating grey/white bands: each sheet reads as a discrete surface, and the dark ground makes the serif display type and the photographic instructor portraits both feel deliberate. Transfers to any long page that needs 5+ chapters without a shadow system.
- **Hairlines instead of cards.** The 24 course cells are separated by 1px rules and bounded by one outer rule — no per-card border-radius, no shadow, no hover lift. The grid reads like a printed schedule or a stock table. A card carries an implicit promise ("this is a self-contained object worth its own elevation"); a ruled cell says "this is one row of many, scan me." For any list longer than ~9 items, drop the card and draw the rule — density goes up and visual noise goes down at the same time. See `patterns/ruled-catalog-grid.md`.
- **One high-contrast typographic pairing carries the entire brand.** A transitional serif (Bureau Serif / Sentient) for every display line, and a *Light-weight* (300) grotesque for every piece of UI text and body copy. 2,876 of 3,115 measured elements are weight 300. Hierarchy therefore cannot come from weight — it comes from **face + scale**: 72px serif against 16px/300 sans is a 4.5× jump across a family switch, and nothing lives in between. The unusual move is body copy at Light rather than Regular; on a dark ground it reads as engraved rather than shouty, and it forces the serif to do all the emphasis. Transfers to any product with an editorial or institutional register.
- **Uniform −4% tracking across the whole display range.** 72px → −2.88px, 32px → −1.28px, 24px → −0.96px, 20px → −0.8px: every size ≥18px is tracked to exactly −0.04em. Most systems ramp tracking with size; this one holds it flat and lets the ratio do the work. Either is defensible — what matters is that it's *computed from the size*, never hand-tuned per heading. Below 18px the tracking stops entirely (body sits at 0).
- **The gutter is fluid; the column is not.** Horizontal padding scales `1rem → 1.5rem → 2.5rem → 5rem` across breakpoints, then at ≥1440px flips to `calc((100dvw - 1280px) / 2)` — the padding becomes the centering mechanism and the content column locks at 1280px forever. One custom property (`--responsive`) is the single source of both page padding and full-bleed breakout (`margin-inline: calc(var(--responsive) * -1)`), so a section can escape the column with one class and no math. Steal the technique verbatim; it removes every `max-width: X; margin: 0 auto` in a codebase.
- **The one decorative flourish is confined to a 7-tile grid.** Category tiles carry a faint blueprint line-drawing and a stacked colored "horizon" at the bottom edge — one hue per category, drawn from a real palette scale, not from a random gradient. Everything else on the page is type, rules, and photography. When exactly one region is allowed to be expressive, it becomes the brand mark; when every region is, none of them is.

## Design-system read
- **Type:** Two families, no third. Display = transitional serif (`fontBureauSerif`, with `fontSentient`/`fontDomaine` as siblings for sub-brands); UI/body = `fontBureauSans` at weight **300**, with 500 as the only emphasis weight (600 appears exactly once on the page). Size ladder 12 · 14 · 16 · 18 · 20 · 24 · 30 · 32 · 40 · 56 · 72 (ratio ≈1.2 through the UI range, then a 4.5× leap to display). Leading is set in px and stays tight: 16px body → 24px (1.5), 72px display → 80px (1.11). Tracking is a flat −0.04em on everything ≥18px, 0 below. Hero H1 compresses 72 → 24–28px on mobile; body stays 16px at both widths.
- **Color:** Ground is navy `#080c28`; content sheets are `#ffffff` and `#fafafa`. Ink is `#171717` on white, `#f7fbff` on navy — neither is pure. Muted text is `#737373` on light and `rgba(255,255,255,0.7)` on dark, which is the only opacity-based color in the system. Rules are `#e5e5e5` (light) and `#333a5a` (dark) — the dark hairline is a *desaturated navy*, not white-at-low-alpha, which is why the dark bands never look milky. Accent is a lapis ramp: `#71aeff` (link/focus/border), `#1c41ab` (product primary), `#8da5ff` (focus ring on dark). The one gradient in the system interpolates `#71aeff → #e18ff2` **in oklab** across the hero's accent words — an oklab ramp is why blue→magenta passes through lilac instead of grey. Category hues (`#9747ff` AI, `#47c8ff` Engineering, …) are decorative labels only; they never encode state.
- **Spacing rhythm:** `--spacing-unit: 8px`, and the page really does hold to it — gaps of 4/8/12/16/24/48, block padding of 8/16/24/40/48/80. Inline padding is delegated entirely to the fluid `--responsive` gutter (16 → 80px, then centering). Whitespace is *not* the chaptering device here (ground color is), so section rhythm stays tight and the page stays dense.
- **Layout system:** Flex-dominant (811 flex containers vs 60 grid). The catalog is a `repeat(3, 1fr)` grid collapsing to 1 column; instructor and lesson rails are horizontal overflow tracks (`scroll-snap` used once). Breakpoints observed at 375/414/420/480/768/960, with the Tailwind-style `--responsive` steps at 28rem/48rem/80rem and the 1440px column lock. 167 images, 163 with `srcset`, 94 lazy — the density is affordable because almost nothing is eager.
- **Layering / page architecture:** No static backdrop, no blend overlay, no `backdrop-filter` (`has_static_backdrop: false`, `has_blend_overlay: false`). The z-axis is pure chrome: a sticky navy masthead at `z-20` with a **second** sticky category subnav pinned beneath it, a `z-30` mobile drawer + bottom CTA, and a `z-9999` third-party widget layer. Depth on the page itself is created by *ground inversion and hairlines*, not by stacking translucent planes — the white catalog sheet sits on navy, and that's the whole story. Notable inversion of the usual pattern: the sticky chrome is the dark layer and the scrolling content is the light one.
- **Motion / easing intent:** Three durations do 95% of the work — 150ms (238 rules), 300ms (187), 500ms (140) — on a single curve, `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1)`, with `cubic-bezier(0, 0, 0.2, 1)` for entrances. Plus one 60s linear loop for the marquee rails. Motion communicates *state changed*, never *look at me*: hovers are text-decoration, reveals are opacity-only with no travel and no stagger.
- **Density:** Very high and legible. 500 interactive elements, 35 visible nav links, 56 `h3`s, 24 catalog cells above the second screen — held together by the hairline grid, one type size per role, and a body face at Light which lowers ink coverage enough that density stops feeling like pressure.
- **Hierarchy:** Face first (serif = important), then scale, then ground color. Weight is deliberately unavailable as a device; color never ranks text.
- **Restraint:** No card shadows (the shadow scale is loaded and essentially unused). No hover lift, no scale-on-hover, no glassmorphism, no hero illustration, no fake product screenshot, no testimonial carousel, no counter animations. The hero is three lines and three choice-tiles, ~260px tall — it refuses to occupy the fold.

## Interaction & motion
Evidence: 11 scroll frames, 34 `@keyframes` / 22 animation rules, **zero** detected animation libraries, 2 IntersectionObservers over 896 targets (`threshold: 0`, `rootMargin: 200px`), 11 marquee elements, one entrance effect (opacity `0.59 → 1.00` first changing at 150ms), three hover effects (all `text-decoration`), no cursor followers, no canvas, no carousels.

- **Duplicated-track marquee rail** — *Trigger:* load (perpetual). *Mechanism:* a flex track whose children are rendered twice, animated `transform: translateX(0 → -50%)` over 60s `linear infinite`; because the second copy is pixel-identical to the first, the wrap is seamless with no JS. Frames s06→s08 show the instructor portrait row shifted left while the section itself is still. `@keyframes marquee` plus siblings `scroll-left/right/up/down` confirm it's a house utility, not a one-off. *Reads as:* an inventory that exceeds the viewport — "there are more of these than fit." *Transfers to:* logo walls, instructor/author rows, testimonial rails, any set whose *size* is the message. *Safety:* **production-safe** — one compositor-only transform on one element, no listeners, no rAF. Cost: perpetual motion beside body copy is a reading tax, so keep it away from paragraphs and pause on hover/focus. See `patterns/duplicated-track-marquee.md`.
- **Opacity-only in-view reveal, no travel, no stagger** — *Trigger:* in-view. *Mechanism:* IntersectionObserver at `threshold: 0` with a **200px `rootMargin`** fades cells `0 → 1` over ~150ms as they approach the viewport. The generous root margin means the element is already opaque by the time it's readable; the observer is doing lazy-render and image loading (94 lazy images, 896 observed targets) more than it's doing choreography. *Reads as:* nothing at all — which is the point on a 24-item catalog, where a staggered rise would turn scanning into waiting. *Transfers to:* every long list, table, and feed. *Safety:* **production-safe**; the rule to copy is `rootMargin` ≥ 200px so the animation finishes off-screen.
- **Two-tier sticky chrome (masthead + category subnav)** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* two independently `position: sticky; top: 0` bars at `z-20`, both painted opaque navy so the white catalog scrolls cleanly under them; the subnav carries the 8 category dropdowns. *Reads as:* the taxonomy stays reachable no matter how deep you scroll a long index. *Transfers to:* docs, catalogs, dashboards with filter bars. *Safety:* **production-safe**; the cost is vertical budget (~100px of a 900px viewport here) — earn it only when the second bar is navigation, never when it's a promo.
- **Ghost rank numerals in a display/pixel face** — *Trigger:* static. *Mechanism:* each catalog cell carries its ordinal (1–24) in the bottom-right in an outlined pixel/ticker face (`fontTickerbit`) at low contrast, sized ~2× the body. *Reads as:* a chart position — "trending this week" is *shown*, not asserted. *Transfers to:* any ranked list where the number is a fact rather than a decoration.
- **Hover is underline, full stop** — *Trigger:* hover. *Mechanism:* the only measured hover deltas on the entire page are `text-decoration: none → underline` on nav links. No transform, no color shift, no shadow. *Reads as:* the page trusts its own hit targets. *Transfers to:* dense UI where 500 interactive elements each animating on hover would be a swarm.

**Reduced-motion: respected, but bluntly.** The stylesheet ships exactly one `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` block — a blanket `*, ::before, ::after { animation: none; transform: none; }` reset (the Tailwind preflight default). It does correctly kill the marquee and the reveals, but a blanket `transform: none` also flattens any layout that legitimately uses `transform` for positioning. Better practice, used in both patterns below: opt each *effect* out and jump it to its end state. (The raw probe reports `respectsReducedMotion: false`/`prmRules: 0`; the CSS disagrees — trust the CSS, which was read verbatim.)

## Production-safety note
Everything here is **production-safe**: opacity, one transform, and a lot of static markup. Three things to fix on reuse: (1) the marquee has no hover/focus pause, so a keyboard user tabbing into a moving rail chases the target; (2) reduced-motion is a global sledgehammer rather than per-effect opt-outs; (3) 18 of ~500 interactive elements fall under the 44px tap-target minimum (footer links, dropdown carets) — the catalog cells themselves are large and exemplary. Focus rings are present and token-driven (`#8da5ff` 2px on dark, `#71aeff` on light), and alt coverage is 100% across 167 images.

## Extracted patterns
- `ruled-catalog-grid` — a dense grid of items separated by shared 1px hairlines instead of cards, with a ghost ordinal per cell; reuse for any index of 9+ real things (courses, listings, changelog entries) where a card grid would add elevation without adding meaning.
- `duplicated-track-marquee` — a seamless infinite horizontal rail built by rendering the track's children twice and translating `-50%`, with hover/focus pause and a reduced-motion end-state; reuse for logo walls, author rows, and any collection whose size is the message.
