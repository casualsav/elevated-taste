## What works
- **The hero carries no words.** A full-bleed, edge-to-edge photo carousel occupies the fold with zero overlaid headline, zero CTA, zero scrim — only a pause control and four dots. The first text on the page is a plain `ABOUT` heading below it. When the product is *sensory* (food, hotels, garments, venues), an overlaid promise ("Elevated seasonal dining") subtracts from the photograph without adding information; the photograph already made the argument. The rule that transfers: if your hero image can't be improved by the sentence you'd lay over it, delete the sentence and let the image be full-bleed. The corollary is that the fold must then *end visibly* — here the carousel stops ~630px down so the next section peeks, which is what actually gets people scrolling.
- **Answers before atmosphere.** Immediately under the fold, every location gets address, phone, email, and per-day hours in a label/value stack, with one reservation button. Brand storytelling ("The Gjelina Group…") appears *after* all five locations. Most restaurant and local-business sites invert this: a manifesto, a mood reel, and the phone number buried in the footer. Anyone who typed the brand name into a search bar wants a fact, and the page's own headings are those facts. Transfers to any site where a substantial share of visits are logistical (clinics, venues, studios, stores, support pages).
- **Two typefaces separated by *width and case*, not weight.** Headings are Anton — an ultra-condensed grotesque — set in all caps at 44–56px. Body is Solway, a slab serif, at 16px/1.5. Both live at weight 400; 700 only appears on inline field labels ("Phone:", "Hours:"). The contrast is so extreme along the width axis that no size ramp is needed between them, which is why an eight-`h1` page never blurs into mush. Transfers anywhere you want a loud heading voice without a bold-weight arms race — pick a condensed display face and a wide text face and hold both at regular.
- **A hairline system in one accent color does the work of cards.** There are no cards on this page: no borders around content, no shadows (the profile finds exactly one `box-shadow`, from a third-party widget), and 2px is the largest radius anywhere. Separation comes from three uses of one brick red — a rule across the top of the page, a rule above the footer, and a thick underline under every heading. Each `<h1>` is *its own divider*. Transfers directly to any design that reaches for a bordered card grid to separate items that are already separated by their headings.
- **Three colors, and one of them is the photography.** Cream ground (`#f9e4c2`), black ink, brick red for accent/rule/button-fill — plus white, which appears only inside images. Because the ground is a warm tint rather than white, the food photography (which is overwhelmingly warm) reads as continuous with the page rather than pasted onto it. Choosing a page ground *sampled from the imagery* is a cheaper unifier than any filter or overlay. Transfers to any photo-led site; the discipline is that the accent is then allowed exactly one hue.
- **The invert-on-hover button.** The primary button is a sharp-cornered brick rectangle with cream, tracked, uppercase text; hovering swaps ground and ink (cream bg, black text, translucent red border) over 200ms. It reads as a stamped card, not a UI chip — appropriate for a brand whose physical artifacts are letterpress menus. A pill with a shadow would have imported a SaaS accent into a restaurant.
- **Section reveals are opacity-only.** Whole sections fade `0 → 1` over ~1s when they cross a 0.2 IntersectionObserver threshold. Nothing slides, nothing staggers per-child, nothing translates. On a 9,000px page of static content this is the entire motion budget, and it costs no layout work. Transfers as the *default* reveal for content-heavy pages: the version that adds a 24px rise is already a more expensive promise than the content is keeping.

## Design-system read
- **Type:** Anton (display, 100% of headings, all-caps, `letter-spacing: normal` — the face is already condensed) and Solway (slab serif; body, labels, nav, buttons). Scale: 56 / 44 / 32 / 24 / 19.2 / 16 / 14.4 / 12px — a ~1.25 ratio through the text sizes with a 2.75–3.5× jump to display. Leading is binary and strict: **1.1 on every heading, 1.5 on every text size** (61.6/56, 48.4/44, 28.8/19.2, 24/16, 21.6/14.4). Tracking exists only on uppercase UI: 1px on nav/buttons, 0.5px on small caps. Weight does almost nothing (474 elements at 400, 52 at 700). Mobile drops the display 56→48px and leaves body at 16px.
- **Color:** Cream `#f9e4c2` is the ground for the *entire* page including the header — there is no white surface tier and no second background. Ink is pure `#000000` (not softened), brick `#b44226` is simultaneously the link color, the rule color (65 border occurrences), the button fill, and the logo's first letter. Cream inverts to become text and hairlines *on* brick (`rgba(249,228,194,0.5)` for translucent borders). A single alert red `#d5413d` appears three times, semantically. `#ffffff` exists only as photography and as text over photography.
- **Spacing rhythm:** Base 8px, but the section rhythm is 80px block padding with 30/40px gutters; smaller values (4/8/16px) are confined to inline chrome and field labels. The generous 80px bands plus the 1.5 body leading are what let a page that is mostly dense contact data feel unhurried.
- **Layout system:** Flexbox only — **zero CSS Grid elements on the page.** Content sits in an 1188px container (a 640px one for prose). Three repeating section types: a two-column "freeform" block that pairs two location cards side by side, a `c-split` alternating text/image row (eight of them, `--odd`/`--even` mirrored, used for the sibling businesses), and a two-column photo mosaic with no captions. Nav collapses from 9 links to a hamburger below the mobile breakpoint.
- **Layering / page architecture:** A **single flat scroll**. `layers_summary` reports `has_static_backdrop: false`, `has_blend_overlay: false`, no `backdrop-filter`, no `mix-blend-mode`, zero `sticky` elements. Five fixed layers, all chrome: the pinned opaque header (frames s00/s03/s06 show it pixel-identical at every scroll depth — same cream ground, same red top rule, no shrink, no blur, no shadow-on-scroll), a mobile action bar, and three third-party widgets (accessibility launcher, reCAPTCHA badge). `transparent_height_pct: 93` is a non-signal here: every section is transparent because they all sit directly on the one cream `<body>`, with nothing behind them to reveal. A header that refuses to restyle itself on scroll is a legitimate choice when the header and the page are the same color — there is no edge to defend.
- **Motion / easing intent:** `0.2s ease-in-out` is the site-wide chrome transition (97 occurrences); `1s ease` (17) is the section reveal; `2s ease` (4) and `1.5s ease` are the carousel crossfade; one `0.5s cubic-bezier(0.77, 0, 0.175, 1)` drives the mobile menu panel. **Zero `@keyframes`, zero animation libraries.** Motion says "this page is composed of quiet chapters," never "look at this."
- **Density:** High in the data, low in the chrome. Eight `h1`s, seven `h2`s, 105 interactive elements, and a full operational dataset for five businesses — carried without a single container border.
- **Hierarchy:** Scale first (56px caps vs 16px body), then the underline rule (which converts a heading into a section boundary), then position. Color ranks *nothing*: every heading is the same black, every rule is the same red.
- **Restraint:** No gradient anywhere (`elevation.gradients: []`). No shadow. No radius. No dark mode. No text over the hero. No testimonial. No three-feature-card grid. No icons except social glyphs in the footer. No second accent hue. The photography is uncropped, unfiltered, and uncaptioned.

## Interaction & motion
Evidence: 11 scroll frames, 4 scroll effects (2 of which are third-party fixed badges), 2 entrance reveals, 0 `@keyframes`, 0 detected libraries, 1 IntersectionObserver at `threshold: 0.2`, `prmRules: 0`. This is a near-static page; the four techniques below are its whole vocabulary.

- **In-view section fade (opacity only)** — *Trigger:* in-view. *Mechanism:* a `.revealable → .revealed` class toggled by a single IntersectionObserver at `threshold: 0.2`; the class transitions `opacity: 0 → 1` over `1s ease`. Confirmed by the entrance probe (hero section `0 → 0.99` at 500ms; About section `0 → 0.83` at 800ms) and by the fact that no `transform` appears in any scroll effect. *Reads as:* pages turning, not elements arriving. *Transfers to:* long content pages where a per-child stagger would be a lie about how much is happening. *Safety:* **production-safe** (compositor-only, no reflow) — but the 1s duration is long enough to be perceived as "slow to load" if content is above the fold, and there is no reduced-motion gate. See `patterns/caption-free-hero-crossfade.md` for the gated form.
- **Caption-free hero crossfade with an explicit pause control** — *Trigger:* load (autoplay) + tap. *Mechanism:* four stacked full-bleed slides in a slick carousel; only `opacity` animates (probe: slide 1 `0 → 0.92`, slide 4 `0.92 → 0.08`) over ~1.5–2s `ease`. A pause button and four dots sit centered at the bottom — over the image, not in a chrome bar. *Reads as:* one restaurant photographed four ways. *Transfers to:* any hero where the imagery is the message; the pause control is the part most sites omit. *Safety:* **production-safe**; autoplay's cost is attention and it must be pausable — this page ships the pause button but *not* a `prefers-reduced-motion` stop, which is the missing half. See `patterns/caption-free-hero-crossfade.md`.
- **Ground/ink inversion on hover** — *Trigger:* hover/focus. *Mechanism:* the CTA swaps `background` and `color` between the two brand colors (brick `#b44226` + cream `#f9e4c2` → cream + black, border to `rgba(196,67,39,0.5)`) over `0.2s ease-in-out`. The nav logo does the same in miniature (black → brick). *Reads as:* a stamped object being pressed. *Transfers to:* any two-color brand; it needs no third hue, no shadow, and no scale transform to register. *Safety:* **production-safe** — but a `background-color` transition on a large element is a paint, not a composite; keep it to buttons.
- **Height-transitioned disclosure panels** — *Trigger:* tap/hover. *Mechanism:* nine disclosure widgets and five tab groups; nav submenus and the announcement banner animate `max-height` (`transition: max-height 1.5s 0.5s` on the banner), not `height`, which avoids per-frame reflow of the content. *Reads as:* content unfolding in place. *Transfers to:* menus, FAQs, nav flyouts. *Safety:* **production-safe**; `max-height` is the correct primitive here, though `grid-template-rows: 0fr → 1fr` is the modern form that doesn't require guessing the ceiling.

**Reduced-motion: NOT respected** (`prmRules: 0`, `respectsReducedMotion: false`). The autoplaying hero and the 1s section fades run regardless of preference. The cost is low (opacity only) but the fix is one media query; the patterns below include it.

## Production-safety note
Every technique is **production-safe** in kind — opacity crossfades, a color swap, and a `max-height` disclosure. Three defects to fix on reuse: (1) no `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback anywhere, and an *autoplaying* hero is the case where that matters most; (2) 40 of 105 interactive elements fall below the 44px tap-target minimum — the footer link columns and the 12px carousel dots are the offenders, while the reservation buttons are exemplary; (3) the page ships 331 rAF calls and 31 mousemove listeners despite having no motion that needs either — that is the accessibility overlay and analytics, not the design, and it is a reason to be suspicious of overlay widgets rather than of this layout. Focus outlines are present and visible (3px on every sampled interactive element) and alt coverage is 100%.

## Extracted patterns
- `underlined-caps-info-block` — a condensed all-caps heading with a thick accent underline over a label/value fact stack and one square CTA; reuse for locations, offices, plans, or team listings that a bordered card grid would flatten.
- `caption-free-hero-crossfade` — a full-bleed autoplaying image hero with no overlaid copy, a real pause control, and a reduced-motion stop; reuse where the photograph is the headline.
