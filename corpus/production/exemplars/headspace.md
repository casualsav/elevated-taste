## What works
- **The hero routes instead of persuading.** Above the fold there is no single headline-plus-CTA; there are two equal cards ("Mental health app with expert-led meditations and tools" / "Online therapy that accepts insurance"), each with its own primary and secondary action. Immediately below sits a six-cell grid of *goals* — Stress less, Sleep soundly, Manage anxiety, Process thoughts, Practice meditation, Start therapy. Where one company sells two genuinely different products to overlapping audiences, the fold's job is disambiguation, not conversion: a visitor who self-selects in the first screen arrives at the pitch already qualified. Transfers to any multi-product, multi-intent company (a bank with consumer + business, a tool with cloud + self-hosted). The failure it avoids is the compromise headline that describes neither product.
- **Saturated full-bleed bands are the chunking device — no rules, no card borders between sections.** Chapters are announced by *ground color*: sunflower yellow, cobalt blue, sky-photo blue, warm off-white. Text and illustration sit directly on the band; the band is the container. Nothing is nested inside a bordered card that is itself inside a section. This is why a ~8,700px page reads as eight rooms rather than one scroll. Transfers wherever you'd otherwise reach for `<hr>` or a boxed section — and it costs one hex per chapter instead of a shadow system.
- **A friendly typeface plus tight tracking is the whole brand voice.** The face is a geometric humanist sans (Apercu) with circular bowls and no sharp corners, and *every* heading is tracked at a flat **-0.03em** (measured: 64px/-1.92px, 52px/-1.56px, 32px/-0.96px, 24px/-0.72px — the ratio is constant), while body sits near -0.0075em. Loose tracking on a round face reads as a children's book; tightening it uniformly keeps the warmth and adds authority. The transferable rule: pick the *tracking* to correct a face's temperature, not a second font.
- **Body copy is set at 500, not 400.** The base weight across ~2,900 nodes is medium, on a warm near-black (#4B4C4D) over a warm off-white (#F9F4F2). Medium-on-warm holds its color at small sizes where regular-on-white goes grey and thin, and it lets headings do their work with **scale alone** — 700 appears only ~280 times. Transfers to any soft/warm palette; it's the cheapest way to make low-contrast ink still feel deliberate rather than washed out.
- **One easing curve, one duration, applied per-property everywhere.** Effectively every interactive element carries `150ms cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.94, 0.6, 1)` on color, background-color, border-color, box-shadow, and transform — a fast-out, long-settle curve that never overshoots. A single signature curve is what makes a hundred unrelated components feel like one product; the moment a page has three curves it has three authors. Transfers verbatim, and note the discipline of enumerating properties rather than `transition: all`.
- **Illustration replaces both stock photography and fake dashboards — but real photos carry real people.** The product modules are drawn (gradient "blob" characters, cut-paper shapes, confetti sprites absolutely positioned around the hero). The two sections that make *social* claims — enterprise customers, "join the millions" — use unretouched photographs of actual faces. The split is principled: drawings for what the product feels like, photographs for who else is here. Reaching for a glowing invented UI screenshot would have broken both.
- **Two radii and no third.** Interactive things are full pills (`border-radius: 2rem` on a 3rem-tall button, `7rem` on the hero CTA — both resolve to a capsule); containers and imagery are 1rem–1.5rem squircles; avatars are circles. Two consistently applied tiers read as a house style; a third reads as decoration.

## Design-system read
- **Type:** *Headspace Apercu* as a solo family (Apercu Pro Mono is loaded but unused on this page — worth noting as restraint). Mobile-first ramp per element: display `2.25rem → 2.5rem → 3.25rem → 4rem` across the three breakpoints; H2 `1.5rem → 2rem`; body `1rem`; small `0.875rem`/`0.75rem`. Ratio ≈1.25 through UI sizes with a ~4× jump to display. Line-heights: `1.1–1.2` on display, `1.375–1.5` on body. Weights: 500 body, 700 heading, 600 rare — hierarchy is **scale + tracking**, never color. `fluid_type: false` — they step at breakpoints rather than clamp.
- **Color:** Ground is white; the recurring surface is a warm off-white (#F9F4F2) used for cards, pills, and the footer. Ink is warm and tiered: #2D2C2B (headings) → #4B4C4D (body) → #63605D (meta). The action color is a single cobalt (#0061EF, hover #0040EA, active #0000DB) and it is the *only* interactive color — links, pills, and the enterprise band all share it. The brand hues (yellow #FFCE00, pink #FFA1CC, purple #3B197F, green #02873E, cyan #00A4FF) are **ground colors only**; none of them ever ranks text or signals state. Semantic color is absent from the marketing page. That separation — accent for action, palette for chapters — is what keeps a five-hue page from looking like a toy.
- **Spacing rhythm:** Base 4/8px, exposed as a named rem scale that the utility classes make explicit: `0.25 / 0.5 / 0.75 / 1 / 1.5 / 2 / 2.5 / 3 / 3.5rem` (2xs→4xl), with `4 / 5 / 6 / 8rem` for section padding. Inline gutters are a flat 24px on mobile, 48px on desktop. Content maxes at `90rem` (1440px) with narrower measures nested inside (`47rem`, `53rem`, `68.5rem`) — prose never spans the shell.
- **Layout system:** Mobile-first, three `min-width` breakpoints only: **48rem / 64rem / 90rem** (768/1024/1440). Grid and flex are mixed pragmatically — `repeat(2, minmax(0, 1fr))` → `repeat(4, …)` for the goal chooser and logo rows; flex for everything else. Below 768px the ten-link nav collapses to a hamburger (2 visible links) and every grid becomes one column. Horizontal carousels (two of them) keep their overflow on mobile rather than restacking.
- **Layering / page architecture:** **Single flat scroll.** `architecture.layers_summary` reports `has_static_backdrop: false`, `has_blend_overlay: false`; `blend_glass` is empty on all three axes (no `mix-blend-mode`, no `background-blend-mode`, no `backdrop-filter`). The only fixed layer that belongs to the site is the opaque white header (`position: fixed`, z-index 9) — every other fixed/sticky node in the profile is the consent SDK at z-index 2147483645+. Depth is *painted*: confetti sprites and blob characters overlap the hero cards, the sky-photo band recedes behind cut-out avatars. The one `clip-path` on the page (`inset(0 -8px -8px)`) exists only so the nav mega-menu's shadow can bleed downward without leaking upward. `surfaces.transparent_height_pct: 100` is noise here — sections are transparent because the colored bands are painted by inner wrappers, not because anything shows through.
- **Motion / easing intent:** Durations cluster tightly: **150ms** (all interactive state), 300ms (card borders), 400ms (carousel dots), 100ms (slider thumb). One signature curve `cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.94, 0.6, 1)` plus plain `ease`/`ease-out` for vendor bits. Zero site-authored `@keyframes` — the only two in the entire stylesheet belong to the cookie SDK. Motion here says *acknowledgement*, never *performance*: nothing enters, nothing reveals, nothing announces itself. The animation budget is spent instead on Lottie characters inside the product art (1,752 rAF calls, SVG `<g>` groups changing transform and opacity in view).
- **Density:** Deliberately low. Nine headings on 8,755px. 126 images, 316 interactive elements — the density lives inside the carousels and the footer's five link columns, not in the reading spine.
- **Hierarchy:** Ground color first (which chapter am I in), scale second, position third. Weight is a coarse binary (500/700) and text color is never used to rank.
- **Restraint:** No gradient mesh, no glassmorphism, no dark mode, no fake dashboard mockup, no three-feature-card grid, no entrance animations, no hover lifts, no second display face despite one being loaded, and — striking for a wellness brand — no full-bleed background video.

## Interaction & motion
Evidence: 11 scroll frames, 5 scroll effects (4 `transform/parallax`, 1 opacity), 8 IntersectionObservers over 36 targets, 26 scroll listeners, 1,752 rAF calls, **0 detected libraries**, 2 `@keyframes` (both OneTrust), 57 transition rules, `prmRules: 0`. `entrance: []` and `hover: []` are empty — the profile agrees with the frames that nothing reveals on scroll.

- **Scroll-linked ticker band** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* a full-bleed 100vw yellow band whose inner strip ("find some headspace" repeated, separated by animated eye/smile glyphs) is translated horizontally as a function of scroll position — the profile catches its `transform` under `trigger: scroll-progress`, and frame s05 shows the strip mid-travel, clipped at both edges. It moves only while you do. *Reads as:* a chapter break with a heartbeat; the brand's voice said once, decoratively, without asking to be read. *Transfers to:* section dividers on long marketing pages, in place of a rule or a centered label. *Safety:* **production-safe** — one transform on one element, no reflow. A *time-based* loop of the same strip would be spectacle-only: perpetual motion next to body copy is a reading tax and a vestibular hazard. See `patterns/scroll-linked-ticker-band.md`.
- **Lottie character loops inside the artwork, not on the page** — *Trigger:* in-view, gated by IntersectionObserver. *Mechanism:* SVG `<g>` groups (the chat bubble in the Ebb card, the closing "Arc" hero) drive `transform` and `opacity` from a JSON animation player mounted into empty `data-testid="…-lottie"` divs; the probe's 1,752 rAF calls and the `g` / `g.italwaysends` scroll effects are these players, not page choreography. *Reads as:* the illustrations are alive, the layout is still. *Transfers to:* any page that wants warmth without moving its own furniture — animate the picture, never the container. *Safety:* **production-safe** on the compositor, but it is real main-thread cost, so gate playback on in-view (they do) *and* on reduced-motion (they don't).
- **Pill-tab filtered carousel** — *Trigger:* tap/click, then horizontal scroll or arrow. *Mechanism:* a row of pill filters ("Featured / Popular / Sleep / Stress / Meditation and Mindfulness") swaps the contents of a horizontally overflowing card rail; the active pill inverts to solid ink while the rest stay on the warm off-white surface. Frames s05→s06 show the rail and its next/prev controls. *Reads as:* a browsable library rather than a curated top-four. *Transfers to:* content catalogs, template galleries, docs landing pages. *Safety:* **production-safe**. Two costs to fix on reuse: the probe reports `tabs: 0`, i.e. no `role="tablist"`/`aria-selected` wiring, and the rail must remain keyboard-scrollable — a mouse-only rail is a dead end for keyboard users.
- **Per-property 150ms state transition on every control** — *Trigger:* hover/focus. *Mechanism:* `color, background-color, border-color, box-shadow, transform` each transitioned at `150ms cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.94, 0.6, 1)`; focus draws a `0.125rem` solid outline in the deep blue (#0040EA). No lift, no scale, no shadow bloom. *Reads as:* responsive, not eager. *Safety:* **production-safe**; the enumerated property list (rather than `all`) is why it stays cheap.
- **Native `<details>`/`<summary>` FAQ with a plus/minus swap** — *Trigger:* click. *Mechanism:* 16 disclosure elements, no JS, icon swap only; mirrored by an `FAQPage` JSON-LD block so the same eight answers are machine-readable. *Reads as:* boring, in the way infrastructure should be. *Transfers to:* every FAQ ever. *Safety:* **production-safe**, keyboard- and screen-reader-correct for free.

**Reduced-motion: NOT respected** (`prmRules: 0`, `respectsReducedMotion: false`). The ticker band, the Lottie loops, and the vendor banner's own fade all run regardless of preference. The exposure is small because the page has no entrance choreography to suppress — but the ticker and the character loops are exactly what a vestibular-sensitive user would want off. The pattern below gates them.

## Production-safety note
Every technique here is **production-safe** in kind. Three defects to fix on reuse: (1) no `prefers-reduced-motion` block exists anywhere in the stylesheet; (2) **168 of 316 interactive elements fall under the 44px tap-target minimum** — the pill CTAs are exemplary, the footer's five link columns and the carousel dots are not; (3) the filter pills are styled buttons without tab semantics. Credit where due: `img_alt_ratio` is 1.0 across 126 images, and focus rings are a visible 2px outline rather than the removed-and-forgotten default.

## Extracted patterns
- `intent-router-hero` — a fold that disambiguates instead of pitching: two equal offer cards side by side, followed by a grid of goal-shaped entry links; reuse when one company genuinely sells two products to overlapping audiences.
- `scroll-linked-ticker-band` — a full-bleed marquee strip whose horizontal travel is bound to scroll progress rather than a time loop; reuse as a chapter divider, with a reduced-motion opt-out and a static fallback.
