## What works
- **A single sustained metaphor carries the brand, not decoration.** The whole page is dressed as a desktop operating system: a top menu bar with traffic-light controls and a "hedgehog.exe" window title, a left rail of app icons, and content sections that read as draggable windows. Because the conceit is *consistent and structural* (it organizes the layout) rather than sprinkled on, density and whimsy feel intentional instead of noisy. Transfers to any product that wants personality: pick one world and commit to it as the layout system, don't garnish a generic page with mascots.
- **Heavy uniform outlines are the structuring device in place of shadow/whitespace.** Nearly every element sits inside the same warm near-black 1px stroke (`#4d4f46`, by far the most-used color in the profile). The repeated outline does the work that elevation and gutters do on minimalist sites — it groups, separates, and gives a hand-drawn "sticker book / comic panel" coherence. Transferable any time you want high information density to still read as ordered: one border color, one weight, everywhere.
- **A warm tinted canvas instead of white de-risks playfulness.** The page background is tan/cream (`#e1d7c2` / `#eef0e9`), not `#fff`. The off-white paper tone makes hand-drawn illustration, bright accents, and dense UI feel editorial and deliberate rather than like an unfinished prototype on white. Cheap trick, big payoff: shift the canvas 5–10% off pure white before adding expressive elements.
- **One friendly family + extreme weight restraint keeps chaos legible.** Everything is set in *one* rounded geometric sans (Open Runde); 88% of text is a single weight (400). With color, illustration, and outlines already loud, holding type to one family and mostly one weight is what prevents the page from tipping into ransom-note slop. Hierarchy is carried by size, color-fills, and the outlined containers — not by font-juggling.
- **A bright single accent anchors a rainbow of category colors.** Orange-red (`#f54e00`) owns every primary CTA and link; the dozen other hues (cyan, magenta, amber, green…) are *coding* for product categories, never for emphasis. So the eye still knows where to click despite the riot of color: one job per color. Reusable in any multi-product/feature directory.
- **Dense, scannable product grid beats prose for a broad platform.** Rather than explaining a suite in paragraphs, PostHog shows a tiled directory of ~16 products as small outlined icon+label cells. For a "we do many things" story this conveys breadth instantly and invites scanning. Transfers to platform/integration/feature-suite sections.

## Design-system read
- **Type:** Single family, Open Runde (rounded geometric sans; an IBM Plex Sans variable face also appears in the capture, embedded in code/UI samples). Tight effective scale — body 16px, secondary 15/14px, section headings ~24px, hero display ~36px. Small range (~2.25× body→display); hierarchy is *not* scale-driven. **Tracking is proportional, tightening with size, not one constant em:** ~-0.025em (-0.6px) at 24px headings, ~-0.025em (-0.54px) at ~21px, ~-0.025em (-0.48px) at ~19px, and 0 on body — i.e. a steady ≈-2.5% applied only above body, so the absolute pixel pull grows with the display size while body stays neutral. Fluid/clamped type (`fluid_type: true`) and tabular/figure numeric features (`var(--tw-numeric-*)`, `proportional-nums`) are present. Weights overwhelmingly 400, with 600 for labels/buttons and rare 700/800 accents.
- **Color:** Warm-neutral system, not greyscale. Canvas = tan/cream (`#e1d7c2`, `#eef0e9`); ink + borders = warm near-black (`#4d4f46`, `#23251d`); secondary text = warm grey (`#9ea096`, `#65675e`). Single brand accent = orange-red (`#f54e00`) for CTAs/links; gold (`#f7a501`) as secondary. A category palette (cyan `#30abc6`, magenta `#b62ad9`, amber, green `#6aa84f`, teal `#29dbbb`) tags products. Borders are a first-class color role here, heavier-used than backgrounds.
- **Spacing rhythm:** Tight base (≈4px unit; gaps cluster at 4/6/2/8px). Dense by design — chips, icon cells, and list rows pack closely inside their outlines, then sections get larger blocks (16/24/48/64px padding). Whitespace is *not* the separator; outlines are, so the page can be dense without feeling crowded.
- **Layout system:** Faux-OS shell — fixed top menu bar + left vertical app-icon rail framing a central content column. Content reads as stacked "windows." Multi-column inner grids: 4-up product directory, tabbed showcase, alternating illustration/text rows. Mobile (1170w capture) collapses the rail, stacks every grid to one column, and keeps the same outlined cells and tan canvas — the metaphor degrades gracefully to a vertical scroll.
- **Layering / page architecture:** The tan "canvas" is **not** a `body` background — it's a **fixed full-viewport wallpaper painted at `-z-10`** (`div fixed inset-0 -z-10`, bg `#e1d7c2`; profile `has_static_backdrop: true`, one `static-backdrop` layer at 100×100 coverage) that never scrolls. Content sections carry **no background of their own** (`surfaces.summary.transparent_height_pct: 100`, 2/2 sampled blocks transparent), so the desktop shows through the gutters around the opaque window "sheets" (the white `home.mdx` editor card) and behind both icon rails. Confirmed in-pixel: the speckled tan desktop and the illustrated keyboard-garden scene (hedges, gardening hedgehogs at the page edges) stay **pixel-identical across frames s00→s10** while only the central window's tabs/banner change. The wallpaper is **theme-swappable** — sibling `hidden` fixed `inset-0` layers gated on `wallpaper-parade` / `wallpaper-coding-at-night` are alternate desktops. This completes the OS conceit at the paint layer: the desktop has a persistent wallpaper and the windows float over it. → `fixed-backdrop-reveal`.
- **Grid / container:** Captured at vw 1440; re-probe confirms inner grids run **4 / 2 / 3 columns** for the three main blocks (4-up product directory, 2-up feature rows, 3-up sub-grid) with a **measured tight ~4–6px gutter** inside cells (16/24px between blocks). **Container max-width still not isolated** — the re-probe surfaced only component/card widths (~496/896/900px), not a single content column, because the layout is a full faux-OS shell rather than one centered column; so the container stays **frame-estimated at a centered ~1100–1200px content column inset from the app rail**. **Responsive:** desktop `[4,2,3]` collapses to mobile `[1,2,7]` at 390w — the 4-up directory becomes a single column while a 3-up reflows to a denser 7-row run; nav drops from **14 visible links → 5** with a likely hamburger; h1 (24px) and body (16px) do **not** downscale on mobile (same sizes both breakpoints).
- **Components (from captured states):**
  - **Button (nav/menu):** resting transparent on tan; **focus** fills `rgb(200,202,193)` (a warm grey wash, focus-visible is present); the primary `Get started – free` CTA shows a real **focus outline `1px rgb(16,16,16)`** and a hover lift `translateY(-2px→-3px)`.
  - **Button (outlined / secondary):** resting border transparent; **hover** reveals border `rgb(158,160,150)` or `rgb(182,183,175)` (the warm-grey hairline) — border-on-hover, not fill.
  - **Chip (numbered):** resting bg `rgb(229,231,224)` / text `rgb(101,103,94)`; **hover** bg→`#fff`, text→warm ink `rgb(77,79,70)`.
  - **Card / category button:** in-view fill/empty recolor (see Interaction & motion) — active = orange/category fill, inactive = outlined-neutral.
  - **Input / disabled:** **disabled** state keeps `opacity:1` (not dimmed) with text greyed to `rgb(158,160,150)`, transparent bg, and `cursor: not-allowed` — affordance is the grey text + cursor, NOT opacity (verify the grey clears AA on tan).
- **Motion / easing intent:** Restrained micro-interaction, not scroll spectacle — the personality lives in the static illustration and chrome, so motion stays cheap. Captured params (`motion.params`): durations cluster at **150 / 300ms** for state/transition work, with **2000 / 3000ms** reserved for slow ambient illustration loops (the `float`, `breathe`, `wiggle`, `bounce`, `shimmer`, `features-ticker` keyframes that make the hand-drawn scene idle). Easings: `cubic-bezier(0.4,0,0.2,1)` (Material standard, dominant on hovers) and `cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)` (a long soft expressive ease-out for entrances/reveals), plus bare `ease` / `ease-out` fallbacks. No explicit per-item stagger value is exposed, though grouped entrances are flagged staggered. The full choreography — in-view reveals, in-view category recolor, pinned chrome — is detailed in **Interaction & motion** below. **Reduced motion is NOT gated** (0 `prefers-reduced-motion` blocks) — an accessibility defect to fix on any port.
- **Density:** High. Sidebar icon rail, 16-cell product grid, tab bars, dense chip rows. It stays legible because of (a) one type family/weight, (b) uniform outlines grouping everything, (c) the warm canvas lowering contrast glare, and (d) color reserved for category-coding not emphasis.
- **Hierarchy:** Dominant device is **enclosure + fill-color**, then position — not type scale. The thing you should click is the orange-filled button; the thing that's a category is the colored-icon cell; the rest is outlined-neutral. Size plays a smaller role than on most sites.
- **Restraint:** Despite the maximalist vibe, the discipline is real: one font family, mostly one weight, one accent for action, one border color/weight, near-zero shadow, near-zero gradient, minimal motion. The "anti-minimalist" look is built from a *very* constrained kit applied densely — that's the trick to not becoming slop.

## Interaction & motion

PostHog's motion is **functional, not scroll-spectacle** — the page does NOT scrollytell. Across the capture frames (s00→s10) the OS-shell hero stays put through ~80% of the scroll (s00–s08 are near pixel-identical); the *only* real choreography is **lazy in-view reveal of every section, an in-view recolor of a category-button row, and pinned chrome** (top menu bar + cookie banner). Evidence: **229 IntersectionObservers over 233 targets** (essentially every cell/section is reveal-gated), 41 `@keyframes` but almost all are *ambient illustration idles* (`float`, `breathe`, `wiggle`, `wobble`, `bounce`, `shimmer`, `pulse`, `gradient-rotate`, `hogfather-jump/roll`, `speechBubble`, `features-ticker`) rather than scroll-bound transforms, 12 scroll listeners, 29 mousemove listeners, **1234 rAF calls** (the looping illustrations + sprite animations), 0 canvas, 0 carousels, **14 disclosure + 5 tab groups**, and **0 detected libraries** (hand-rolled IO + CSS). **Reduced motion is NOT respected** (0 `prefers-reduced-motion` blocks) — the one real defect. Stack: `IntersectionObserver` · CSS `@keyframes` · ambient rAF illustration loops.

- **In-view fade/slide reveal (everything)** — *Trigger:* in-view (IO, 233 targets, threshold mostly `[0,0.01]` = fire on first pixel). *Mechanism:* a class flip on intersect drives CSS `opacity`/`transform` (keyframes `slideUpFadeIn`, `fadeIn`, `fade`, `slideUp/Down/In`, `jump-in`) over a `cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)` settle; grouped cells are flagged staggered. *Reads as:* a dense, comic-panel page that "assembles" panel-by-panel as you scroll instead of dumping all at once. *Transfers to:* any tall, information-dense directory/grid page that would feel like a wall if it all appeared at once. *Safety:* **production-safe** (GPU-cheap, once-only) — but here it's *un-gated* for reduced motion, so a port MUST add the `prefers-reduced-motion` resting state. → `scroll-reveal-in-view`.
- **In-view category-row recolor** — *Trigger:* in-view. *Mechanism:* as the "Ship features safely…" section enters, the active category button fills and the previously-active one empties — `Test & roll out changes` background `rgb(182,42,217)`→transparent + text white→grey, while a sibling fills transparent→`rgb(47,128,250)` + grey→white; the section's own banner background crossfades magenta `#b62ad9`→blue `#2f80fa`. *Reads as:* the category-coded UI "lights up" the relevant product as that story scrolls into view — the color system made temporal. *Transfers to:* any category/tab/segmented row where you want the in-view item to self-highlight on entry. *Safety:* **production-safe** (one-shot color/bg transition) **but state must not be hue-only** — pair the fill with a text/weight change or aria-current so colorblind users still read which item is active. → no file yet (`patterns_to_build: in-view-category-recolor-row`); closest existing recipe is `scroll-linked-recolor`.
- **Pinned chrome (menu bar + cookie banner)** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress (native `position: fixed`). *Mechanism:* the faux-OS top menu bar and the bottom-right "Legally-required cookie banner" stay pinned to the viewport while content scrolls beneath (visible in every frame s00–s10). *Reads as:* a persistent desktop-OS frame — the conceit holds because its "window chrome" never scrolls away. *Transfers to:* sticky app/marketing nav, consent bars, persistent toolbars. *Safety:* **production-safe** (no JS, no rAF); just ensure pinned bars don't cover content on short viewports and the cookie banner is keyboard-dismissible/focus-managed. → `sticky-pin-scrollytelling` (pin mechanism; here used for chrome, not scrollytelling).
- **Static desktop backdrop (fixed wallpaper under transparent windows)** — *Trigger:* none — a `position: fixed`, `inset-0`, `-z-10` wallpaper that paints once and never moves (the inverse of the pinned chrome above: fixed *behind* content, not on top). *Mechanism:* the full-viewport tan wallpaper (`#e1d7c2`) sits below everything; content sections are transparent (`transparent_height_pct: 100`), so as the page scrolls the opaque window "sheets" and both icon rails slide while the desktop stays fixed and shows through the gutters around them — pixel-identical across s00→s10. *Reads as:* real OS depth, content windows floating over a stationary desktop, at zero motion cost. *Transfers to:* any page that wants a persistent textured/illustrated ground under scrolling content without parallax math or the iOS-buggy `background-attachment: fixed`. *Safety:* **production-safe** (one painted fixed layer, no JS/rAF, reduced-motion-irrelevant since nothing animates); keep the wallpaper near-monochrome or add a scrim where text sits directly on it. → `fixed-backdrop-reveal`.
- **Micro scroll-scale on hero accents** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* two small elements take a tiny transform tied to scroll — an SVG (`w-[189px]`) scales `1.01→1.13` and the "Get started" CTA breathes `1.035→1.01`. *Reads as:* a barely-there liveliness on the hero; easy to miss. *Transfers to:* a hero accent or badge you want subtly reactive without a real parallax rig. *Safety:* **production-safe** (compositor-only transform, tiny range) — keep the delta small as captured; cap it under reduced motion. → `scroll-scale`.
- **Ambient illustration idle loops** — *Trigger:* load (autonomous rAF/CSS loops, not scroll). *Mechanism:* the hand-drawn hedgehogs and scene elements run slow looping keyframes (`float`, `breathe`, `wiggle`, `wobble`, `bounce`, `heart-pulse`, `speechBubble`, `features-ticker`) on 2–3s durations — responsible for most of the 1234 rAF calls. *Reads as:* the comic world feels alive/inhabited rather than a flat illustration. *Transfers to:* mascot/illustration-led brand pages wanting warmth without scroll spectacle. *Safety:* **spectacle-leaning** — cheap individually but *continuous* (never idles), so it's a battery/main-thread cost on long sessions and is **un-gated for reduced motion here**. Gate behind `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` (freeze to a static frame), and pause off-screen. → no standalone recipe (bespoke to the illustration set; reuse the *principle*: subtle gated idle loops, paused off-screen).
- **Hover micro-states** — *Trigger:* hover. *Mechanism:* primary CTA lifts ~1px (`translateY(-2px)`→`-3px`); outlined buttons reveal a border (`transparent`→`rgb(158,160,150)`/`rgb(182,183,175)`); a numbered chip swaps fill grey→white + text to the warm ink — over `0.15s cubic-bezier(0.4,0,0.2,1)`. *Reads as:* the sticker/comic cells feel pressable. *Transfers to:* all outlined interactive chrome. *Safety:* **production-safe**.

## Production-safety note
- **Faux-OS shell (menu bar + app-icon rail + window chrome):** *production-safe as styling*, but the chrome is decorative — it must not trap focus or hijack real OS affordances. Keep traffic-light dots/menu items non-interactive (or aria-hidden) unless they truly do something; ensure the central content has a normal document reading order and the rail icons are real labeled links or marked decorative. Cost: extra DOM and a strong opinion that won't fit every brand.
- **Heavy outlined-everything on tan:** production-safe; verify the warm-near-black border + warm-grey text clears WCAG AA against the tan canvas (the `#9ea096` muted-on-tan combo is the risky one — check it). The outlines actually *help* low-vision users by defining regions.
- **Hand-drawn illustration set:** production-safe but asset-heavy and brand-bespoke — needs an illustrator; don't fake it with stock or it reads cheap. Provide `alt` or mark decorative.
- **Dense 16-cell icon grid:** production-safe; just give each cell a real accessible label (icon alone isn't enough) and ensure the 4-up collapses cleanly on mobile.
- **A11y audit (captured, honest):** **focus-visible is present** (nav buttons get a warm-grey focus fill; the primary CTA gets a real 1px focus outline) — good. **Defects to fix on any port:** (1) **reduced motion is NOT respected** — 0 `prefers-reduced-motion` blocks despite continuous ambient illustration loops + in-view reveals; ship resting states and gate the loops. (2) **img `alt` ratio ≈ 0.67** — roughly a third of the 21 images lack alt text; the hand-drawn illustrations need `alt` or explicit `aria-hidden`/decorative marking. (3) **Tap targets: 69 of 107 interactive elements are below the 44px minimum** — the dense outlined chips/icon cells are finger-hostile on touch; enlarge hit areas or add padding on mobile. (4) Recolor/category state is **hue-carried** — add a non-color cue (text/weight/`aria-current`). (5) The `#9ea096` muted-grey-on-tan combo is the AA contrast risk to verify.

## Extracted patterns
- os-shell-window-frame — wrap a marketing page in a fake desktop-OS chrome (title/menu bar with window controls + an app-icon side rail) as the layout's organizing conceit; use when a product wants strong, structural personality rather than sprinkled decoration.
- outlined-app-icon-grid — a dense, scannable grid of colored-icon + label cells, each in a uniform heavy outline on a warm canvas; use to show the breadth of a multi-product platform or a feature/integration suite.
- scroll-reveal-in-view — (reused, motion) IntersectionObserver-gated fade/slide that fires once per element; PostHog uses it pervasively (233 targets) so a dense comic-panel page assembles panel-by-panel instead of as a wall. Add the reduced-motion resting state PostHog itself omits.
- scroll-linked-recolor — (reused, motion) closest existing recipe for PostHog's in-view category-row recolor; muted/neutral resolves to a category fill on entry. Pair with a non-color cue.
- tabbed-product-showcase — (reused) tab/category rows swapping a framed product panel (5 tab groups + 14 disclosures captured); use for multi-feature showcases instead of stacked screenshots.
- fixed-backdrop-reveal — (reused) the tan desktop is a fixed `-z-10` wallpaper that never scrolls while transparent sections (`transparent_height_pct: 100`) slide over it — same mechanism as `emergent`; here it completes the faux-OS conceit (windows float over a persistent desktop) and is theme-swappable.
- **patterns_to_build › in-view-category-recolor-row** — NEW technique with no file yet: as a section enters view, an entire segmented/category button row toggles fill↔empty (active button fills its category color + text inverts; the prior active empties), so the in-view item self-highlights. Build as an IO-gated, `prefers-reduced-motion`-aware, `aria-current`-backed recipe before citing in `patterns:`.
