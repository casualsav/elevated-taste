## What works
- **The transaction is the hero, not a link to the transaction.** The fold is a full-bleed portrait with the *entire first step of the donation form* laid over its left third: a monthly/once frequency toggle, a four-cell amount grid with $40 preselected, a free-entry cell, an impact line ("Gives 12 people clean water every year on average"), and one button. Nothing is promised on a later page. The transferable rule: when your primary conversion is a short, well-understood form (donate, subscribe, book, search, get a quote), put the form's *first field* above the fold instead of a CTA that scrolls or navigates to it — the cost of a click is paid before intent has decayed, and the preselected default does the "how much is normal?" work that a blank input pushes onto the visitor. See `patterns/hero-inline-transaction.md`.
- **Every claim is priced in human units before it is priced in dollars.** "$40/mo" is annotated with *people served*; "696 million people lack basic access" is the section headline, not a footnote; the proof band leads with 21,641,908 people served and a "Last updated: June 24, 2026" date. The conversion device is not urgency or scarcity — it is **denomination**: convert the abstract number into the unit the visitor actually cares about, and stamp it with a freshness date. Transfers to any page selling an outcome rather than a feature (impact, savings, hours reclaimed, tickets resolved).
- **A dark band earns its darkness by holding evidence, not atmosphere.** One near-black (#222520) full-bleed section interrupts a warm cream page, and the only thing in it is a live data product: a stat header (people served / projects / countries) sitting on a dark Mapbox world with clustered, tappable project pins. The page's single most-designed component is the one that lets you *audit* the claim. Where most marketing pages spend their dark band on a testimonial or a gradient, spend it on the artifact that proves you. Transfers wherever trust is the bottleneck — nonprofits, security, marketplaces, health.
- **Serif display over sans everything-else, with no medium tier.** Kazimir Text (a warm transitional serif) carries every headline; Proxima Nova carries body, labels, and every button. Body is a large 20px/30px; H1 is 46px/52px — only a ~2.3× jump, far short of the usual display leap — yet hierarchy is unambiguous because the *families* differ and the sans never grows large. Pairing does the ranking that scale would otherwise have to do, which is what lets the page run a big, comfortable reading size without the headlines needing to be enormous. Transfers to any content-heavy page where a 4× display headline would feel shouty.
- **One saturated accent, spent only on the act of giving.** Yellow (#FFCA0A) appears on the logo mark, the announcement bar, the Give button, the selected amount cell, the link underline, and the map's data pins — and essentially nowhere else. Navy (#003366) handles secondary/system buttons, so the yellow never has to compete for "which of these is the action." A single hue reserved for a single verb is stronger brand recognition than a palette, and it makes the CTA findable at any scroll depth without a sticky bar shouting.
- **Photography with room to breathe, no crop-to-card.** Images are large, uncropped-feeling, and captioned by a small white card floating *inside* the frame rather than a caption bar bolted beneath it. Subjects are at ease and making eye contact with an object (a glass, a tap), not the lens. The image is never boxed into a feature-card grid. The rule: when your photography is your credibility, give it width and let the copy overlap it — do not shrink it into a card so it can sit beside two siblings.
- **Restraint about the give ask.** Only three CTAs on the whole page ("Give" in nav, "Join today" in the hero form, "Give today" at the crisis stat), plus a card carousel of ways to help. No interstitial modal, no exit-intent, no scroll-triggered popover, no countdown. The pinned announcement bar carries the one time-bound offer (a matching campaign) at the very top, where it can be ignored.

## Design-system read
- **Type:** Two families, both doing one job each. **Kazimir Text** (serif) for all headings: H1 46/52 desktop → 35px mobile, section H2 ~40px, all at regular weight. **Proxima Nova** (humanist sans) for body 20px/30px, secondary 16px/24px, and micro-labels 12–14px uppercase at 1.4–2.0px tracking (~0.13–0.15em). Weight is nearly unused as a hierarchy device — 1069 elements at 400, 257 at 500, 16 at 700. The site's own scale is a modest ~1.2 ratio (`--heading-sm .9rem → 3xl 2.8rem`). **Hierarchy = family + case + size**, in that order.
- **Color:** Ground is a warm off-white (#FAF7F0) with cream (#F8EED3) as the secondary surface for callouts and the "why water" band; pure white is reserved for elevated cards (donation widget, photo caption, form panel). Ink is a green-black (#222520), never pure black; muted text is a warm grey (#71716C). Accent yellow (#FFCA0A, hover #FFD53B) is the *action* color. Navy (#003366) is the *system* color (nav sign-in, secondary buttons, consent CTA) and #5493D3 the link-accent. Dark band = the same ink used as ground with #FFFFFF text. Semantic color is absent; nothing is red or green.
- **Spacing rhythm:** A strict 4px base with a long linear ramp (`spacing-1: 4px` … `spacing-50: 200px`) plus a named component-padding scale (8 / 16 / 24 / 48 / 60 / 104px). Section padding runs 104px; the page's calm comes from the coarse end of that ramp, not from sparse content.
- **Layout system:** Flexbox only — 207 flex containers, **zero CSS Grid**. Containers step 320 / 400 / 640 / 800 / 960 / 1200px; the content spine is 1200px with an 800px measure for centered prose. Breakpoints cluster at 640/641, 991/992, 1024/1025. The hero is a true split (form over the left of an edge-to-edge photo); everything after it is a centered single column of full-bleed bands. Mobile collapses to one column, drops the hero form to a single "Give today" button, and — notably — **re-sorts the proof band**: the desktop map-first order becomes stat-first (projects → countries → people), because a world map is worthless at 390px.
- **Layering / page architecture:** Effectively a **single flat scroll with pinned chrome**. `has_static_backdrop: false`, `has_blend_overlay: false`, no `backdrop-filter`, no `mix-blend-mode`. The frame sequence shows the header pixel-identical at every scroll stop while content flows under it — a fixed, opaque white bar (the profile's layer roles miss it; the frames don't). The other fixed layers are third-party: consent bar, a bottom-left CTA nudge, Stripe iframes. Depth is *painted*: cards with 2px-blur shadows sitting on photos, the caption card overlapping the image edge. Nothing translucent, nothing blended.
- **Motion / easing intent:** Durations 100/150/200/300/500ms; easings `linear` (101 rules, all 0.15s micro-hovers), `ease-in-out`, `ease-out`, and one signature `cubic-bezier(0.37, 0.01, 0, 0.98)` — a hard-out, long-settle curve — on the nav/menu transitions. 126 transition rules but only 18 animation rules and **zero** entrance reveals. Motion here means *state feedback*, never choreography: the page never animates content into view.
- **Density:** Low-to-medium and deliberately uneven. The marketing bands are one idea each with 800px measures; the proof band and the footer (four link columns + newsletter + currency selector + charity registration numbers) are dense. Density tracks trust: the more you scroll, the more auditable the page becomes.
- **Hierarchy:** Family first (serif = a claim, sans = a fact), then ground color (which band am I in), then size. Color ranks *actions*, never text.
- **Restraint:** No entrance animations, no parallax, no gradient mesh, no glassmorphism, no dark mode, no three-feature-card grid, no fake dashboard mockup, no testimonial slider. Two font families, two shadow values, three radii. The one gradient on the page (cream → white) is a band transition, not decoration.

## Interaction & motion
Evidence: 11 scroll frames, 16 `@keyframes` (of which `mapboxgl-spin`, `swiper-preloader-spin`, `mdc-ripple-*` are vendor), 18 animation rules vs 126 transition rules, **0 IntersectionObservers**, 0 entrance reveals, 1 Swiper carousel, 1 canvas (Mapbox), 22 scroll listeners, `prmRules: 0`. This is a near-static page whose motion budget is spent entirely on interactive state.

- **Condensing pinned nav** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress (a threshold crossing, not a continuous map). *Mechanism:* a fixed opaque header that, past roughly one viewport, drops its secondary link cluster and keeps only the wordmark and the single primary CTA; the yellow announcement bar scrolls away with the page rather than pinning. Frames s00 (six nav links + Sign in + Give) vs s01–s10 (wordmark + Give only) show the discrete change. *Reads as:* the chrome getting out of the way while the one thing you might want stays put. *Transfers to:* any long page with exactly one conversion — it removes the need for a separate sticky CTA bar. *Safety:* **production-safe**; the trap is animating `height`/`width` (reflow) rather than crossfading + `transform`, and losing focus position when links unmount. See `patterns/condensing-scroll-nav.md`.
- **Filter chips that swap a single hero photo + its caption** — *Trigger:* tap/click. *Mechanism:* four pill chips (Health, Education, Women, Economic Growth) overlay the top edge of one large photo; selecting one swaps the image and the white caption card floating inside its lower-right corner. One frame, four stories, no layout shift. *Reads as:* "pick the outcome you care about," rather than a four-card grid asking you to read all of them. *Transfers to:* any section with 3–5 *parallel* claims that would otherwise become a card grid. *Safety:* **production-safe**; must be built as a real tablist (`role="tab"` / `aria-selected`) — here the probe finds no tab semantics and the chips read as links.
- **Drag/swipe card carousel for the secondary asks** — *Trigger:* drag, tap, arrow keys. *Mechanism:* Swiper, one row of photo cards ("Join monthly giving", "Sponsor a water project", "Start a fundraiser", "Give to a fundraiser") with the fifth card deliberately half-cut at the right edge to signal overflow, and a prev/next pair beneath. *Reads as:* an optional menu, subordinate to the hero's single ask. *Transfers to:* secondary CTAs you don't want competing with the primary one. *Safety:* **production-safe**; the arrows here are ~32px hit areas, under the 44px minimum.
- **Live, dated impact counters over an interactive map** — *Trigger:* load. *Mechanism:* server-supplied totals rendered as an abbreviated value plus its full value (the DOM carries both `21.6m` and `21,641,908`), with a `PulsingText`/`LoadingBar` keyframe pair covering the fetch, over a Mapbox canvas with clustered project pins, a country filter and a search field. *Reads as:* a dashboard the org runs, not a graphic the marketing team drew. *Transfers to:* any "trust us" moment that can be backed by queryable data. *Safety:* **production-safe** in kind; the canvas + tile fetches are the page's heaviest cost, so lazy-mount it below the fold (as here) and ship the numbers as text so they survive a failed map.
- **Micro-hover feedback only** — *Trigger:* hover/focus. *Mechanism:* 101 rules of `0.15s linear` on `color`/`border-color`/`background-color`; the Give button lightens #FFCA0A → #FFD53B, nav links lighten their ink, amount cells lighten their grey, links grow a yellow underline. No transforms, no shadows on hover. *Reads as:* responsiveness without theater. *Transfers to:* everywhere — this is the cheapest, most durable hover system there is.

**Reduced-motion: NOT respected** (`prmRules: 0`). In practice the exposure is small — there is nothing to disable but the nav transition, the carousel's slide, and the map's spinner — but the carousel autoplay/drag inertia and the map's easing should still be gated. The patterns below gate them.

## Production-safety note
Everything here is **production-safe** in kind: color transitions, a discrete nav state change, a drag carousel, and a canvas map. Three defects to fix on reuse: (1) no `prefers-reduced-motion` rule anywhere; (2) 56 of 174 interactive elements are under the 44px tap-target minimum — the footer's four link columns and the carousel arrows, while the CTAs are exemplary at 48–56px; (3) `img_alt_ratio` is 0.43 on a page whose entire argument is photographic — for image-led storytelling, alt text is the argument, not an attribute. Focus rings are present and visible.

## Extracted patterns
- `hero-inline-transaction` — the first step of the conversion form (frequency toggle, preset amount grid, impact microcopy, one button) rendered directly over the hero photo instead of a CTA that navigates to it; reuse for donate, subscribe, quote, and booking flows.
- `condensing-scroll-nav` — a fixed header that sheds its secondary links past the first viewport and keeps only the wordmark and the single primary CTA, replacing a separate sticky CTA bar.
