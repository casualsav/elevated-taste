## What works (transferable principles)

- **Make the page a camera over a canvas, not a column of sections.** The whole site is seven fixed-aspect `1200×720` sheets laid out on one horizontal rail; a `position: fixed` stage holds them at viewport center and scroll only moves and scales that stage. The document's own scroll height comes from an empty, `pointer-events: none` "ghost" spacer sized `calc(5208px + 100vw)` in *both* axes — so the native scrollbar, trackpad, arrow keys and mobile fling all still drive the experience, and nothing has to be re-implemented. The transferable rule: **when you want camera motion, don't intercept scroll — give the document real scrollable length and read `scrollX/scrollY` as a camera coordinate.** You keep native inertia, keyboard scroll, and browser find-on-page while getting canvas-like navigation.

- **Chrome that lives on a zooming canvas must be counter-scaled, not re-sized.** Each sheet's caption sits at `scale(1.133)` when the camera is at `0.882` and `scale(1.667)` when the camera reaches `0.6` — exactly `1 / zoom` at every frame — and its offset (`top: -31.7px` at 0.882) is likewise `-28px / zoom`. The label therefore renders at a constant optical 14px and a constant optical 28px gap no matter how far out the camera pulls. Transfers to any zoomable surface — map pins, canvas editors, node graphs, image annotators: **apply `scale(1 / var(--zoom))` and divide offsets by `--zoom` so UI stays UI while the world scales.** → `counter-scaled-chrome`

- **Size display type to the container, then let the container crop it.** The section glyphs are `font-size: 720px` — literally the sheet's height — with `white-space: nowrap`, so "Devouring Details", "Craft" and "Projects" are *guaranteed* to overflow and be clipped mid-letterform. The crop is not an accident to be tuned; it is a consequence of binding type size to the frame. This produces a bold, unmistakably intentional edge at every viewport and requires zero fluid-type math. Transfers to editorial covers, chapter dividers, portfolio index tiles — **bind the display size to a container dimension and clip, instead of picking a number and hoping it fits.**

- **A saturated pure-hue set survives a monochrome page precisely because there are only four of them, each appearing once.** Yellow `#FFFF02`, orange `#FF6100`, `#0000FF`, and a hand-scrawled `#FF484C` each occupy exactly one sheet, on a field of `#EDEDED` ground and `#FFFFFF` sheets with `#171717` text. No shadows, no gradients, no tints. Because the canvas is desaturated and shadowless, a single flat circle of yellow reads as a designed event rather than decoration. Transfers to any portfolio or brand index: **ration one hue per surface; never let two saturated colors share a viewport.**

- **Depth without shadow: use edge, gap, and blend.** The profile records zero box-shadows and zero gradients. Sheets separate from the ground by value contrast alone (white on `#EDEDED`), with a 40px gutter between neighbours. The only "layer" cue is a 20×20 crosshair fixed at viewport center running `mix-blend-mode: difference`, which inverts against whatever sheet passes beneath it. Transfers to flat/Swiss systems: **let value contrast + air do elevation, and reserve blend modes for a single fixed reticle or marker that must remain visible over unknown content.**

- **Give a hijacked interaction a legible instrument.** Because scroll here means "pan the camera," the user loses the scrollbar as a position cue (`::-webkit-scrollbar { display: none }`). It is replaced by a fixed 20-tick minimap where a bordered tracker slides along the ticks in proportion to camera travel, and three of the ticks are real `<a href>` jump links. Transferable rule: **any time you take away a native affordance, you owe the user a replacement that shows both position and reachability** — not just progress. → `index-rail-scroll-progress`

- **Degrade the trick, keep the content.** At `(pointer: coarse)` the ghost spacer collapses to `height: 1px` and the stage flips from `fixed` to `absolute`, turning the whole camera into an ordinary horizontally-scrolling flex row with native momentum and `overscroll-behavior-x: auto`. The desktop conceit is an enhancement layered on a plain rail — that ordering is what makes it shippable, and it's the part most "canvas site" clones skip.

## Design-system read

- **Type:** One family for everything — a custom neo-grotesque (`X`, served as a single `dd.woff2`, Helvetica-class) at weights 400 and 500 only; `Georgia` is aliased to `<em>`/`<i>` and `JetBrains Mono` to code, neither appears on the index. The scale is not a ratio ramp but **three disconnected clusters**: chrome (14/16px), statement (75/85px), and container-derived display (720px = frame height). The ~5.3× jump from 16 → 85px, and then the ~8.5× jump to the clipped glyph, means hierarchy is carried entirely by **scale**; weight never changes. Line height on the statement type is set by the six clipped line boxes, not by a `line-height` token.
- **Color:** Ground `#EDEDED` (Radix `gray4`) → sheet `#FFFFFF` → text `#171717` (`gray12`) → muted/captions `#6F6F6F` (`gray11`) → hairline & minimap ticks `#8F8F8F` (`gray9`). Accents are pure, unmixed hues used once each: `--yellow #FFFF02` (also the `::selection` background and the active minimap tracker fill), `--orange #FF6100`, `#0000FF` for the pixel-cursor glyph, `--colors-red #FF484C` for the handwritten signature. Every accent has a `display-p3` upgrade behind `@supports (color: color(display-p3 1 1 1))` — the sRGB value is the fallback, not the design. A full Radix scale (blue/green/amber/teal/red, light + dark) is loaded but unused on this page.
- **Spacing rhythm:** 8px base, exposed as `--space-1…11` (8→88px). In practice the page uses a coarse subset: 8px (flex gap), 9px (minimap tick gap), 40px (rail gutter, from the 1240px stride on 1200px sheets), 50px (contact + quote sheet padding — the one off-grid value), 64px (main sheet padding, and the indent of every second headline line). Whitespace is not "breathing room" here; it is the gutter between camera subjects.
- **Layout system:** No grid, no container query, no fluid type. A single flex row of absolutely-positioned sheets at `left: n × 1240px`, each `width: 1200px; height: 720px` (5:3). Sheets themselves are `display: flex` with `align-items: center` and one child. The only breakpoint is `@media (pointer: coarse)` — capability, not width. `craft.responsive` confirms `h1` and body stay 32px/16px from 1440px down to 390px: nothing reflows, because the camera scales instead.
- **Layering / page architecture:** Three fixed layers over an empty scroll well. (1) The **ghost** — a transparent `pointer-events:none` div of `5208px + 100vw` × `5208px + 100vh` whose only job is to make the document long; it is the scroll *input device*, invisible. (2) The **stage** (`position: fixed`, centered by `translate(-50%,-50%)`, covering 83%×80% of the viewport) carrying all seven sheets; scroll writes `translate` + `scale` onto it. (3) Two pieces of **instrument chrome**, both fixed: the minimap (top center) and a 20×20 crosshair at exact viewport center running `mix-blend-mode: difference`. `has_static_backdrop: false`, `has_blend_overlay: false` — nothing is revealed *through* anything. The z-story is not parallax depth; it is **world vs. camera vs. instruments**, which is the OS/canvas model, not the document model.
- **Motion / easing intent:** Two durations only — `200ms ease-in-out` (minimap tracker/tick state) and `350ms ease-in-out` with an `800ms` delay (sheet entrance). A `--transitions-snappy: cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1)` token exists but is unused on the index. Everything else is not a transition at all: it's a per-frame `transform` written from scroll position (547 `requestAnimationFrame` calls across one scroll pass, 4 scroll listeners, 2 IntersectionObservers over 16 targets, **0 libraries — hand-rolled**). Intent: *the page is a physical surface you move a camera across*; discrete animation is reserved for arrival (load) and instrumentation (the tracker).
- **Density:** Deliberately near-zero. Each sheet holds one object — a sentence, a glyph, a cursor, a scrawl, four links. Density is expressed as *rail length*, not as elements per screen. This is the opposite of a dashboard and it's why the 85px type never feels shouty: there is nothing for it to compete with.
- **Hierarchy:** Scale, then position. Within a sheet, one element is enormous and everything else is 14px. Across the rail, order is authored (identity → work → play → contact → manifesto) and the camera enforces it — you cannot skip a sheet without passing it.
- **Restraint:** No shadows (`elevation.shadows: []`), no gradients (`elevation.gradients: []`), no hover effects at all (`hoverEffects: 0`, `cursorFollowers: 0`), no dark mode on the index despite a full dark token set being loaded, no imagery (`img_count: 0` — every visual is text, SVG, or a `border-radius: 9999px` div), no second typeface, no bold weight. The circles are `aspect-ratio: 1/1` + `radius: full` on a div. The whole page ships zero raster assets.
- **A11y (honest, incl. defects):** **`focus-visible` is genuinely good** — a 4px `#8F8F8F` ring with 4px offset, and crucially `:focus-visible` on the frame link draws the ring on the *inner sheet*, so the ring follows the visual object rather than the hit box; the minimap trackers get their own 2px ring. Every sheet link carries an `aria-label`, decorative glyphs are `aria-hidden`, the copy-email button exposes an `aria-live` region. **Defects:** (1) `prefers-reduced-motion` is never queried (`prmRules: 0`) — the camera zoom, the largest motion on the page, runs regardless; a vestibular user gets the full pull-back. (2) `<meta name="viewport" … maximum-scale=1.0, user-scalable=no>` blocks pinch-zoom outright. (3) 3 of 13 interactive elements are under 44px (the 30×18px minimap trackers). (4) `overscroll-behavior: none` + a hidden scrollbar removes both the escape hatch and the position cue on desktop; only the minimap replaces them.

## Interaction & motion

The page has essentially **one continuous effect and one entrance**, both hand-rolled: no GSAP, no Lenis, no Framer (`libraries: []`, `keyframeCount: 3` and one of those three belongs to an unrelated demo). Evidence: 547 rAF calls in a single scroll pass, 4 scroll listeners, 8 mousemove listeners, 2 IntersectionObservers (`rootMargin: 200px`, 16 targets — sheet culling, not reveal), and a `scroll_effects` diff showing the stage scaling `0.882 → 0.6` while all six captions scale `1.133 → 1.667` in lockstep. The frames confirm it: from `s00` to `s04` the sheets get smaller and more of the rail is visible, while the captions stay the same pixel size. **Reduced motion is not respected.**

- **Scroll-as-camera (pan + zoom over a ghost spacer)** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* an empty oversized div supplies document scroll length in both axes; a `position: fixed` stage reads that scroll offset each frame and writes a single `transform: translate(px, px) scale(z)`. Scroll distance maps to camera travel; scroll depth maps to `z` (`0.882 → 0.6` — the camera pulls **back**, revealing more rail as you go). *Reads as:* moving through a physical exhibition rather than scrolling a document; the pull-back turns "further down the page" into "seeing more of the whole." *Transfers to:* portfolio indexes, product tours, presentation decks, any set of peer objects with no natural vertical order. *Safety:* **spectacle-only** — one composited transform is cheap, but scroll direction is remapped, the scrollbar is hidden, `overscroll-behavior: none` traps the gesture, and a scroll-linked zoom is a textbook vestibular trigger. Ship it behind `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference` and `(pointer: fine)`, over a real horizontal rail. → `scroll-camera-rail`
- **Counter-scaled caption chrome** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress (derived from the camera's `--zoom`). *Mechanism:* every caption gets `scale(1 / z)` and `top: -28px / z`; measured pairs `(z 0.882, s 1.133)` and `(z 0.6, s 1.667)` are exactly reciprocal. *Reads as:* the labels belong to the *viewer*, not the world — an instantly legible signal of what is content and what is UI. *Transfers to:* maps, canvas editors, zoomable diagrams, image annotation, any pinch-zoom surface. *Safety:* **production-safe** — pure CSS off one custom property, no extra reads, no reflow (`scale` only). → `counter-scaled-chrome`
- **Staggered sheet arrival** — *Trigger:* load. *Mechanism:* the main sheet's six headline lines each sit in an `overflow: hidden` box with an inner `<span>` that rises from below the clip edge; the remaining six sheets hold at `opacity: 0` until an `0.35s ease-in-out 0.8s forwards` keyframe fades them in as a group. So the identity statement composes itself first, *then* the world it lives in appears. *Reads as:* "read this, now here's everything else" — sequencing as editing. *Transfers to:* any hero whose primary message competes with a rich background. *Safety:* **production-safe** if gated; note the site itself ships an escape hatch (`[data-cancel-animation="true"]` zeroes the delay) but never wires it to reduced-motion. → `clip-reveal`
- **Minimap tracker as position readout** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* 20 fixed 1×18px ticks; a 30px bordered tracker `translateX`es in proportion to camera position and fills `--yellow` when it lands on a sheet; ticks fade `opacity 0/1` to show which region of the rail is on screen. Three trackers are real `<a>` jump links. *Reads as:* the replacement scrollbar — and, because the ticks light up, a map of where the sheets are. *Transfers to:* long horizontal scrollers, scroll-hijacked pages, image sequences. *Safety:* **production-safe** (transform + opacity only); enlarge the 30×18px hit target and drive it from IntersectionObserver rather than a scroll listener. → `index-rail-scroll-progress`
- **Difference-blend center reticle** — *Trigger:* none (static, fixed). *Mechanism:* a 20×20 white crosshair fixed at viewport center with `mix-blend-mode: difference`, so it inverts against whatever sheet passes under it and is legible on white, yellow, orange and black alike. *Reads as:* a camera viewfinder — it names the interaction. *Transfers to:* any fixed marker (cursor, crop guide, center mark, drag handle) that must sit over unknown content without a halo or shadow. *Safety:* **production-safe**; `mix-blend-mode` creates a stacking context and forces compositing on the layer beneath — keep the element tiny (this one is 20px) and never blend a full-viewport layer.

## Production-safety note

- **The camera itself is the one spectacle-only technique.** Cost: remapped scroll axis, hidden scrollbar, `overscroll-behavior: none`, `user-scalable=no`, and a zoom driven by scroll — the combination is a motion-sickness risk *and* a zoom-accessibility failure. The site's own mobile path (`(pointer: coarse)` → 1px ghost, `position: absolute` stage, native horizontal scroll) is the correct production fallback; wire that same fallback to `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, keep the scrollbar, and drop `user-scalable=no`.
- **Counter-scaled chrome, the difference reticle, and the minimap are all production-safe** and are the parts worth stealing for real product UI — they are how you make a zoomable surface legible.
- **Container-derived display type (`font-size: <container height>` + `nowrap` + clip)** is production-safe and free of fluid-type math, but demands `aria-hidden` on the glyph plus a real accessible name on the link (this site does exactly that) — otherwise a screen reader announces a cropped word.
- **Zero raster assets** (`img_count: 0`, no shadows, no gradients) is why a seven-screen canvas site loads instantly. The perf budget is spent on one webfont and one `transform` per frame.
- **Do not copy** the missing reduced-motion query, the pinch-zoom lock, or the 30×18px minimap hit targets.

## Extracted patterns

- **scroll-camera-rail** — a ghost spacer supplies real scroll length; a fixed stage of fixed-aspect frames reads it as a pan+zoom camera, over a native horizontal rail fallback. Reuse for portfolio indexes and peer-object galleries. *(motion, spectacle-only)*
- **counter-scaled-chrome** — labels/handles on a zooming canvas get `scale(1 / var(--zoom))` and offsets divided by `--zoom`, holding constant optical size. Reuse on maps, canvas editors, node graphs. *(motion, production-safe)*
- **clip-reveal** *(existing, house)* — the six masked headline lines that compose the identity sheet before the rest of the rail fades in.
- **index-rail-scroll-progress** *(existing, from stripepress)* — the tick minimap; Rauno's is the horizontal, scrollbar-replacing case of it.
