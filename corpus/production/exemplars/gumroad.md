## What works
- **Elevation is displacement, not light.** There is not one blurred shadow in the entire stylesheet. Every raised object is a 1px black outline plus a solid, zero-blur offset (`.25rem .25rem 0`, `.5rem .5rem 0`, `4px 4px 0 #000`). Depth reads as a second copy of the object shifted along a diagonal — the way a die-cut sticker sits on paper — instead of as a light source that has to be modelled consistently across a hundred components. The payoff is compositional as much as stylistic: a shadow ramp forces you to invent `sm/md/lg` elevation semantics and then defend them; an offset outline has exactly two states (down, lifted) and can never look muddy against a colored ground. Transfers to any brand that wants tactility without a shadow system.
- **The accent hue is the *hover* state, not the resting state.** At rest the primary CTA is black on warm paper; the brand pink (`#ff90e8`) appears only when you touch something — the CTA fills pink, the secondary Log-in button fills pink, checkmark bullets are pink. Most pages spend the brand color on the resting button and then have nowhere left to go, so hover becomes a 6% darken nobody sees. Reserving the hue for interaction makes the whole page quieter *and* makes every affordance unmissable. Transfers directly; it costs nothing to adopt.
- **Interaction moves things; it does not merely recolor them.** The primary CTA is three stacked planes — a yellow rectangle, a red rectangle, a black face — coincident at rest so it reads as one plain bordered button. On hover the face translates `-8px,-8px` and the red plane `+8px,+8px` over 150ms, and the sticker *opens* into three visible layers. Nothing about the resting layout changes (all three are `position:absolute; inset:0`), so there is zero reflow and zero risk of the button being wrong when JS or motion is off. See `patterns/offset-stack-button.md`. Transfers to any single hero CTA that wants to be the loudest thing on the page without being the biggest.
- **The hero refuses the product screenshot.** No fake dashboard, no laptop mockup, no gradient glow. A centered type stack (`Go from 0 to $1`, 96px, leading 1.0) floats over five decorative coins stamped with the logo mark, drifting at 2–5% mouse intensity. The only product surface in the fold is a *real* marketplace search field that actually submits to `/discover`. When the product has no photogenic screen — a marketplace, an API, a tool — put the working affordance in the fold and let brand objects do the atmosphere. It is both more honest and cheaper than commissioning a UI render.
- **Asymmetric basis rows, not a three-equal-card grid.** Feature cards run in 2-up flex rows with `basis-2/3` + `basis-1/3`, and the ratio *inverts* on the next row (1/3 + 2/3). Cards hold a fixed height (30rem) and the illustrations are absolutely positioned to bleed past the border (`-top-16`, `-right-2`, `bottom-0 left-0`) inside an unclipped card. The result keeps a grid's alignment discipline while destroying its monotony — and the bleed makes each card feel like a window onto something bigger rather than a box containing a PNG. The generic template's failure mode (three identical cards, three identical icons) is structurally impossible here.
- **A three-value ground system does the work a shadow would.** Page is warm paper (`#f4f4f0`), surfaces are pure white, rules are pure black. Because the ground is *off*-white, a white card reads as lifted with no shadow at all — the contrast is already there. That is what frees the shadow budget to be spent entirely on interaction. Reverse the choice (white page, white cards) and you are forced into borders or shadows to separate anything.
- **Uniform tracking, not a proportional ramp — and it's defensible here.** Every one of 615 text nodes carries `letter-spacing: -0.4px`, from 14px captions to the 96px display. That is the inverse of the usual size-proportional ramp: at 96px, −0.4px is ≈ −0.004em (invisible); at 16px it is ≈ −0.025em (a real body-copy tightening). So what looks like a global rule is in practice *a body-text tweak that evaporates at display size*. It works because ABC Favorit is a geometric grotesque whose display sizes need no optical tightening. The transferable lesson is not "use −0.4px" — it is: check whether your display sizes actually need tracking before you build a ramp for them; the tightening you feel you need is usually only wanted at body size.

## Design-system read
- **Type:** ABC Favorit as a solo family (geometric grotesque, self-hosted woff2, four faces: 400/700 × roman/italic) with `font-feature-settings: "ss11","ss04"` on `body` — the stylistic sets that give the single-story `a` and the cut `G` you see in the wordmark. Scale in px: 14 · 16 · 18 · 20 · 24 · 36 · 48 · 72 · 96, roughly a 1.2–1.25 ratio through the UI sizes with a **6× jump from body (16) to display (96)**. Display leading is 1.0 (`leading-none`); body is 16/26 (1.625). Hierarchy is created by **scale alone** — 507 of 629 text nodes are weight 400, and the "medium" class (500) resolves back to the 400 face anyway since no 500 file is served. Mobile display compresses 96→60px (1.6×) while body stays 16px.
- **Color:** Warm paper `#f4f4f0` grounds the page; `#ffffff` is the only surface; `#000000` is the only rule color; ink for prose is a soft near-black `#242423` (never pure), and secondary prose is that ink at 50% alpha. Accent pink `#ff90e8` is interaction-only. A five-hue "toy box" (`#f1f333` yellow, `#ffc900` orange, `#dc341e` red, `#23a094` green, `#90a8ed` periwinkle) exists in the token layer and is spent almost exclusively on the CTA's hidden understack and on illustration fills — never on text ranking. Semantic colors are aliased to the same toy box (`--warning: 255 201 0` is literally the orange), which is a signal that this is a brand palette wearing a system's clothes.
- **Spacing rhythm:** 4px base, but the *used* rhythm is coarse and gappy: gaps at 12/16/32/64px, card padding 32px, section padding 96/128/160/224/256px. Content lives in a 1152px (`max-w-6xl`) wrapper, prose in 768px. The luxury is vertical: `py-64` (256px) around the marquee section. Whitespace is what keeps a page carrying eleven sections from reading as a scroll marathon.
- **Layout system:** Flexbox-dominant. CSS Grid appears exactly twice (the header wrapper, and `grid-cols-1 lg:grid-cols-2` for the four testimonials); every other 2-up is `flex-col lg:flex-row` with asymmetric `basis`. Breakpoints 640 / 768 / 1024 / 1280 / 1536, and everything collapses to one column below 1024. Notably the collapse also reorders: illustrations carry `order-2 md:order-0` so on mobile the *words* always precede the picture.
- **Layering / page architecture:** **A single flat scroll with one pinned rail.** `architecture.layers_summary` reports `has_static_backdrop: false`, `has_blend_overlay: false`; the `blend_glass` census is empty — no `mix-blend-mode`, no `backdrop-filter`, nowhere. The only fixed layers are chrome: an opaque white sticky header (z 50, 100%×9vh, hard black bottom rule) and the mobile menu drawer beneath it. Within the hero, five coins sit at `z-1` behind a `z-10` type stack in a `pointer-events:none` overlay — depth is *painted* (overlapping absolute art, offset shadows) rather than *stacked* (translucent planes over a fixed world). Worth naming as a counterexample: a page can feel physical without a single glass or blend layer.
- **Motion / easing intent:** One curve for everything interactive — `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1)` — and one duration, 150ms (114 of 134 transition declarations; `--transition-duration: .14s` is the token). Ambient motion runs on `linear` at 20s/60s (marquees) and is a different register entirely. The intent is *acknowledgement*: hover confirms you touched a thing, in under two frames of perceived lag, and then stops.
- **Density:** Low text density, high object density. One `h1`, four `h2`, ~130 images (mostly SVG icons in the marquee), 146 interactive elements — 80 of which are the duplicated marquee tag pills.
- **Hierarchy:** Scale first, position second (centered = important), color never. Ranking text by color is simply not in the vocabulary; the only tonal step in prose is ink → ink/50%.
- **Restraint:** No blurred shadow. No gradient. No glassmorphism. No blend mode. No fake dashboard. No three-equal-feature-card grid. No second typeface. No entrance animations at all (`entranceReveals: 0`) — nothing fades up as you scroll, which is why the page feels fast rather than staged.

## Interaction & motion
**Evidence caveat, stated plainly:** the 11 captured scroll frames are pixel-identical to `s00` — the scroll capture never advanced, so it shows nothing. Everything below is grounded in the stylesheet (`@keyframes parallax-scroll, marquee, marquee-scroll, spin, pulse`; 3 `prefers-reduced-motion` rule blocks), the inline hero script, and the probe counts (1 IntersectionObserver / 1 target, 2 scroll listeners, 1 mousemove listener, 1776 rAF calls, 0 animation libraries). Correspondingly, the probe's three `transform/parallax @ scroll-progress` effects are **misclassified**: the elements are the tag-marquee rows (`"blender meditation comic notion template…"`), whose transform differs between scroll stops because they are on a *time-based* `linear infinite` loop, not because scroll drives them. Treated as time-based below.

- **Offset-stack sticker CTA** — *Trigger:* hover / focus-within. *Mechanism:* three coincident absolutely-positioned planes (`inset: 0`); on hover the top face translates `-8px,-8px` and the plane beneath it `+8px,+8px`, both `transform` only, 150ms `cubic-bezier(0.4,0,0.2,1)`; the face simultaneously swaps black→pink. *Reads as:* a physical sticker being peeled off the page — the button was always three things, you just couldn't see it. *Transfers to:* the single hero CTA on any brand-forward page; also to cards. *Safety:* **production-safe** — transform + background only, no reflow, resting state complete. See `patterns/offset-stack-button.md`.
- **Pause-on-hover infinite tag marquee** — *Trigger:* load (ambient). *Mechanism:* a `w-[200%]` flex track holds the tag list twice, translated `0 → -50%` on a `linear infinite` loop (20s mobile, 60s ≥768px); `group-hover:[animation-play-state:paused]` freezes it so the links can be clicked. Crucially the animation classes are `motion-safe:` prefixed — i.e. the marquee is authored **off by default** and switched on only under `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`, with `motion-reduce:animate-none` as a belt-and-braces stop. *Reads as:* a marketplace with more inventory than fits — motion as a *quantity* claim. *Transfers to:* logo walls, category browsers, "works with" strips. *Safety:* **production-safe**, with two costs the source pays: perpetual compositor work beside body copy, and 80 duplicated `<img alt=" icon">` that a screen reader announces 80 times. See `patterns/pause-on-hover-tag-marquee.md` (clone is `aria-hidden`, pause also on `:focus-within`).
- **Dual-input lerped hero parallax** — *Trigger:* mousemove + scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* five decorative coins each carry `data-parallax-intensity` (±0.02–0.05) and `data-scroll-intensity` (−0.15…−0.20); a single rAF loop lerps each coin's current offset toward its target at factor 0.1 and writes one `translate3d()` per coin per frame. The scroll handler is throttled; the mouse target is not smoothed at source but at destination. *Reads as:* the page has air in it — the coins lag your cursor the way objects lag behind glass. *Transfers to:* any hero whose atmosphere is made of discrete brand objects rather than a photograph. *Safety:* **production-safe in kind** (transform-only, `pointer-events:none`, coins are `alt`-labelled decoration, and the init function returns early on `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` before touching the DOM). The cost is that the rAF loop runs unconditionally once started — 1776 calls observed, never cancelled when the hero scrolls out of view. Gate it on an IntersectionObserver.
- **Hover lift with a hard shadow appearing under it** — *Trigger:* hover. *Mechanism:* the tag pills translate `-4px,-4px` and simultaneously grow `box-shadow: 4px 4px 0 0 #000`, so the shadow is the *hole the pill just left*. Both properties are `motion-safe:` gated. *Reads as:* the object detached from the page. *Transfers to:* any pill, chip, or card in an offset-shadow system; it is the same idea as the CTA at 1/2 scale.
- **Nav-link border reveal** — *Trigger:* hover. *Mechanism:* nav items own a transparent border at rest and transition `border-color` transparent→black over 150ms. Zero layout shift because the border is always occupying its 1px. *Reads as:* the underline was there all along. *Transfers to:* any nav; it is strictly better than transitioning a pseudo-element's width.
- **Autoplaying Lottie mascot badge** — *Trigger:* load. *Mechanism:* `<lottie-player autoplay loop>` on a JSON mascot inside an orange circular badge (the page's only third-party motion runtime, `@lottiefiles/lottie-player@2.0.12`). *Safety:* the weak link. It loops forever with **no reduced-motion gate** and no in-view gate, and it drags a whole player library onto the critical path for one decorative circle. Reuse an inline SVG `<animate>` or a static frame under `reduce`.

**Reduced motion: respected** (`respectsReducedMotion: true`, 3 `prefers-reduced-motion` rule blocks). The interesting technique is the *polarity*: the marquees and the hover-lifts are authored inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`, so the still page is the default and motion is the enhancement, rather than the reverse. The one gap is the Lottie badge.

## Production-safety note
Every technique here is transform/opacity/border-color — nothing animates layout, nothing runs a scroll-linked layout read. Four defects to fix on reuse: (1) the hero rAF loop never stops, even when the hero is off-screen; (2) the Lottie mascot autoplays and loops regardless of motion preference; (3) 19 of 146 interactive elements fall under the 44px tap-target minimum (footer link columns, the GitHub star chip) while the hero CTA is exemplary at 56–64px tall; (4) `img_alt_ratio` reads 1.0 but 80 of the 129 images are marquee icons sharing `alt=" icon"` — coverage without meaning. Focus rings are real (`:focus-visible` outline 2px, offset 2px, in the pink accent) though several controls opt into `focus:outline-hidden` + a ring instead. Dark mode is a genuine `prefers-color-scheme` re-theme (`#242423` ground, black surfaces), not an inverted filter.

## Extracted patterns
- `offset-stack-button` — a CTA built from three coincident planes that separate into a sticker on hover; reuse as the single loudest affordance on a brand-forward page, in any system where elevation is an offset rather than a blur.
- `pause-on-hover-tag-marquee` — an ambient infinite marquee of real, clickable category links that freezes on hover *and* keyboard focus, authored motion-off-by-default; reuse for logo walls, category browsers, and any "more than fits" inventory claim.
