## What works
- **The page has exactly three grounds — white, cool-grey, black — and a section declares which one it is.** Every module wrapper sets two custom properties (`--module-background`, `--module-text-color`) from a theme class; every descendant reads them via `var()`. Because the theme is a *token pair* rather than a stack of `.dark .thing { … }` overrides, a section is retheme-able by swapping one class, and any component dropped into it inherits correctly with zero per-component dark styling. This is the single most reusable idea on the page and it costs about six lines of CSS. See `patterns/section-theme-tokens.md`.
- **Persistent chrome reads the ground it is floating over and inverts.** One fixed "Join Now" pill lives at bottom-center for the whole 10,868px scroll. Over black sections it is white-on-black; over white sections it is accent-blue-on-white. The mechanism is an attribute (`data-inview-theme`) flipped by an IntersectionObserver whose `rootMargin: -80% 0% -10% 0%` collapses the viewport to a thin band *exactly where the pill sits* — so the CTA styles itself from whatever is actually behind it, not from scroll position. Transfers to any fixed CTA, toolbar, or player bar on a page with alternating grounds; it replaces the usual "add a scrim so the button survives every background."
- **Display type is huge, light, and negatively leaded — hierarchy is pure scale.** The h1 is 120px at weight 400 with a 96px line-height (leading 0.8) and −0.03em tracking. Body is 16/24 at the same weight. That is a 7.5× size jump with *no weight change*: 1,361 of 1,634 text nodes are weight 400. Tight leading is what makes a two-line 120px headline read as one object instead of two stacked lines. Transfers anywhere you want authority without bolding: buy the contrast with size and leading, not weight.
- **Tracking is a function of size, and it crosses zero.** −0.03em on the 120px display, −0.03em at 16px body, but **+0.10em uppercase** on the 12–14px buttons and eyebrows. Big type closes up, small type opens up. Sites that apply one tracking value to everything look either loose at display size or cramped at label size; the ramp is what makes both tiers feel drawn rather than defaulted.
- **The hero is the only spectacle, and it is a scrim, not a story.** A 100lvh sticky wrapper holds the headline; a JS-written `--scroll-progress` (0–100) drives one `translateY` and one overlay `opacity` via `calc()`. The headline drifts up and dims behind a darkening scrim while the next section slides over it. Everything after the hero is a flat, boring, fast scroll. Spending the whole motion budget in the first viewport and none of it on the twelve content sections is a defensible allocation — the sections are where people read.
- **Proof is faces and numbers, never a fake dashboard.** The credibility section is a 12-tile masonry of real athletes (Ronaldo, Mahomes, Sha'Carri) intercut with plain-text member quotes on grey tiles, at the same size and radius as the photos. Quote tiles being *peers* of photo tiles — not smaller, not bordered differently — is what stops the section from reading as a celebrity wall with testimonials bolted on. The only product UI on the page is a literal screenshot of the real app inside a real phone.
- **One accent, used only for actions.** Accent blue (#4a53ff) appears on the announcement bar, the header button, and the CTA pill over light grounds — nowhere else. No hero gradient, no tinted headings, no colored section. Semantic color (a single mint #00f19f) appears once, as the hover outline on the recommended membership card. When the accent means "click," a page can be almost monochrome and still feel branded.

## Design-system read
- **Type:** Proxima Nova, solo (five widths loaded from Typekit; only the base is used on this page). Sizes are fluid `clamp()` throughout — e.g. a display step of `clamp(2.5rem, 1.18rem + 5.63vw, 6.25rem)` — so there are ramps, not steps. Anchors: display 120px/96px (leading 0.8, tracking −0.03em), h2 ~100px, section head 32px, lead 20/30, body 16/24 (leading 1.5), eyebrow & button 12–14px uppercase at +0.10em. Weights: 400 (body *and* display), 600 (buttons, labels), 700 (rare). Hierarchy is created by **scale and leading**; weight and color are held in reserve.
- **Color:** Three grounds — white `#ffffff`, cool grey `#f3f5f9`, black `#000000` — declared per section as `--module-background`. Ink flips to `#ffffff` on black; nested dark surfaces sit at `#191919`. Muted greys form a real ramp (`#666` body-secondary → `#939393` on-dark-secondary → `#e5e5e5` hairline). Accent `#4a53ff` is action-only, with `#ffffff` as its foreground. One semantic hue: mint `#00f19f` (recommended-plan outline; a 15%-alpha tint as its badge fill). No gradients on the page except a `rgba(0,0,0,0.2→0.6)` scrim over hero video and a few soft radial washes behind pricing cards.
- **Spacing rhythm:** 8px base, coarsely used — gaps cluster at 8/16/24/30px inside components and 50–100px between them. Content sits in a fluid container `min(12.93px + 85.35vw, 77.625rem)` (→ 1242px cap) inside a 1440px shell; heroes and image rows break full-bleed. Section padding does most of the separating; there are no divider rules anywhere.
- **Layout system:** Flex-dominant (279 flex vs 21 grid). Two grid idioms only: a 12-column `repeat(12, 1fr)` for editorial headline/copy rows, and `repeat(auto-fit, minmax(min(20.9375rem, 100%), 1fr))` for card decks — one intrinsic rule that yields 3-up at desktop and 1-up at mobile with no breakpoint. 16 declared breakpoints, but the load-bearing ones are 768 and 1024. Mobile keeps the full 43-link nav (no hamburger collapse) and compresses the display from 120→50px (2.4×) while body stays 16px.
- **Layering / page architecture:** No static backdrop, no blend overlay (`has_static_backdrop: false`, `has_blend_overlay: false`). Depth exists only in the hero: two `position: sticky` layers (a black 100vh wrapper at z0, a parallax content container at z200) that the following section scrolls over. Above them, three pieces of fixed chrome at deliberate z-tiers — header (300), floating CTA (700), a pointer-events-none header overlay (800). Sections themselves are opaque and self-grounding, which is exactly why the theme-token system works: nothing shows through, so a section's class fully determines its appearance. `backdrop-filter` appears three times, on small pill badges, never on a full surface.
- **Motion / easing intent:** 150/200/250/300/500/600/800ms with `ease`, `ease-in-out`, and `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1)`. 120 `@keyframes` — but ~60 of them are the *same* build-generated `fade-in` (`0% {opacity:0} → 100% {opacity:1}`), one hashed copy per CSS module. Real motion vocabulary: fade, slide-up, accordion height, and the hero's scroll-progress translate. Nothing rotates, scales, or blurs. Motion says "this arrived," never "look at me."
- **Density:** Low. 13 sections over ~11,000px; one idea and one CTA per section. The dense artifacts (a 45.8 strain ring, a Healthspan graph, a 12-tile athlete masonry) are *contained* — inside a phone frame or inside a card — so the page around them stays quiet.
- **Hierarchy:** Scale first, then ground color (which chapter am I in), then position. Color never ranks text; weight barely does.
- **Restraint:** No dark mode toggle (`supportsDark: false` — the black sections are content, not a theme), no fake dashboard mockup, no three-feature-card grid, no glassmorphism, no cursor follower, no scroll-jacked chapters, no second typeface. Seven distinct box-shadows on the entire page, all of them `0 4px 15–20px` in a near-transparent grey.

## Interaction & motion
Evidence: 11 scroll frames · 6 IntersectionObservers over 118 targets · 25 scroll listeners · 260 rAF calls · 120 `@keyframes` (60 of them duplicate `fade-in`) · 14 `prefers-reduced-motion` rules · **zero** detected animation libraries — all hand-rolled. No `animation-timeline` / `view()` anywhere; scroll-linked effects are the pre-native custom-property form.

- **Theme-sensing fixed CTA** — *Trigger:* in-view (viewport band). *Mechanism:* an IntersectionObserver with `rootMargin: -80% 0% -10% 0%` reduces the observation area to the 10% strip of viewport the fixed pill occupies; whichever section intersects that strip writes `data-inview-theme="dark|light"` on a root element, and a plain attribute selector restyles the pill (`[data-inview-theme="dark"] .cta > a { background:#fff; color:#000 }`). Frames s00 (white pill on black hero) → s01–s02 (blue pill on white) → s08 (white pill on black) confirm the flip. *Reads as:* the button belongs to whatever you are currently reading. *Transfers to:* fixed CTAs, audio players, toolbars, cookie bars — anything that outlives one section. *Safety:* **production-safe** — one attribute write per section boundary, no scroll handler, no scrim needed. See `patterns/inview-theme-adaptive-sticky-cta.md`.
- **Enter/exit slide of the persistent CTA** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* two mirrored keyframes (`opacity 0→1` with `translate(-50%, 100px) → translate(-50%)`, 500ms `ease`) toggled by a `--hidden` class, plus `transition: bottom 0.6s ease-in-out` so the pill lifts above the cookie banner and the footer rather than colliding with them. *Reads as:* the offer stepping aside, not disappearing. *Transfers to:* any persistent bar that must yield to other fixed chrome. *Safety:* **production-safe** (transform + opacity); note the pill covers a 12%×7% patch of viewport at the exact spot a thumb rests on mobile — check it never sits on top of an in-page control.
- **Custom-property scroll-progress hero** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* a `position: sticky; top: 0` wrapper (z0) holds a 100lvh parallax container (z200); JS writes `--scroll-progress: 0…100` on the section, and CSS consumes it — `transform: translateY(calc(var(--main-content-move-distance) / 100 * var(--scroll-progress) * -1))` on the headline, `opacity: calc(var(--scroll-progress) / 100)` on a darkening `::after` scrim. One custom property, N consumers: the JS never touches an element's style beyond that single variable. *Reads as:* the headline sinking under the page as content arrives. *Transfers to:* any single-hero pin. *Safety:* **production-safe in kind, over-built in fact** — the effect is transform+opacity only, but 25 scroll listeners and 260 rAF calls back it. Prefer `animation-timeline: view()` and keep the custom-property form as the fallback.
- **Fade-rise on section entry, jump-cut for reduced motion** — *Trigger:* in-view. *Mechanism:* an `opacity 0→1` keyframe (thresholds 0 / 0.5 / 0.7) applied per module. Frame s06 catches "Built to be worn 24/7" mid-fade at ~50% opacity. Notable inversion: the base rule names the animation but sets **no duration**; the duration and easing are added only inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`. Reduced-motion users therefore land on the end state instantly, by construction, with no `!important` override to forget. *Reads as:* content settling in. *Transfers to:* everywhere — this is the correct default authoring order for entrance animation.
- **Grey-out disclosure and card hover** — *Trigger:* hover/tap. *Mechanism:* accordions animate `height` via paired `accordion-up`/`accordion-down` keyframes (17 disclosure elements); membership cards gain a `0 4px 20px rgba(0,0,0,0.2)` shadow plus a 2px mint `outline` with `outline-offset: -1px` on hover — an *inset* outline, so the card never grows and the grid never reflows. Nav links go `#b6b6b6 → #ffffff` in 200ms. *Reads as:* the whole page confirming your pointer at one consistent speed. *Safety:* **production-safe**; animating `height` reflows — prefer `grid-template-rows: 0fr → 1fr`.

**Reduced motion: respected** (14 `prefers-reduced-motion` rules; `respectsReducedMotion: true`), via the no-preference-gates-the-duration idiom above. The hero's `--scroll-progress` parallax, however, is *not* covered by those rules — it is driven from JS and runs regardless. Reuse must gate it.

## Production-safety note
Everything here is **production-safe** in kind: opacity, transform, one attribute write, one custom property. Three defects to fix on reuse: (1) the hero parallax ignores `prefers-reduced-motion` because it lives in JS, not CSS — gate the listener, not just the keyframes; (2) 63 of 150 interactive elements fall under the 44px tap-target minimum (footer columns, carousel `+` buttons, the athlete-tile expanders), while the primary pills are exemplary; (3) the fixed CTA occupies bottom-center on mobile — the highest-value real estate for both the thumb and any in-page control that lands there. Focus rings are present (1px solid) and alt coverage is 100% across 51 images.

## Extracted patterns
- `section-theme-tokens` — a section wrapper that declares its ground as two custom properties, so any component inside it themes itself with zero per-component dark styling; reuse on any long page that alternates light and dark chapters.
- `inview-theme-adaptive-sticky-cta` — a fixed CTA that inverts to match whatever section is behind it, using a viewport-band IntersectionObserver instead of a scrim; reuse for persistent CTAs, players, and toolbars on multi-ground pages.
