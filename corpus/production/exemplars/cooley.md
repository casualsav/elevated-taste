## What works
- **Heading weight *descends* as size ascends.** Body is 400; the display headline is 300. The h1 sets at 140px in a light-weight serif, h2–h3 stay at 300, and only the small tiers climb back to 400/700. Most sites bold their way up the scale, which makes a large headline both big and heavy — two hierarchy signals doing the same job, and the page turns shouty. Inverting the weight ramp lets *scale alone* rank the page, and the thinner strokes make a 140px word read as composed rather than loud. Transfers to any page with a display tier: pick the lightest weight the face survives at, and reserve bold for 12–14px labels where it is the only affordance you have.
- **One hue, three values, applied as an attribute — not a palette.** The entire site is maroon `#33040e`, red `#fd1434`, and rose `#fff2f4` on white. Every section and card carries a tone attribute that remaps the *same* three values into bg / text / accent roles: maroon ground with red text, red ground with maroon text, rose ground with red text. Because the roles rotate instead of the hues multiplying, a page can go from white to blood-red to near-black without ever introducing a fourth color, and a new component inherits the system by declaring which tone it is. See `patterns/monochrome-tone-triad.md`. Transfers to any brand with one strong hue that must survive across dozens of section types.
- **The brand red has an accessible sibling, and the code knows which is which.** `--color-primary-red: #fd1434` (3.9:1 on white — legal for 24px+ display, illegal for body) sits beside `--color-primary-red--accessible: #de0220` (5.1:1). Display type and large arrows get the vivid one; small text and links get the darker one; nobody has to remember. Naming the compliant variant as a first-class token is the cheapest way to stop a brand color from silently failing WCAG at 14px — most systems ship one red and lose the argument later.
- **Color blocks the page; there are no cards, no shadows, no gradients.** Five distinct grounds do the chaptering (white hero → white feature carousel → three tone-blocked resource tiles → full-bleed maroon insights list → full-bleed red video). `craft.elevation.gradients` is empty and the only real box-shadows on the page belong to the cookie widget. Section identity comes from ground color and a hairline rule; separation comes from `--component-vertical-space: clamp(4rem, 10vw, 8rem)`. Transfers anywhere you'd otherwise reach for a bordered card to say "this is a different thing."
- **The nav grid names its own escape hatch.** One `grid-template-columns: [full-start] 0 [main-start] repeat(var(--grid--cols),1fr) [main-end] 0 [full-end]` is declared once on every layout container; `--grid--cols` is 4 at 1rem gutter on mobile and 12 at 2rem from 800px up. A section that must bleed edge-to-edge writes `grid-column: full` — no negative margins, no `calc(-50vw + 50%)` hack, no separate full-bleed wrapper component. A parallel `--grid--col-equiv--N` ladder lets absolutely-positioned elements (the hero's photo tiles) line up on the same column widths without being grid children. Transfers to every design system: the bleed is a grid line, not an exception.
- **Fluid tokens are *pairs*, not single clamps.** Alongside a fixed 0.125→16rem spacing scale, the system ships named interpolations between two rungs — `--spacing-clamped--sm-to-md: clamp(1rem, 2vw, 1.5rem)`, `--lg-to-xxl`, `--xxs-to-md`. Every type size is the same shape (`--heading--l1--font-size: clamp(4.25rem, 8.5vw, 8.75rem)`). Naming the *endpoints* rather than inventing a new number keeps the fluid layer auditable against the static scale — you can always see which two steps a value is travelling between.
- **The reveal animation is defined only inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`.** The hidden state (`opacity: 0; translate3d(0,100px,0); blur(6px)`) lives *in* the gate, not outside it. Ask for less motion and the elements are never hidden in the first place — there is nothing to un-hide, no `!important` override, no flash. This is the correct polarity for every scroll reveal and almost nobody ships it. See `patterns/no-preference-gated-reveal.md`.

## Design-system read
- **Type:** Two families, opposite jobs. **GT Sectra** (a high-contrast serif with cut, blade-like terminals) carries every heading and every link title; **Arial Nova** carries body, eyebrows, meta, and buttons. Scale is clamp-driven end to end: h1 `clamp(4.25rem, 8.5vw, 8.75rem)` (68→140px), h2 3.375→5.75rem, h3 2.625→3.75, h4 2→3, h5 1.5→2.25, h6 1.25→1.5, body 1rem, caption 0.75rem — a ~1.25 ratio through the small tiers exploding to an 8.75× body→display jump. Leading inverts against size (h1 at 0.9, headings at 1.0–1.1, body at 1.2–1.4) and so does tracking: `-0.01em` on headings with `word-spacing: -0.05em`, `+0.02em` on body, `+0.03em` on uppercase eyebrows and captions. Hierarchy = **scale × family × case**; weight runs *backwards* (300 display, 400 body, 700 eyebrow).
- **Color:** Ground white. Ink is maroon `#33040e`, never black. Accent is red `#fd1434`, with `#de0220` as its AA-passing text twin. Rose `#fff2f4` is the only tint. Roles rotate by tone rather than expanding: on maroon the text *is* the red; on red the text is the maroon; on rose the text is the red on maroon ink. A dormant second theme (`data-page-theme="blue"`: royal `#5a46ff`, navy `#05046e`, sky `#e6eaff`) sits in the tokens for sub-brands and is unused on the homepage. There is **no gray tier at all** — secondary text is the same ink demoted by size and case.
- **Spacing rhythm:** Base 0.25rem, scale 0.125 / 0.25 / 0.5 / 0.75 / 1 / 1.5 / 2 / 3 / 4 / 6 / 8 / 16rem, plus the clamped endpoint-pairs above. Section rhythm is one token: `--component-vertical-space: clamp(4rem, 10vw, 8rem)`. Gutter is the spacing unit: `--grid--gutter` is 1rem / 2rem and doubles as hero padding.
- **Layout system:** A single named-line 12-column grid (4 at mobile), gutter 2rem, live area `100vw − scrollbar − 2×gutter` capped at 1920px, with gutter columns added beyond that. Twenty declared breakpoints, but the two that matter are 599/600 (hero collage hides) and 799/800 (4→12 columns). The hero is a separate 24 × 12 sub-grid holding five absolutely-placed photo tiles at `clamp()` widths. Density is low-to-medium: one h1, nine h2, thirty-four h3 — a stack of scannable link directories, not prose.
- **Layering / page architecture:** Essentially a **flat scroll with one glass layer**. `has_static_backdrop: false`, `has_blend_overlay: false`. The only real chrome layer is the sticky header (z 99, `rgba(255,255,255,0.8)` + `backdrop-filter: blur(2px)`) — enough to keep a red wordmark legible over a red band without going opaque. Section headers use `position: sticky; top: var(--site-header--height)` so a section's label holds while its list scrolls past it. Depth in the hero is *painted*, not stacked: photo tiles at z 0 behind an h1 at z 1, separated by parallax rate rather than shadow. (`mix-blend-mode: lighten` exists on the `.formations` video layer, but only on interior page templates.)
- **Motion / easing intent:** 88 transition rules, 3 animation rules. Durations cluster at **330ms** (the house transition, 98 uses), 200ms for micro-states, 600ms for image filters, 800ms for entrance reveals. Easing is `ease-in-out` everywhere except the entrance, which uses `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` — a hard expo-out that arrives fast and settles long. The intent is *confirmation*, not choreography: things acknowledge you and get out of the way.
- **Density:** Low visual density, high link density (17 nav links, 34 h3 link titles). It stays legible because every list row is a serif headline at h4/h5 size with its metadata dropped to 0.75rem uppercase sans — the two registers never compete.
- **Hierarchy:** Scale first, ground color second (which tone-block am I in), family third (serif = content, sans = label). Weight and gray are not used to rank anything.
- **Restraint:** No gradients. No shadows. No dark mode. Radii of 2–3px only (plus a pill for tags) — on a page this saturated, a rounded card would read as a UI widget. No stock-photo hero, no three-feature-card grid, no glass, no cursor follower, no hover-scale on anything except imagery (`scale(1.05)`).

## Interaction & motion
Evidence: 11 scroll frames; 12 scroll effects (4 `transform/parallax`, 3 `opacity`, 3 `pin` — all three pins are the cookie widget, not design); 11 staggered entrance reveals; 6 IntersectionObservers over 9 targets; 5 `@keyframes` (2 vendor); 1 `prefers-reduced-motion` rule; **zero** hover-state deltas recorded by the probe (they are `::after` pseudo-elements the probe can't diff). Stack: GSAP + Locomotive (`data-scroll="out"`) + Swiper.

- **No-preference-gated stagger reveal** — *Trigger:* in-view (IO, `rootMargin: 0px 0px -20% 0px`). *Mechanism:* the hidden state *and* the animation are both authored inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`; JS flips `data-gsap-ready` on the section, and each child animates `gsap-stagger-fade-in-up` (`opacity 0→1`, `translate3d(0,100px,0)→0`, `blur(6px)→0`) for 0.8s on `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`, delayed by a per-item `--gsap-delay` in 0.08s steps. *Reads as:* the list assembling itself in reading order. *Transfers to:* any feed, card row, or link directory. *Safety:* **production-safe** — transform/opacity/filter, `will-change` set and then cleared to `auto` on completion. The `blur()` is the one real cost (it forces a filter pass); drop it below 800px. See `patterns/no-preference-gated-reveal.md`.
- **Scroll-parallax photo collage behind a display headline** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* five photo/video tiles are absolutely placed on a 24 × 12 hero sub-grid at `clamp()` widths; each gets an inline `transform: translate(0, Nvh)` written per frame from scroll position, at differing rates, while the h1 (z 1) drifts on its own rate. Frames s00→s02 show the tiles rising past the headline and re-composing the letterforms' negative space. *Reads as:* the firm's world moving behind its claim. *Transfers to:* any hero whose subject is a portfolio of images rather than one product shot. *Safety:* **production-safe** in kind, but this build spends 7 scroll listeners and ~1,700 rAF calls on it; prefer `animation-timeline: view()`. Below 600px the collage is `display: none` — the honest resolution, since the resting hero must be complete without it. Corpus already carries the recipe: `patterns/scroll-parallax-tile-collage.md`.
- **Blur-and-rise headline entrance** — *Trigger:* load. *Mechanism:* the h1 is split into word spans; the block enters from `translateY(40px)` with a `filter: blur()` that resolves to 0, each span transitioning `transform, scale` over 0.33s. *Reads as:* the sentence coming into focus, literally. *Transfers to:* one headline per page, never two. *Safety:* **production-safe** at this scale; `filter` on a 140px serif is a large paint area, so keep it to the single hero and never on a scroll-linked timeline.
- **Scroll-exit dissolve on full-bleed sections** — *Trigger:* scroll-progress. *Mechanism:* the hero and the video band carry `data-scroll="out"` and expose a `--visible-y` custom property; opacity and translate are driven off the element's own exit range as it leaves the viewport (probe: header `opacity 1→0`, `background rgba(255,255,255,0.8)→transparent`). *Reads as:* one chapter clearing before the next lands. *Transfers to:* the seam between two full-bleed bands. *Safety:* **production-safe**; fading *out* is safe in a way that fading *in* is not — nothing that has already been read gets taken away.
- **Arrow-rotate link affordance** — *Trigger:* hover/focus-visible. *Mechanism:* every list row and card ends in an icon-font arrow pseudo-element; on hover **and** `:focus-visible` it `rotate(-45deg)` over 0.33s (→ points up-and-right, the "go there" glyph), while inline links pull their underline offset from 8px to 4px. Buttons instead slide their arrow in from `translate3d(-100%,0,0)` with `opacity 0→1`. *Reads as:* the row is a destination, and the whole row is the target — not just the words. *Transfers to:* any text-link directory. *Safety:* **production-safe**, and note it is bound to focus as well as hover, so keyboard users get the same signal.
- **Translucent sticky header + sticky section labels** — *Trigger:* scroll. *Mechanism:* `position: sticky` header at 80% white with `backdrop-filter: blur(2px)`; separately, each section's `header.intro` sticks at `top: var(--site-header--height)` while its item list scrolls beneath. *Reads as:* you never lose your place, and the header never covers what it's labelling. *Safety:* **production-safe**; 2px of blur is a deliberate minimum — it separates the layers without the frosted-glass cost.

**Reduced-motion: respected**, via the inverted gate (`prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`) rather than a `reduce` override. Caveat for reuse: because the hidden state is applied by CSS and cleared by JS, a JS failure leaves motion-tolerant users with invisible content — the pattern below adds the `:where(html:not(.js))` failsafe this build lacks.

## Production-safety note
Every technique is **production-safe** in kind: transform, opacity, filter, and one 2px backdrop blur. Three defects to fix on reuse: (1) parallax is hand-driven through 7 scroll listeners and ~1,714 rAF calls where `animation-timeline: view()` would do it on the compositor; (2) the entrance's hidden state depends on JS to clear it — gate it on a `js` class too; (3) **29 of 111 interactive elements fall under the 44px tap-target minimum** (footer link columns, meta links, carousel arrows) — the tab buttons and card CTAs are fine, the small print is not. Focus rings are excellent: 2–3px solid outlines in the brand red at `0.2rem` offset, present on links, buttons, and the skip link. Alt coverage is 100% (5 images), though `srcset` is used on none of them.

## Extracted patterns
- `monochrome-tone-triad` — one brand hue expressed as three values that rotate through bg/text/accent roles via a `data-tone` attribute, plus a named AA-passing text variant; reuse when a single strong brand color must ground full-bleed bands, cards, and body copy without a fourth hue.
- `no-preference-gated-reveal` — a staggered fade-rise-unblur whose hidden state exists *only* inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`, so reduced-motion users are never hidden from their own content; reuse as the default polarity for every scroll reveal.
